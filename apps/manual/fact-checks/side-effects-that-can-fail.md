## The research question, answered before drafting

**Question.** What should a model be told after a write that failed, was undone or never answered, and how do idempotency keys and one approval for a whole plan keep a retry from doing the work twice?

**Answer, from the todo's sources only.** A model should be told one of five outcomes in the result body: `applied`, `not_applied`, `compensated`, `partial` or `unknown`, each with a retry rule. `unknown`, a timeout or lost response, may be retried only with the same idempotency key, after asking the other side what happened ([server rollback report](../../../docs/research/reversible-actions/server-rollback.md), [Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/error-low-level.md)). The key has to be saved before the call, so a crash leaves a trace that a resume can read ([Stripe, Idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests.md)). A plan of several writes is a saga: compensable steps, one pivot after which nothing is undone, and retryable steps after it. One approval has to come before the first compensable step and cover every step's arguments, because a person who rejects a later step after the pivot strands the plan ([sagas report](../../../docs/research/reversible-actions/sagas.md)).

**Gaps.** No source measures how models react to different outcome wording, and no source discusses approval relative to a pivot; both are our reasoning, and the chapter says so.
