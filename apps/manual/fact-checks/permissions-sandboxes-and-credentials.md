## The research question, answered before drafting

**Question.** How do a permission pipeline, a sandbox and a credential boundary each contain a different failure, and why is the outbound leg the one to cut?

**Answer, from the todo's sources and the repository's own documents only.** A permission pipeline judges a tool call before it runs, from what the call says it will do: Claude's SDK evaluates hooks, deny rules, ask rules, the mode, allow rules and a callback, in that order, and a deny rule beats every mode ([Configure permissions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/permissions.md)). Pi ships no such system and says so ([Run Pi safely](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi/blob/v1.5.2/packages/coding-agent/docs/security.md)). A sandbox limits what a command can reach once it runs, whatever the pipeline allowed, and each of our backends states which failures it stops and which it does not. A credential boundary keeps a secret out of the sandbox by adding it at the host's egress. The three answer different failures, so none replaces another. The lethal trifecta explains why the outbound leg is the cheapest cut: private data and untrusted content are usually what makes the agent useful, while a way to send data out is often a convenience ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)).

**Gap.** No source measures how often each containment catches a real failure, so the chapter makes no claim about their rates.
