# Fact-check log: agent-to-agent protocols (A2A) report

The agent-to-agent protocols report holds up, and its thesis stands: A2A is the standard way to hand a task to an agent we neither own nor can see inside, and our agents should use it only across that ownership boundary, treating every card and reply as untrusted. Of the 138 rows below, 97 are confirmed (✅), 31 needed a correction (✏️) and 10 rest on inference, on absence or on untested code (⚠️). Each section was checked as soon as it was drafted, before the next was written. The intro and conclusion were drafted last, so that they summarise checked sections, and the rows come in ten blocks: one per section group, then the re-checks of the writing review's fixes (rows 119–125) and of the two code-review rounds' fixes (rows 126–132 and 133–138).

None of the corrections changes a finding. Seven fixed claims that overstated or misquoted a source: the card's required fields (row 21), the 0.3-to-1.0 rule (row 24), two MCP sampling quotes (row 37), the spec's "SHOULD" on webhook validation (row 60), the name-collision counts (row 86), the bearer-token rejection (row 103) and the count of A2A mentions in our repo (row 109). Five fixed our own wording: a contradiction about ADK (row 96), an unsupported line count (row 101), a missing SDK (row 114), the terms heading (row 116) and an unchecked claim about editors (row 117). Four fixed only a citation (rows 31, 54, 69 and 100). The writing review added four more ✏️ (design) rows for wording (119, 120, 122 and 125) and re-confirmed three changed claims (121, 123 and 124). The code review's first round found three claims logged as ⚠️ that read as fact (rows 126, 128 and 130); a re-read of every ⚠️ row found a fourth (row 131), and one citation was moved inline (row 127). The second round found redirecting links and local scratch paths: five more ✏️ (citation) rows move links to their final URLs (rows 133–137), and one ✏️ (design) row removes the paths (row 138).

The ⚠️ rows fall into three groups. Six are our own synthesis, and the report labels each as ours: the line between MCP and A2A, the harness pause, the five rules, task durability, peer pinning and the five teaching steps (rows 42, 45, 57, 59, 87 and 98). Three are absence claims or untested code: no A2A mention in MCP's documents, ADK's offline delegation path, and the example's checks 2 and 5 (rows 33, 78 and 104). The last concerns a source: A2ABreak's abstract does not say which spec version it modelled (row 84). Row 62 is ✅ for ADK and an absence claim for the other seven SDKs; it is counted once, as ✅.
