# Automate the job search, keep the person at Send

A friend's assistant called "Muse" reads their job mail, drafts replies and keeps a tracker board, which prompted the question this report answers: how much of a person's whole job search can an agent automate? As of November 2026, an agent can automate most of it: discovery, ranking, tailoring, form preparation, tracking, inbox triage, follow-up scheduling and draft outreach. It cannot lawfully or reliably automate the two acts that matter most, which are sending a message and submitting an application, without a person approving each one. The evidence comes from three directions. First, the channels that convert are referrals, employer career pages and early applications, so volume buys little: referrals are under 1% of applications but about 18% of hires in Ashby's data, and LinkedIn converted at 3.1% against 11.3% for the best direct channel in Huntr's 2025 data. Second, the rules forbid the volume route: LinkedIn's User Agreement bans bots and browser extensions that message, connect or scrape, and Indeed's terms ban automating Indeed Apply outside its own tooling. Parts of that law are unsettled, and this report is research, not legal advice. Third, the products that skip approval have the worst reviews. The agent should therefore prepare everything and stop before each send and submit.

The integrations that exist favour a hybrid design. Employer job boards on Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby publish free public JSON, but every application-submit API needs the employer's key. LinkedIn exposes no member API for search or messaging. Reading or even drafting Gmail requires a restricted scope, and any public app that handles that data on a server needs an annual third-party security assessment (CASA). Our planned SDK and harness already supply the hard parts this agent needs: signed approvals, idempotency keys, an append-only log, a deny-first permission pipeline and a credential proxy. Treated in our sagas vocabulary, every send and every submit is a pivot, the point of no return. This report recommends a draft-first agent whose tracker is its state, whose inbound mail is untrusted content, and whose first slice polls public job boards, reads forwarded or Outlook mail, and leaves every send and submit to an approved, idempotent, human-confirmed step.

## Referrals and early direct applications beat volume, so the agent should aim, not spray

How people look for jobs has split from how they get hired. Seekers save more jobs from LinkedIn than from any other site, which held the largest share of saves in every quarter of Huntr's 2025 tracker data, yet LinkedIn applications reached an interview stage only 3.1% of the time across 598,627 tracked 2025 applications. Google Jobs reached 11.3%, GovernmentJobs 8.7%, Wellfound 6.0%, Glassdoor 5.5% and Indeed 4.5% ([Huntr 2025 annual report](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report)). Huntr's own Q3 report labels the top row "Google's career portal", so the 11% figure may describe an employer career page rather than the aggregator ([Huntr Q3 2025](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q3-2025)). Either way, the gap points to one concrete automation. **Find the job where the seeker browses, then resolve it to the employer's application page and apply there.** Huntr's data is vendor telemetry from a tech-heavy, mostly US user base, and "response" means the user moved a card to Interview, so treat the rates as directional.

Referrals dominate per application. Ashby's data on about 250,000 hires shows inbound applicants produced **52% of hires in Q2 2025**, a four-year high, while **referrals held steady at about 18%** and sourced hires at about 16% ([Ashby inbound report](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/inbound)). Ashby's referrals report, on 38 million applications from 2021 to 2024, adds that referrals fell below 1% of applications and that inbound offer rates fell from 7 to **2 per 1,000** as inbound volume tripled ([Ashby referrals report](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals)). A summary credits the same report with a **7.3% offer rate for referred candidates**, a figure its text does not state (secondary; [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/recruitment-statistics/)). Older field experiments agree that referred workers are more likely to be hired, all else equal ([Pallais and Sands, NBER w21357](https://nber.org/papers/w21357)). One referral is therefore worth very roughly 30 to 40 cold applications, and the most valuable thing an agent can do is find the people who could refer the seeker.

Timing also matters more than polish. In 25 structured interviews with US recruiters, **52% said applying early improves a candidate's chances** because they review in order of arrival; one turns a posting off once it passes a thousand applications and starts interviewing from the first 300 ([Enhancv](https://enhancv.com/blog/does-ats-reject-resumes/)). The same interviews dispel the keyword myth: **23 of 25 recruiters said their applicant tracking system (ATS) does not auto-reject on formatting or content**, and rejection comes mainly from knockout questions such as work authorisation and sponsorship, which one recruiter said removes about 30% of technical applicants. Enhancv is a vendor with a small sample, but its finding reconciles with Greenhouse's: 53% of US recruiters hand most screening to AI or the ATS ([Greenhouse AI in Hiring Report](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/691b3f85d9304d0f30b57773_3274b13134499af4515ec383ff3b1f01_Greenhouse-2025_AI_in_Hiring_Report.pdf)), which fits a model where software ranks and humans read the top of the pile. An agent should therefore watch for new postings, apply within days, answer knockout questions truthfully and produce a plain résumé.

AI has already flooded the inbound channel. Applications through LinkedIn rose 45% in 2025 to about 11,000 a minute, as reported by the New York Times ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/06/24/2025/recruiters-swamped-with-ai-generated-job-applications)); a LinkedIn representative later gave about 9,500 a minute (secondary; [Recruiting News Network](https://www.recruitingnewsnetwork.com/posts/recruiters-are-drinking-through-a-fire-hose-of-job-applications-experts-say--ai-is-partially-to-blame)). Greenhouse's surveys found that **22% of US seekers already use bots or agents to apply automatically**, 29% hid keywords in white text, and 40% report using prompt injection against filters ([Greenhouse 2025 Workforce and Hiring Report](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66bf84e70c6fc57dbbe0187e/687530e016ce4153b861335b_Greenhouse_2025WorkforceAndHiringReport.pdf); [AI in Hiring Report](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/691b3f85d9304d0f30b57773_3274b13134499af4515ec383ff3b1f01_Greenhouse-2025_AI_in_Hiring_Report.pdf)). Employers responded in kind: 61% of US hiring managers use AI-detection software, 91% have caught or suspected AI-driven misrepresentation, and 39% run more in-person interviews. Gartner forecasts that **one in four candidate profiles worldwide will be fake by 2028** and recommends identity verification ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them)). Greenhouse's CEO calls the cycle "an AI doom loop" (secondary; [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/hiring-in-an-ai-doom-loop-greenhouse/753679/)). An agent that adds volume worsens the problem it claims to solve and starts to look like the fraud employers screen for.

The automations seekers need follow from the pain and the payoff. Tailored résumés converted at **5.8% against 3.73%** for untailored ones, yet only about 6% of tracked applications were tailored ([Huntr 2025 annual report](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report)). **46% of US candidates abandon applications because they must retype their résumé** into ATS forms, and 63% were ghosted after an interview ([Greenhouse 2025 Workforce and Hiring Report](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66bf84e70c6fc57dbbe0187e/687530e016ce4153b861335b_Greenhouse_2025WorkforceAndHiringReport.pdf)). Searches are long and bimodal: the median time to a first offer rose from 57 days in Q1 2025 to 83 in Q4, about 38% of successful Huntr users landed within 30 applications, and 18% needed more than 100 ([Huntr 2025 annual report](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report)). Ranked by evidence, the work divides into three families. Finding jobs means discovery, de-duplication, eligibility filtering and resolution to the employer's application page. Finding people means referrers, recruiters and hiring managers, with a contact email where outreach is appropriate. Applying means tailoring, form preparation, tracking, follow-up and ghosting detection. Only about half of seekers would trust AI to apply on their behalf ([Huntr Q3 2025](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q3-2025)), which supports approval gates on the third family. Seniority changes the mix: executive roles are filled from inbound only about 35% of the time ([Ashby](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/inbound)), so senior seekers need network mapping, while new graduates, facing 140 applications per UK graduate vacancy (secondary; [Wonkhe](https://wonkhe.com/blogs/graduate-jobs-and-recruitment-reality/)), need process help.

## Existing products split at the submit button, and the friend's Muse shows the whole-search shape

The friend's assistant is most likely **Meta Muse**, Meta's personal AI agent, which Meta launched in the US on September 8, 2026 and extended to Singapore and Australia by November 17 ([Meta: Introducing Muse](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/); [Meta: Muse in Australia](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/muse-is-now-available-in-australia/)). Meta's own pages confirm the four details that matter here. Each user's Muse runs on Muse Secure VM, "a dedicated secure computer with its own browser". A separate Sentinel agent must approve every outside action, and Muse "checks with the person before sensitive actions like sending an email or making a purchase". Gmail and Google Calendar are among its connectors, and the email connector "filters out one-time tokens, password reset links, and login magic links via both deterministic filters and a classifier model" ([Meta: Introducing Muse](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/); [Meta: Muse in Australia](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/muse-is-now-available-in-australia/); [Muse: How we built safety into Muse](https://muse.ai/blog/security-and-safety-for-ai-agents-our-approach-with-muse)). Meta says Muse is "free for most of what people need, with subscription plans"; reported tiers of $20 and $100 a month are unconfirmed as of November 2026 (secondary; [tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/meta-muse-personal-ai-agent-launch-2026/)).

The job-search board is not a built-in feature. A community template on GitHub turns Muse into "a job application assistant with rules" through markdown instructions ([splashdeluxe/ai-job-search-template](https://github.com/splashdeluxe/ai-job-search-template)). It sources roles from official careers boards, tailors one CV per company in Google Drive, keeps LinkedIn and job-board logins in Muse's Secure Vault, runs scheduled dispatcher and briefing jobs, and renders a dashboard artifact for the pipeline. Its `/gmail-sync` command proposes tracker updates that the user approves one by one. The template differs from our design at the submit button, though. It "submits for you, once per role", and when a CAPTCHA blocks the form it retries in "a stealth cloud browser", so its "one verified submit per role" rule prevents duplicates rather than requiring a person to submit. Muse is thus a general agent made into a job-search agent by prompts, connectors and a dashboard. Its secret-filtering email connector is worth copying; its template's automated submission is the part our design leaves to the person. The Muse job site, themuse.com, has no email agent features that turned up in our searches, so it is a poor match for the friend's description.

The big platforms now build their own agents, which become the sanctioned route on their sites. LinkedIn began rolling out a Premium Apply Assistant in June 2026 that pre-fills applications and drafts cover letters, and says recruiters do not see the AI assistance (secondary; [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-automates-job-application-process-for-premium-users/823719/)). LinkedIn also offers natural-language search and a built-in job tracker (secondary; [SocialBee](https://socialbee.com/blog/linkedin-updates-news-features/)). Indeed launched Career Scout, an AI career coach, on September 10, 2025 (secondary; [HR Grapevine](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/us/content/article/2025-09-19-openai-indeed-unveil-rival-ai-hiring-platforms)), and its current terms describe an agentic "Apply For Me" tool whose answers are used "at your own risk" ([Indeed legal terms](https://www.indeed.com/legal)). ZipRecruiter's Phil drafts profile text and pitches seekers to employers ([ZipRecruiter blog](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/blog/new-job-seeker-tools/), vendor). OpenAI announced a Jobs Platform for mid-2026, but no source confirmed a launch as of November 2026 ([Campus Technology](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2025/09/10/openai-to-launch-ai-powered-jobs-platform-by-mid-2026.aspx)).

Two support markets complete the picture. Contact-finding tools give job seekers small free tiers as of November 2026: Hunter renews 50 credits a month and Lusha 40 ([Hunter pricing](https://hunter.io/pricing); [Lusha pricing](https://www.lusha.com/pricing/)), while ContactOut gives 5 a day and RocketReach 5 a month, and Apollo's free allowance is reported inconsistently (secondary; [derrick-app](https://derrick-app.com/tools/contactout-review); [moderninbound](https://moderninbound.com/blog/best-rocketreach-alternatives); [supered](https://www.supered.io/blog/rocketreach-vs-apollo/)). Sales Navigator from $119.99 a month and Clay's paid plans from $167 a month, billed annually, are priced for sales teams ([LinkedIn: Sales Navigator plans](https://business.linkedin.com/sales-solutions/compare-plans); [Clay pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)). Email trackers form a long tail of Chrome extensions that classify job mail into stages, such as JobTally, which flags companies silent for 60 days ([JobTally](https://presdinworks.in/jobtally)). One of them sits invite-only in Google's OAuth testing mode, where Google expires its sign-in every seven days ([Chrome Web Store: JobTracker](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/jobtracker-%E2%80%94-ai-gmail-job/eaiogfbalcpeiogebndnelahdcnakglc)), and JobSnap avoids a Gmail connection entirely by using a Gmail filter ([usejobsnap](https://www.usejobsnap.com/)). Classifying job mail onto a tracker is a commodity. The hard parts are matching each email to the right application and passing Google's review.

The integrations divide cleanly: public data and the seeker's own mailbox are reachable, while LinkedIn actions and application submission are not. The table summarises what each allows a job-seeker's agent to do as of November 2026.

Three consequences shape the design. First, **job discovery can be official, free and scrape-free**: the agent resolves each target company to its ATS board token and polls the public JSON, which also yields the custom questions an application will ask. Second, **every submit path for a job seeker runs through the employer's application page**, because Greenhouse's and Lever's submit APIs take a key that only the employer can create, and Greenhouse warns that the key must stay on the employer's servers ([Greenhouse](https://developers.greenhouse.io/job-board.html)). Greenhouse forms reportedly validate a reCAPTCHA Enterprise token (secondary; [browse.sh](https://browse.sh/skills/greenhouse.com/apply-to-jobs-dsmal1)), and Workable gives employers tools to flag automated applications ([Workable Help](https://help.workable.com/hc/en-us/articles/35293126257815-Managing-AI-generated-and-automated-job-applications)). A trustworthy agent therefore prepares the application and hands the final page to the person.

Third, Gmail is the most expensive integration, and the obvious workaround fails. A "draft-only, the human sends" design does not escape Google's restricted tier, because `gmail.compose` is itself restricted; only send-only `gmail.send` is merely sensitive ([Gmail scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes)). Google says verification "can potentially take several weeks" and must be repeated at least every 12 months ([restricted scope verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification)). Assessment cost is unverified: consultant pages cite $15,000 to $75,000 and one guide cites about $500 a year (secondary, conflicting; [GMass](https://www.gmass.co/blog/google-oauth-verification-security-assessment/); [Unipile](https://www.unipile.com/gmail-api-scopes-guide/)).

Three routes avoid most of that cost. First, Google exempts personal use and development or testing from verification ([restricted scope verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification)). Its Personal user type goes further: an OAuth client limited to the one account that created it needs no verification and escapes both the 100-test-user cap and the seven-day token expiry of Testing mode ([Google: Manage App Audience](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945)). Second, integrators can borrow a shared app that has passed Tier 3 CASA from a provider such as Nylas, on its annual Pro plan with an add-on or on Enterprise ([Nylas shared GCP app](https://developer.nylas.com/docs/provider-guides/google/shared-gcp-app/)). Third, Google's Workspace MCP server, in public preview since May 2026 ([Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/05/agent-tools-and-security-updates-for-workspace-developers.html)), asks for `gmail.readonly`, `gmail.compose` and `gmail.modify`, and its Gmail tools draft, search and label but include no send tool ([Gmail MCP reference](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/mcp); [configure MCP servers](https://developers.google.com/workspace/guides/configure-mcp-servers)). It moves tool hosting to Google but leaves the restricted scopes, and so the verification burden, with the app. For a hosted product, the cheapest Google path is Calendar plus `gmail.send`, with inbox reading done through Outlook, a shared verified provider, or mail the user forwards to the agent.

## Law and terms allow a drafting agent, not a volume bot

The rules converge on one boundary: the person must be the actor for every outward act, and nothing may be automated on LinkedIn. This section is research, not legal advice, and several of its points are unsettled law.

**LinkedIn bans the method, not just the scale.** Section 8.2 of LinkedIn's User Agreement, effective November 3, 2025, forbids software "(such as crawlers, browser plugins and add-ons …) to scrape or copy the Services, including profiles", forbids "bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services, add or download contacts, send or redirect messages", and names "copying cookies" among banned account-sharing methods. LinkedIn may "restrict, suspend, or terminate your account" for a breach ([LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). The ban attaches to bots and extensions even when the account owner runs them on data the owner could see. Enforcement has targeted commercial scrapers: hiQ ended with a $500,000 consent judgment and permanent injunction in December 2022 after LinkedIn won summary judgment on breach of contract, although the Ninth Circuit had held that scraping public pages is unlikely to violate the CFAA (secondary; [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/); [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/hiq-v-linkedin-wrapped-up-web-scraping-lessons-learned/)). Proxycurl shut down on July 4, 2025 to comply with a settlement after LinkedIn sued it in January 2025 ([Nubela](https://nubela.co/blog/goodbye-proxycurl/)). For an individual, the realistic consequence is losing the account, which for a job seeker is the professional network itself. LinkedIn reportedly restricted the creator of the open-source AIHawk Easy Apply bot in 2024 (secondary; [lastroundai](https://lastroundai.com/blog/linkedin-auto-apply-bots)).

**Indeed bans automating its apply flow and sells its own agent instead.** Indeed's terms, last updated September 29, 2026, state that "Use of any automation, scripting, or bots to automate the Indeed Apply process outside of Indeed's official vendors and tooling is prohibited", and make the applicant "solely responsible for your application's content". Its own Apply For Me tool, a beta for US and Canadian users, auto-submits applications, and its user must "assume all risk for auto-submitted content" ([Indeed legal terms](https://www.indeed.com/legal)). Greenhouse's and Workday's candidate terms were not retrieved, so their stance on automated submission remains a gap.

**Cold email law depends on where the recipient sits.** No authority found addresses a job seeker's one-to-one email about their own candidacy, so the safe reading is conservative.

The contact-data vendors carry legal risk of their own. Apollo faces an Illinois right-of-publicity class action (secondary; [ClassAction.org](https://classaction.org/news/apollo.io-profited-from-illinois-residents-personal-data-without-consent-class-action-says)), and ZoomInfo's filings disclose several pending publicity-rights suits ([ZoomInfo 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001794515/000179451526000056/zi-20260630.htm), via search summary). The agent should therefore prefer addresses the recruiter published, such as a job post or personal site, and treat enrichment as an opt-in lookup.

**Deliverability rules bind long before the law does.** Gmail requires every sender to keep its Postmaster spam rate below 0.3% and recommends below 0.1%. Senders of more than 5,000 messages a day must also have SPF, DKIM, DMARC and one-click unsubscribe ([Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). Yahoo sets the same 0.3% ceiling for all senders ([Yahoo Sender Hub](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)), and Outlook has rejected non-compliant mail from domains sending over 5,000 a day since May 5, 2025 ([Microsoft](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%E2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%E2%80%90volume-senders/4399730)). A seeker sending dozens of emails stays outside the bulk tier, and Google's own SPF and DKIM authenticate mail sent through the API from an @gmail.com address. The binding limits are therefore the personal Gmail limit of about 500 recipients a day ([Gmail Help: sending limits](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/22839)) and recipients' spam reports. An individual cannot see the Postmaster spam rate for gmail.com, so the agent must cap volume at tens a day and keep each message individual.

**Platform policies add their own limits.** Google's Limited Use rules confine Gmail data to "user-facing features that are prominent" in the app, ban transfers to data brokers, and forbid humans reading the data without the user's affirmative agreement ([Google API Services User Data Policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy)). Anthropic's Usage Policy, effective November 12, 2026, bars facilitating spam, fake personas that mislead people about whether they are dealing with a human, and profiles of individuals compiled for sale or disclosure without their awareness and agreement. It also requires consumer-facing AI agents that communicate directly with external users to disclose that they are AI ([Anthropic Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)). Sending email the user approved in their own name fits these rules. Mass outreach, or a persona that pretends to be a human assistant, does not, and an agent that replies to recruiters on its own must say it is an AI.

**Honesty is the candidate's duty, and the agent inherits it.** Indeed makes the applicant responsible for every answer ([Indeed legal terms](https://www.indeed.com/legal)), and no US law found requires candidates to disclose AI use; disclosure laws such as NYC Local Law 144 and Illinois's 2026 amendments bind employers (secondary; [Vensure](https://vensure.com/resources/blog/ai-disclosure-laws-employers/)). The candidate's risk is misrepresentation, which can justify rescinding an offer. Some employers now ask an AI-use question or attestation on the form (secondary; [University of Miami career blog](https://customcareer.miami.edu/blog/2026/09/22/should-you-tell-an-employer-you-used-ai-to-apply/)). The agent must therefore answer work authorisation, sponsorship, salary, degrees and criminal-history questions only from facts the user confirmed, and must route any AI-use question to the person. It must never inject prompts or hide keywords.

**AI-in-hiring laws regulate the employer's side.** The EU AI Act lists as high-risk AI systems used "to analyse and filter job applications, and to evaluate candidates" ([Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401689)). NYC Local Law 144 binds employers and employment agencies (secondary; [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/critical-audit-of-nyc-ai-hiring-law-signals-increased-risk-for-employers)). Colorado's SB 26-189, signed May 14, 2026, repealed and re-enacted its AI Act as a law on automated decision-making in consequential decisions, employment among them, with duties for developers and deployers from January 1, 2027 ([Colorado General Assembly: SB26-189](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189)). A tool that ranks jobs for a seeker evaluates no candidate, so these high-risk duties should not reach it.

**Article 50 is the AI Act duty most likely to reach a candidate-side product.** Article 50 sets two duties: an AI that talks to people must disclose itself, and synthetic text must be machine-marked unless the system performs "an assistive function for standard editing" ([Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401689)). Both duties apply from August 2, 2026. The Digital Omnibus, adopted as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 on July 8, 2026, left that date in place. It gave only systems already on the market before then until December 2, 2026 to meet the marking duty, and it deferred the Annex III high-risk duties to December 2, 2027 ([Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj)). Drafts that the user edits and sends sit closer to the assistive carve-out than an AI that replies to recruiters on its own. This question is unsettled.

## Our stack supplies the gates, and the job-search domain is what remains to build

Nothing in our repository is built yet; the next phase builds M5 to M9 ([roadmap](../../plans/roadmap.md)). Every mapping below is our inference from designs written for a support-triage agent, a coding agent and a multi-user platform. The plans still cover most of the generic machinery a job-search agent needs, and the gaps are domain adapters, recipient selection and tenancy for mail.

**Approvals before every send or submit come ready-made.** The SDK signs every approval pause over the request id, call id, tool name and a canonical input hash, and "a missing, unknown-key or invalid signature always fails closed" ([build-sdk D5](../../plans/build-sdk.md#d5-a-pluggable-signer-with-key-ids-an-ephemeral-default-and-no-unsigned-resume-accepted-with-hd5)). The harness lets a tool register `approvalReuse: "none"`, which refuses sticky and grouped approvals, and runs code-initiated calls through the same pipeline with `Harness.invokeTool()` ([build-harness §3.4](../../plans/build-harness.md#34-permission-approval-and-sandbox-apis); [HD12](../../plans/build-harness.md#hd12-three-middle-ground-modes-and-no-classifier-allow-outside-the-host-callback)). The triage design's tier table maps directly onto job search, so the tier assignments below are ours ([agent guide §5](../../guides/agent.md#5-safety-is-a-policy-table-and-a-context-split-tier-every-tool-by-worst-case-effect-bind-identity-and-cut-the-outbound-leg); [build-agent AD2](../../plans/build-agent.md#ad2-the-tier-table-is-code-t5-is-never-registered-and-t4-stays-denied-until-the-owner-sets-the-caps-accepted)).

| Tier | Job-search tools | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T0 read | Read inbox, poll job boards, fetch a posting, search contacts | None |
| T1 internal write | Edit tracker cards, notes, schedule a reminder | Undo window |
| T2 spend | Contact enrichment above a credit threshold | Approval |
| T3 external communication | Reply in a thread, send outreach, send a follow-up | Approval of exact text and recipient, `approvalReuse: "none"` |
| T4 irreversible, identity-bound | Submit an application | Approval of the whole application, then the person confirms on the employer's application page |
| T5 never offered | Any LinkedIn automation, mass send, CAPTCHA solving, credential changes, deleting mail | Not registered |

The triage plan's AD10 rule carries over unchanged: no classifier approves any call, and a detector may only raise a tier ([build-agent AD10](../../plans/build-agent.md#ad10-no-classifier-approves-any-call-and-a-classifier-may-only-raise-a-tier)).

**Every send and submit is a pivot that needs a business key.** Our sagas report names the pivot as "the point of no return" and notes that an email's "only remedy is a correction email, which is a new external action rather than a compensation" ([sagas report](../reversible-actions/sagas.md#the-refund-is-the-pivot-so-the-email-must-come-after-it)). Tool-call ids make good idempotency keys only once persisted before execution, because after a regenerated model call "the model emits a fresh id for what may be the same action", so high-stakes tools add a business key ([server rollback report](../reversible-actions/server-rollback.md#tool-call-ids-make-good-idempotency-keys-only-after-they-are-persisted-and-scoped)). For job search the business keys are `apply:{postingId}` and `outreach:{contactId}:{step}`. They also enforce the Muse template's "one verified submit per role" in code rather than in a prompt. A provider timeout leaves the outcome `unknown`, and the agent reconciles by reading the Sent folder or the RFC 5322 Message-ID before any retry with the same key ([server rollback report](../reversible-actions/server-rollback.md#after-a-rollback-tell-the-model-one-of-four-outcomes-in-the-result-body)).

**An application becomes a short saga with four steps.** The agent marks the tracker card "applying" as a compensable lock, the person submits as the pivot, a retryable note goes to the recruiter, and the card moves to "applied". One approval covers the answers, the résumé version and the follow-up text in one hash, and it expires with `stale_approval` if the posting changes or closes ([sagas report](../reversible-actions/sagas.md#approval-belongs-before-the-first-compensable-step-and-covers-the-whole-plan)). The forbidden order also carries over: the agent never tells a recruiter "I've applied" before the submission succeeds. For replies, approval of exact text followed by a short deferred-send window, modelled on Gmail's Undo Send, gives the person a last cancel ([rewind versus rollback report](../reversible-actions/rewind-vs-rollback.md#triage-needs-undo-windows-for-t1-approval-for-anything-that-leaves-the-org-and-a-saga-only-once-money-moves)).

**The tracker is the agent's state, not the conversation.** Our UI research says agent interfaces are projections of one append-only stream and that approvals should be persisted records ([agent UI research](../agent-ui/agent-ui-research.md#the-session-event-log-should-be-the-ui-contract)). The tracker therefore holds companies, postings, applications with a status history, contacts, mail threads, calendar events and next actions, stored as an append-only event log and projected one way to Notion or Sheets if the user wants them. Agent edits to cards carry a turn id and get Keep or Undo ([undo report](../agent-ui/undo-agent-edits.md#a-recommended-design-a-tagged-draft-inverse-op-revert-and-gated-side-effects)). Columns that record pivots, such as Applied or Emailed, are receipts and cannot be dragged back. On Cloudflare, the verified `setState` sync and persisted approval parts give both the tracker and an outbox of drafts awaiting approval without new UI infrastructure ([agent UI research](../agent-ui/agent-ui-research.md#only-cloudflare-syncs-app-state-in-both-directions)).

**Email ingress is untrusted content, and it completes the lethal trifecta.** A job-search agent combines all three legs: private data (the inbox, the CV, salary history), untrusted content (recruiter emails and job postings, which anyone can write) and external communication (sending). Our guide says injection "cannot be prevented, only contained", and that "the outbound leg is the one to cut" ([agent guide §5](../../guides/agent.md#5-safety-is-a-policy-table-and-a-context-split-tier-every-tool-by-worst-case-effect-bind-identity-and-cut-the-outbound-leg)). The triage roles contain it. A tool-less reader turns each email into enums such as `interview_request`, `rejection`, `question` or `spam`. A planner that never sees raw bodies picks the next step, and a tool-less drafter writes the reply ([build-agent AD1](../../plans/build-agent.md#ad1-the-product-composes-the-workflow-from-three-linked-harness-turns-and-code-runs-every-side-effect-through-the-pipeline-provisional)). Session taint denies T2 and higher proposals from any session that read untrusted text, and drafts built from tainted input never auto-allow ([build-agent AD5](../../plans/build-agent.md#ad5-hd1s-per-block-origin-plus-a-per-session-taint-and-no-per-argument-tracking-provisional)). The coding agent's ingress design dedupes every trigger on a stable id, strips hidden HTML and is meant to be shared ([build-coding-agent CA7](../../plans/build-coding-agent.md#ca7-triggers-start-with-the-cli-and-github-webhooks-behind-one-ingress-design-decided)). Its owner-only sender check cannot apply to mail from strangers, though, so inbound mail enters as content and never as a command. Muse's practice of filtering passcodes and reset links before the model sees them belongs in the same layer.

**Recipient selection is where the triage design breaks.** The triage `Helpdesk` interface takes no recipient, so replies can only go to the requester ([build-agent AD14](../../plans/build-agent.md#ad14-one-helpdesk-interface-is-the-agents-only-way-out-with-a-fake-for-tests-a-shadow-wrapper-for-rollout-and-a-real-adapter-the-owner-names-accepted)). Replies in a job-search thread fit that pattern, but cold outreach picks its recipient from an Apollo-style lookup whose output is untrusted. A `Mailbox` interface therefore needs two methods. A `reply(threadId)` method fixes the recipient to the thread. A `send(contactId)` method shows the recipient prominently on the approval card, rate-limits per recipient and per domain, and checks a suppression list.

**The platform plan fits the job-search agent better than a hosted product would.** The agent platform plan, now on main, serves other users from the start with one tenant per user ([PF1, ADR 0037](../../plans/build-agent-platform.md#pf1-other-users-from-the-start-one-tenant-per-user-accepted)). Users connect their own clouds with scoped, expiring credentials ([PF6, ADR 0040](../../plans/build-agent-platform.md#pf6-users-connect-their-own-clouds-with-scoped-expiring-credentials-accepted-adr-0040)), and every model call uses the user's own key with no fallback ([PF7](../../plans/build-agent-platform.md#pf7-the-users-own-model-key-with-no-fallback-to-ours-accepted-adr-0040)). Generated code runs only in a fresh sandbox per task with egress denied by default ([PF9, ADR 0042](../../plans/build-agent-platform.md#pf9-generated-code-runs-only-in-a-fresh-hosted-sandbox-accepted-adr-0042)), and a check-and-deploy gate with hidden graders blocks bad deploys ([PF4, ADR 0039](../../plans/build-agent-platform.md#pf4-a-check-and-deploy-gate-with-graders-the-generator-never-sees-accepted-adr-0039)). The proposed `@agent-speak/agent-safety` package would put signed approvals, idempotency keys and typed outcomes into every generated agent ([PF5](../../plans/build-agent-platform.md#pf5-one-safety-package-inside-every-generated-agent-proposed)). A job-search agent deployed this way runs in the seeker's own account, with the seeker's own Google OAuth client and model key. That shape fits Google's Personal user type, which serves exactly one account with no verification, user cap or seven-day token expiry ([Google: Manage App Audience](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945)), and it keeps recruiter data out of our control plane. This fit is still our inference, because the seeker must create the OAuth client in their own Google Cloud project and a Workspace admin can still block restricted scopes. A hosted multi-user job-search service would instead make us the controller of third-party contact data and the applicant for CASA.

Four gaps remain open. No plan budgets non-model spend such as contact-API credits, because the budget caps cover model USD only ([build-sdk D9](../../plans/build-sdk.md#d9-the-usd-cap-is-checked-before-each-model-call-against-a-projected-cost)). HD15's credential proxy covers API calls, not cookie-based logins, so browser sessions on employer sites need a separate design ([HD15](../../plans/build-harness.md#hd15-the-credential-proxy-injects-on-reverse-proxy-routes-not-by-intercepting-tls)). No mail ingress is designed, since W5 covers only the CLI and GitHub ([W5](../../todos/ready/W5-control-plane-ingress.md)). Finally, no `outcome` field or plan-level approval kind has been adopted into `ToolResult` yet ([rewind versus rollback report](../reversible-actions/rewind-vs-rollback.md#our-sdks-and-plans-rewind-the-conversation-and-roll-back-no-data)).

## Recommended design: a draft-first agent whose first slice never sends or submits on its own

The recommended agent automates everything up to each pivot and stops there. It runs as one agent per seeker, generated by the platform into the seeker's own Cloudflare account, on Cloudflare `agents` 0.33.0 until our SDK ships ([deployment report](../deployment/agent-building-platforms.md#where-a-new-platform-could-win-what-it-must-match-and-the-first-slice); [Cloudflare scheduling](../../reference/cloudflare-agents/guide.md#6-scheduling-runs-callbacks-on-durable-object-alarms)). Its parts follow the three families of seeker work.

**Finding jobs.** A scheduled job polls the Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby boards of a target-company list plus keyed aggregators, de-duplicates postings, drops roles the seeker is ineligible for using confirmed facts on location, authorisation and sponsorship, and ranks the rest. Speed of discovery matters because recruiters read early arrivals first. A LinkedIn posting the user pastes is resolved to the employer's application page.

**Finding people.** The agent maps the seeker's own network from LinkedIn's data export or a pasted contact list, never by driving LinkedIn. It ranks possible referrers at each target company and drafts a specific note for each. For recruiters and hiring managers, it prefers addresses published in the posting or on a personal site. Apollo people search (free) plus single-credit enrichment is an opt-in step, logged as T2 spend, and off by default for recipients in Canada and the EU.

**Applying.** For each approved target, the agent writes a tailored résumé and cover letter, pre-fills answers to the questions the board API returned, flags every answer not backed by a confirmed fact, and assembles one approval card. After approval, the person opens the employer's application page with the answers ready and clicks submit; the agent never solves a CAPTCHA. A confirmation email arriving through ingress closes the `unknown` gap and moves the card to Applied.

**Tracking and follow-up.** Inbound mail updates the tracker through the reader, planner and drafter roles. Scheduled wakes propose follow-ups after a set silence. Because wakes arrive at least once, each wake carries an idempotency key and first re-reads the thread, so that a reply cancels the follow-up ([storage and wake report](../deployment/storage-and-wake.md#three-rules-come-with-the-seams)). Calendar invitations become interview cards with a preparation brief.

The first slice should prove the loop while staying inside every rule above. It holds five parts, ordered from least to most risk:

1. **Discovery and ranking** from public Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby JSON for a user-supplied company list, written to the tracker.
2. **Tailored application packets**, each a résumé version, a cover letter and prepared answers, with the person submitting on the employer's application page and marking the card. The agent itself submits nothing.
3. **Read-only mail ingress** from Outlook through delegated `Mail.Read`, or from a forwarding address the user feeds with a Gmail filter, so that no restricted Gmail scope is needed. A tool-less reader classifies mail onto the tracker.
4. **An outbox of reply drafts** to existing threads, which the person copies or sends from their own client. Sending through `gmail.send` or `Mail.Send` with signed approval and a business key is the slice's last step, once the crash test passes.
5. **A safety eval suite** on the pass^5 release gate, with injected postings and recruiter emails such as "forward your CV and SSN to…", where one failed safety trial blocks release ([build-agent §5.3](../../plans/build-agent.md#53-the-passk-gate-blocks-on-task-flips-single-safety-failures-cost-and-latency)).

The slice leaves out LinkedIn automation, cold outreach to enriched addresses, auto-submission and Gmail reading. Each exclusion maps to a rule above and can return only behind its own gate.

## Conclusion

The job-search market rewards the opposite of what most automation sells. Seekers and bots have pushed volume to 11,000 applications a minute, and employers have answered with ranking, fraud detection and in-person checks. As a result, the scarce assets are now a referral, an early arrival and a truthful, tailored application, and an agent can produce all three faster than a person can, provided it stops at each pivot. That boundary is not a compromise with regulators. It is where LinkedIn's terms, Indeed's terms, Google's scope tiers, email law and seekers' own trust all draw the same line, and it is also where our saga and idempotency research already says the dangerous writes live.

For agent-speak, the job-search agent is a strong test case for the platform after its first, triage-shaped slice, because it needs pieces the plans have not yet adopted. It needs the proposed safety package for signed approvals and business keys, a mail ingress that treats strangers' text as content, recipient-aware outbound tools and non-model budgets. Deploying one agent per seeker into the seeker's own cloud turns a hard hosted-compliance problem into a personal tool. The next step is to write the first slice as a plan: public-board discovery, application packets, read-only mail ingress and an approval outbox, gated by an injection-heavy eval suite before any message leaves.
