# How people look for jobs in 2025–2026, and which steps they most need automated

> **Fact-checked 2026-11-18.** This note keeps its original text. Corrections are logged in [fact-check-job-search-agent.md](../fact-check-job-search-agent.md) and applied in [the report](../job-search-agent.md), which supersede this note where they disagree.

Notes as of 2026-11-18. Source-quality key: **[primary]** official statistics, peer-reviewed or NBER papers; **[vendor-platform]** a vendor's own telemetry (ATS or job-tracker logs), first-hand but biased toward that vendor's user base; **[vendor-survey]** a vendor-run opinion survey; **[secondary]** press coverage or a search-engine summary that I could not check against the original. Pages marked "read directly" were downloaded and read; the rest come from search summaries.

## 1. Which channels produce interviews and hires (referrals, outreach, boards, career pages, Easy Apply, agencies)

### Takeaway
Inbound applications still produce about half of all hires because they are most of the volume. Per application, though, referrals convert roughly 30–40x better, and direct or niche channels beat the big boards about 2–3x. A typical 2025 application had about a 3–4% chance of reaching an interview, and a hire took around 300 applications on the employer side. A seeker needed a median of tens of applications and 2–3 months to reach a first offer.

### Cited Findings
**Source of hire (employer side)**
- Ashby (Oct 17, 2025; about 250K hires, Jan 2021–Jun 2025; read directly) [vendor-platform]: inbound applicants made up 43–52% of hires over four years, reaching a four-year high of **52% of hires in Q2 2025**. **Referrals held steady at ~18%** and sourced hires at **~16%**, while agency hires declined slightly and internal hires rose. Referrals stay at **~17% of hires regardless of a job's inbound volume**, and sourcing dominates jobs with little inbound. Executive roles are filled from inbound only about 35% of the time, and tech jobs skew toward sourcing (24% vs 18% for business roles). — [Ashby, "How many hires really come from inbound?"](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/inbound)
- Several summaries attribute these figures to Ashby: referrals are **~1% of applications but ~18% of hires**, referred candidates get offers at **7.3% vs ~0.2% for inbound** (described as late-2024 data), and **applications per hire roughly tripled from 2021 to 2024**, peaking at about 319 in Q4 2024. One summary also gives about **291 applications per hire in Q1 2026**, though sources disagree on whether that figure covers all roles or only technical ones [secondary; not verified against Ashby]. — [Pin recruitment statistics](https://www.pin.com/blog/recruitment-statistics/); [noon.ai referral statistics](https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/360-employee-referral-statistics-2026); [FMC Group](https://fmcgroup.com/average-applications-per-hire-statistics/)
- Academic evidence [primary, older]: Pallais & Sands (NBER WP 21357, 2015; field experiments on oDesk) found that "referred workers are more likely than non-referred workers to be hired, all else equal" and that referrals carry information about performance and persistence that observable characteristics miss. — [NBER w21357](https://nber.org/papers/w21357) (read directly). A 2023 working paper cites worker and firm surveys showing a referral is used somewhere in hiring for **about half of all jobs** [primary, pre-2024; via search summary]. — [New York Fed Staff Report 987](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr987.pdf)
- I found no 2024–2026 NBER paper that estimates referral effects (see Gaps).

**Seeker-side conversion by channel (Huntr job-tracker telemetry, 2025 annual report, published early 2026; read directly) [vendor-platform; tech-heavy, ~62% US]**
- Data: 1.78M job entries from more than 57K seekers (Jan–Dec 2025). "Response" means the user moved a job to Interview or beyond. Response rates across 598,627 applications: **Google Jobs 11.3%, GovernmentJobs 8.7%, Wellfound 6.0%, Glassdoor 5.5%, Handshake 5.1%, Welcome to the Jungle 4.5%, Indeed 4.5%, LinkedIn 3.1%, ZipRecruiter 2.8%**. LinkedIn was still the largest place seekers *discover and save* jobs in every quarter. — [Huntr 2025 Annual Job Search Trends Report](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report)
- Huntr's Q3 2025 report says "Google's career portal" (most likely careers.google.com, an employer's own career page, rather than the Google Jobs aggregator) had an **11.21% interview rate vs sub-4% on LinkedIn and Indeed**. LinkedIn drove about half of all job saves. — [Huntr Q3 2025](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q3-2025) (read directly). *Note: the annual report labels the same row "Google Jobs", so the label is ambiguous.*
- **LinkedIn Easy Apply**: only **27.5%** of surveyed seekers said it had led to at least one interview in 2025 ("fails to deliver interviews for nearly 3 in 4"). — [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report) (the headline was read directly; the 27.5% figure comes from a search summary)
- Average interview rate per application was **~3.5% in 2025** [secondary summary of Huntr monthly data]. — [Huntr job search statistics](https://huntr.co/blog/job-search-statistics)

**Applications per offer, time to offer (Huntr 2025 annual, read directly) [vendor-platform]**
- Median time to first offer rose from **57 days (Q1) to 63 (Q2), 67 (Q3) and 83 (Q4 2025)**. Application to first interview took **~5.6 days** on average where an interview happened, and interview to offer took a median of **12 days**. Half of seekers who got any interview got their first within **23 days**, while the 90th percentile waited **more than four months**. — [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report)
- Applications per offer: **~7%** of successful users got an offer with fewer than 10 applications, **~16%** with 11–20, **~14%** with 21–30 (so **~38% within 30 applications**), and **18% needed more than 100**. — same source
- Cadence: the median seeker submitted **~16 applications/week**, the 75th percentile 40/week and the 90th percentile **~85/week** (83/week in Q3). — same source; [Huntr Q3 2025](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q3-2025)
- Search length (Huntr survey, n=1,049, Dec 2025): most seekers searched 1–6 months, while **~1 in 4 had searched a year or more** and **~10% more than two years**. — [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report)
- Greenhouse (survey of more than 2,200 workers in the US, UK and Ireland; report Jul 2025; read directly) [vendor-survey]: active seekers had searched **<3 months 34%, 3–6 months 35%, 6–12 months 18%, 1–2 years 8%, >2 years 6%**. — [Greenhouse 2025 Workforce & Hiring Report (PDF)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66bf84e70c6fc57dbbe0187e/687530e016ce4153b861335b_Greenhouse_2025WorkforceAndHiringReport.pdf)

**Cold outreach to recruiters and hiring managers**
- I found no rigorous, independent measurement of job seekers' cold-email reply rates. Vendor blogs give a general B2B cold-email baseline of **~2–3.4% replies** (one cites an Instantly analysis of 100M+ emails at 3.4%) and suggest planning for **5–15%** on targeted job-seeker outreach. One source gives a **7.8% median positive-reply rate** from a "recruiter cold-emailing benchmark" [secondary/vendor, methodology unclear]. One aggregator says explicitly that it could not find a verifiable job-seeker figure. — [whali.com benchmarks](https://whali.com/blog/cold-email-response-rates-benchmarks); [jobply.ai guide](https://jobply.ai/guides/cold-email-hiring-managers); [studojo report](https://studojo.com/reports/do-hiring-managers-read-cold-emails-2026)
- In Enhancv's recruiter interviews (Sep–Oct 2025, n=25 US recruiters; read directly) [vendor-survey, small n], **32% (8/25) recommended proactive LinkedIn outreach (InMail, mutual connections, referrals) to cut through volume**, and 16% said a thoughtful cover letter can tip a borderline decision. — [Enhancv, "Does ATS reject resumes?"](https://enhancv.com/blog/does-ats-reject-resumes/)

**Market context**
- BLS JOLTS for September 2026 (released Nov 3, 2026): job openings **~7.52M** with an openings rate of **4.5%** (up from 4.3% in August), hires **~5.40M**, layoffs ~1.64M and quits ~3.37M [secondary; the BLS release page returned 403 to curl. The 4.5% rate appears in BLS's TED summary title; the hires rate of 3.4% comes from a single press source]. — [BLS TED, Sept 2026](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/job-openings-rate-little-changed-at-4-5-percent-in-september-2026.htm); [BLS JOLTS archive, Nov 3 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/jolts_11032026.htm); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/03/jolts-labor-market-ai-jobs)
- Greenhouse 2025: only **7%** of seekers believe the market favours them, and 66% of US seekers call it extremely or very competitive. — [Greenhouse 2025 Workforce & Hiring Report](https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/greenhouse-2025-workforce-hiring-report) (read directly)

### Inferences
- An agent that maximises raw application count is fighting the channel economics. The highest-yield actions are getting a referral (one referral is worth very roughly 30–40 cold inbound applications, using Ashby's 7.3% vs 0.2% offer rates), applying through employer career pages or niche boards rather than aggregators, and applying early (see section 4).
- The distribution is bimodal: ~38% of successful seekers land within 30 applications and 18% need more than 100. The agent therefore needs to detect a stalled search early and change strategy (target, channel, resume), not just raise volume.
- The gap between where seekers *find* jobs (LinkedIn) and where applications *convert* (career pages, niche boards) suggests a concrete automation: discover a job on LinkedIn, then resolve it to the employer's own ATS posting and apply there.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 peer-reviewed estimate of referral hire rates. The 7.3% vs 0.2% figure is Ashby data that I saw only through secondary summaries.
- No credible job-seeker cold-outreach reply-rate study. All the figures are vendor blogs with unclear definitions.
- Huntr's response rates come from self-reported stage moves on a tech-skewed user base. Agency and recruiter-channel conversion from the seeker side was not found.
- I could not read BLS JOLTS directly (403). Ashby's primary pages on referral conversion and applications per hire were not fetched beyond the inbound report.

## 2. How AI has changed the market: AI-written and auto-submitted applications, recruiter countermeasures, application spam

### Takeaway
AI use by seekers is near-universal (74–86%), and a meaningful minority (about 22% in the US) already use bots or agents to submit applications automatically. Application volume has risen sharply: LinkedIn reported a +45% year-on-year rise to about 11,000 applications per minute in mid-2025. Employers have answered with AI screening, fraud detection, more in-person interviews and a loss of trust, and seekers in turn apply to even more roles. Both Greenhouse's CEO and the press call this an "AI doom loop".

### Cited Findings
- **Volume**: applications via LinkedIn **rose 45% in 2025, to ~11,000 per minute** (reported in the NYT, June 2025). One consultant received 1,200 responses to one post within days. — [Semafor, Jun 24 2025](https://www.semafor.com/article/06/24/2025/recruiters-swamped-with-ai-generated-job-applications) (read directly). Earlier baseline: ~9,000/minute in Jan 2025 [secondary] — [TechCrunch, Jan 15 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/15/linkedin-ads-free-ai-tools-for-job-hunters-and-recruiters/). A LinkedIn representative later gave **~9,500/minute** (Oct 30 2025) [secondary; conflicts with 11,000] — [Recruiting News Network](https://www.recruitingnewsnetwork.com/posts/recruiters-are-drinking-through-a-fire-hose-of-job-applications-experts-say--ai-is-partially-to-blame)
- **Seeker AI use** (Greenhouse AI in Hiring Report, Nov 2025; 4,100+ seekers, recruiters and hiring managers in the US, UK, Ireland and Germany; read directly) [vendor-survey]: **74% of US and 78% of European seekers use AI to job hunt**. **40% of US and 42% of European seekers report using prompt injection to bypass filters**, and 51% of the US non-users would consider it. **49% of US seekers apply to more positions "just to get past automated filters"**, compared with **21% in Europe**. — [Greenhouse 2025 AI in Hiring Report (PDF)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/691b3f85d9304d0f30b57773_3274b13134499af4515ec383ff3b1f01_Greenhouse-2025_AI_in_Hiring_Report.pdf)
- **Auto-apply bots**: **22% of US seekers use bots or AI agents to apply automatically** (31% of Gen Z per the blog summary; UK 17%, Ireland 16%). 45% use AI for interview prep, 22% draft personalised networking messages with AI, 18% use it for salary negotiation, 28% admit generating fake work samples and 32% claim AI skills they lack. **29% hid keywords in white text.** — [Greenhouse 2025 Workforce & Hiring Report (PDF)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66bf84e70c6fc57dbbe0187e/687530e016ce4153b861335b_Greenhouse_2025WorkforceAndHiringReport.pdf); [Greenhouse blog](https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/greenhouse-2025-workforce-hiring-report) (both read directly)
- Huntr Q3 2025 survey (n=527) [vendor-survey]: **85.6% use AI weekly or daily**; **52% would trust AI to apply on their behalf**; **63.2% report AI-mediated rejections**; 71% reject one-way AI interviews (83% in the Dec 2025 survey). — [Huntr Q3 2025](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q3-2025); [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report) (read directly)
- **Employer response** (Greenhouse AI in Hiring, Nov 2025): **53% of US recruiters have handed most screening to AI or the ATS**; 25% are not confident in it and 8% don't know what their AI prioritises. **61% of US hiring managers use software to detect AI use**. **91% of US hiring managers have caught or suspected AI-driven misrepresentation**. 68% of US hiring managers are more involved in hiring than a year earlier, and **39% run more in-person interviews to verify candidates**. 50% of US managers name authenticity as their top challenge, while 52% in the UK name application volume. **46% of seekers say their trust in hiring fell over the past year** (62% of entry-level seekers). — [Greenhouse AI in Hiring Report (PDF)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/691b3f85d9304d0f30b57773_3274b13134499af4515ec383ff3b1f01_Greenhouse-2025_AI_in_Hiring_Report.pdf)
- The Greenhouse CEO described hiring as "an AI doom loop" [secondary]. — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/hiring-in-an-ai-doom-loop-greenhouse/753679/)
- Ashby has shipped "AI-Assisted App Review" (one recruiter reviewed 1,500 applications in 6 hours) and "Fraudulent Candidate Detection", because fake candidates are "creeping into" inbound. — [Ashby inbound report](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/inbound) (read directly)
- UK early careers: the ISE 2025 Student Recruitment Survey found **140 applications per graduate vacancy (2024–25)**, the highest in two decades (38 in 2002–03). The 2025–26 edition reports 141 (172 in London). **92% of employers suspected or confirmed student AI use** (85% a year earlier), 79% are redesigning or reviewing processes, and only 10% have banned AI [secondary; the ISE PDF URL returned 404]. — [ISE survey PDF](https://ise.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/reports/student_recruitment_survey_2025.pdf); [Wonkhe](https://wonkhe.com/blogs/graduate-jobs-and-recruitment-reality/); [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/large-employers-hire-more-apprentices-graduate-jobs-cut-back)

### Inferences
- An auto-apply agent that adds volume makes the problem it is solving worse, and it is likely to be detected and down-weighted: 61% of hiring managers already run AI-detection software, and countermeasures such as in-person rounds are spreading. A design that holds up should favour fewer, verified, honest, targeted applications and must never do prompt injection or keyword stuffing.
- Because recruiters now rely on knockout rules and AI ranking, the agent has to answer knockout questions accurately (work authorisation, location, sponsorship). Misrepresentation is a fraud-detection risk as well as an ethics problem.

### Gaps
- No primary LinkedIn statement for the 11,000/min figure (it traces to the NYT). Its share of AI-generated or auto-submitted applications is not measured anywhere I found.
- No hard data on how much AI-sounding text lowers callback rates. The evidence is recruiter opinion only.

## 3. What seekers spend time on, and which tasks they want automated

### Takeaway
Survey data on hours by task is thin. The strongest signals are behavioural: tailoring works (about 1.5–1.6x the conversion of a generic resume) yet only about 6% of tracked applications use it; manual re-entry into ATS forms makes 46% of US seekers abandon applications; ghosting makes tracking and follow-up painful; and seekers already use AI most for interview prep (45%), resume and keyword work, outreach drafting (22%) and salary research (18%).

### Cited Findings
- **Tailoring**: tailored resumes convert to interview, offer or hire at **5.8% vs 3.73%** for untailored (1.6x). Only 65,088 applications were tailored, against more than 1M untailored. — [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report). Q3 2025: **6.5% vs 4.3%**. — [Huntr Q3 2025](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q3-2025) (both read directly) [vendor-platform; Huntr sells a tailoring tool]
- Resume format (Huntr 2025, about 128K resumes): two-page resumes had the highest interview rate (**3.24%**), against 3.06% for one page, 2.6% for three and 2.12% for four or more. Including a LinkedIn URL correlated with interviews (71.7% vs 64.3%). — [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report)
- **Form friction**: **46% of US candidates abandon applications because they must manually re-enter resume information**. 31% abandon when there is no autofill (44% of Gen Z). In the UK, 42% cite re-entry and 41% multi-page forms that don't save progress. — [Greenhouse 2025 Workforce & Hiring Report (PDF)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66bf84e70c6fc57dbbe0187e/687530e016ce4153b861335b_Greenhouse_2025WorkforceAndHiringReport.pdf) (read directly)
- **Ghosting**: **63% of US candidates were ghosted after a job interview** (78% of Gen Z, 55% of Boomers); UK 56%, Ireland 60%. **50% of US candidates have ghosted employers.** — same source. Huntr: "nearly 90% of job seekers experience being left without closure after interviews". — [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report)
- **Bait-and-switch**: **72%** of candidates say the job turned out different from the posting. — [Greenhouse blog](https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/greenhouse-2025-workforce-hiring-report)
- **What seekers use AI for (US, Greenhouse 2025)**: interview prep 45%, highlighting relevant experience 43%, interview questions 24%, drafting personalised connection messages 22%, an agent applying on their behalf 22%, salary negotiation and compensation evaluation 18%, take-home assignments 18%. — [Greenhouse 2025 Workforce & Hiring Report (PDF)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66bf84e70c6fc57dbbe0187e/687530e016ce4153b861335b_Greenhouse_2025WorkforceAndHiringReport.pdf)
- **Salary research**: only **30.71%** of jobs saved on Huntr list salary. Disclosure varies by state, from about two-thirds of tech listings in Washington and Oregon to under a third in Texas and Florida. — [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report)
- **Time spent**: Howdy (late 2025, n=812) puts the average at **~6 hours/week and ~7 applications/week**, with 76% burned out [vendor-survey; secondary, the page returned 429]. — [Howdy job hunter survey](https://howdy.com/blog/job-hunter-survey). Clutch (n=590 US): **86% say AI helped them submit more applications per week** [secondary; 403]. — [Clutch state of AI hiring](https://clutch.co/resources/state-ai-hiring). ResumeGenius (n≈900): seekers know tailoring works but struggle to do it, and customising resumes, writing cover letters and filling in forms take several hours a week [secondary; 403]. — [ResumeGenius state of job hunting](https://resumegenius.com/blog/job-hunting/state-of-job-hunting)
- **Trust limits on automation**: only 52% would trust AI to apply on their behalf, and 83% reject AI interviews. — [Huntr Q3 2025](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q3-2025); [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report)

### Inferences
- These are the most valuable automations, ranked by evidence of pain multiplied by evidence of payoff:
  1. Per-job tailoring of resume and answers: a proven ~1.5x lift that few seekers do.
  2. ATS form-filling and autofill from one profile: a 46% abandonment driver.
  3. Tracking plus follow-up and ghosting detection: 63–90% ghosting, and a 12-day median from interview to offer that needs nudging.
  4. Interview prep: already the top AI use.
  5. Finding referrers and contacts and drafting personalised outreach: highest yield (section 1), already used by 22%.
  6. Salary research where postings omit pay.
  7. Search and filter with de-duplication, and resolving each posting to the employer's career page.
- The fact that only about half of seekers trust AI to apply argues for approval gates before submission: an agent that drafts everything and lets the person approve, rather than one that submits silently.

### Gaps
- Primary sources I could not reach or did not find: LinkedIn, Glassdoor, Jobscan, Teal, Pew, Gartner and SHRM 2025–2026 surveys on hours by task or desired automation. No study breaks seeker time down by task (search, tailoring, outreach, interviews).
- The Howdy, Clutch and ResumeGenius figures come from search summaries only; their pages blocked fetching.

## 4. The recruiter and hiring-manager side: what they read, ATS screening in practice, welcome outreach vs spam

### Takeaway
ATS software rarely auto-rejects on content. Rejection happens through knockout questions (above all work authorisation and sponsorship) and through recruiters who stop reading once the first few hundred applications arrive. Applying early, a clean one- or two-page reverse-chronological resume, and a human route in (referral, InMail, mutual contact) matter more than keyword stuffing.

### Cited Findings
- Enhancv (25 structured interviews with US recruiters, Sep–Oct 2025, published Nov 2025; organisations from 120 to 50,000+ employees on more than 10 ATSs including Workday, iCIMS and Greenhouse; read directly) [vendor-survey, small n]:
  - **92% (23/25) said their systems do not auto-reject for formatting, content or design**. **Only 2 of 25 used content-based auto-rejection** (for example a skills gate such as "reject if fewer than 7 of 10 required skills"). The common pattern is **knockout rules for eligibility plus human triage guided by scores**.
  - One recruiter said the sponsorship and residency knockout question alone removes **~30%** of applicants for technical roles.
  - Recruiter estimates of volume: entry-level and admin **400–600 applicants per opening**; customer-service and remote support **1,000+ in week one**; remote or hybrid tech **2,000+**; specialised or senior roles **under 200**.
  - **52% said applying early improves chances**, because they review in order of arrival. Several pause or unpost a role after about 300–1,000 applicants and interview from the first batch; 36% said timing makes little difference.
  - Turn-offs: Canva-style designed resumes (20%), 7–8 page resumes (8%), oldest-first ordering (8%). 68% said the "75% auto-reject" myth reached them from seekers repeating social media posts.
  — [Enhancv, "The ATS Rejection Myth"](https://enhancv.com/blog/does-ats-reject-resumes/)
- Greenhouse: **53% of US recruiters have handed most screening to AI or the ATS**, which conflicts in tone with Enhancv's "ATS doesn't reject". The two are reconcilable if AI *ranks* applications and humans read only the top. — [Greenhouse AI in Hiring Report (PDF)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/691b3f85d9304d0f30b57773_3274b13134499af4515ec383ff3b1f01_Greenhouse-2025_AI_in_Hiring_Report.pdf)
- 55% of US candidates suspect AI evaluated their application without their being told, and 87% want employers to be transparent about AI [secondary for the 87%]. — same PDF; [Greenhouse newsroom](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair)
- Hiring managers are more hands-on: 68% (US) and 65% (Europe) are more involved than a year earlier. — same PDF

### Inferences
- The agent should (a) answer knockout questions truthfully and filter out roles the person is ineligible for before applying, (b) apply within the first days of a posting (monitor new postings and keep latency low), and (c) produce a plain, parseable resume rather than an "ATS-gamed" one.
- Outreach should look like a referral: specific, low-volume and tied to a mutual connection or a concrete role. That is the behaviour recruiters recommend; mass templated InMail is what they ignore. Evidence for the "spam" side is anecdotal only.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of how long recruiters spend per resume in 2025–2026 (older "6–7 second" eye-tracking claims are pre-2024 and not verified here).
- No data on which outreach features (length, personalisation, mutual connection) raise reply rates.

## 5. Differences by seeker type and region

### Takeaway
Entry-level and Gen Z seekers face the hardest market: the highest ghosting, the most AI use and the largest trust loss. UK graduate schemes see about 140 applications per vacancy. Senior and executive roles are filled less through inbound and more through sourcing and networks. US seekers react to AI filters with more volume (49%) than Europeans (21%).

### Cited Findings
- **New grads (US)**: the New York Fed reports the **recent-graduate unemployment rate at 5.2% in Q3 2026** (lowest in more than a year) and **underemployment at 42.5%**. — [NY Fed, Labor Market for Recent College Graduates](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market) (read directly) [primary]
- **Gen Z**: 78% ghosted after an interview (vs 55% of Boomers); 45% say it's harder than ever to stand out; 31% use auto-apply bots; 44% abandon applications that lack autofill. Entry-level trust loss is 62%. — [Greenhouse 2025 Workforce & Hiring Report (PDF)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66bf84e70c6fc57dbbe0187e/687530e016ce4153b861335b_Greenhouse_2025WorkforceAndHiringReport.pdf); [Greenhouse AI in Hiring](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/691b3f85d9304d0f30b57773_3274b13134499af4515ec383ff3b1f01_Greenhouse-2025_AI_in_Hiring_Report.pdf). Huntr: 63% of Gen Z use AI daily, nearly double the Boomer rate. — [Huntr Q3 2025](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q3-2025)
- **Senior and executive roles**: about 35% filled via inbound (vs ~52% overall), and tech roles lean on sourcing (24%). — [Ashby inbound](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/inbound). Recruiters estimate fewer than 200 applicants for senior or specialised roles, vetted more deeply. — [Enhancv](https://enhancv.com/blog/does-ats-reject-resumes/). Resumes that reached interviews on Huntr had a median of 11 years' experience vs 9.92 for those that did not. — [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report)
- **Career switchers**: enterprises accept equivalent experience in place of degrees at nearly twice the rate of startups (13% vs 7%), and infosec leads with 1 in 5 roles open without traditional credentials. — [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report). (No switcher-specific conversion data found.)
- **UK/EU vs US**: US seekers are the most pessimistic (44% not confident vs 31% in Ireland). Applying to more roles to beat filters: US 49% vs Europe 21%. Top hiring challenge: US managers cite authenticity (50%), UK managers volume (52%). Removing older experience from a resume: US 57%, UK 64%, Ireland 69%. Only 27% in Germany report lost trust. — [Greenhouse 2025 Workforce & Hiring](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66bf84e70c6fc57dbbe0187e/687530e016ce4153b861335b_Greenhouse_2025WorkforceAndHiringReport.pdf); [Greenhouse AI in Hiring](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/691b3f85d9304d0f30b57773_3274b13134499af4515ec383ff3b1f01_Greenhouse-2025_AI_in_Hiring_Report.pdf). UK graduates: 140–141 applications per vacancy, 172 in London. — [ISE via Wonkhe](https://wonkhe.com/blogs/graduate-jobs-and-recruitment-reality/)
- **Pay transparency**: in the US, salary disclosure follows state law (about 66% of tech listings in WA and OR, under 33% in TX and FL). — [Huntr 2025 annual](https://huntr.co/research/2025-annual-job-search-trends-report). (The EU Pay Transparency Directive's June 2026 transposition deadline is relevant but was not verified in this round.)

### Inferences
- New grads need help with volume and process (structured schemes, assessments, form-filling, tracking), while senior seekers need network mapping and targeted outreach, because inbound fills only about a third of executive roles. The agent should switch strategy by seniority.
- The European market reacts less with volume and is shaped more by GDPR and works councils. An EU mode should be more conservative about scraping contacts and finding emails.
