# Rewind moves the agent, never the data

- **Status:** research report for todo U7 ("Round U" research), 2026-11-18. It builds on three sibling reports, each fact-checked and merged: U4 on [undo in agent UIs](../agent-ui/undo-agent-edits.md), U5 on [server rollback](server-rollback.md) and U6 on [sagas](sagas.md).
- **Versions checked (as of November 2026):** `@langchain/langgraph` 1.6.0 with `langchain` 1.6.1, and Python `langgraph` 1.2.22; Pi (`@earendil-works/pi-coding-agent`, `pi-agent-core`) 1.5.2; `@anthropic-ai/claude-agent-sdk` 0.3.327; Python `google-adk` 2.14.1 and TypeScript `@google/adk` 2.5.0; `@openai/agents` 0.21.0; `ai` 7.0.162; `agents` 0.33.0 with `@cloudflare/think` 0.23.0; `@github/copilot-sdk` 1.0.31.
- **Evidence:** two research notes, [agent rewind mechanisms](notes/agent-rewind-mechanisms.md) and [rollback in our SDKs and plans](notes/rollback-in-our-sdks-and-plans.md). They cite the LangChain docs mirror at commit `9d9747d`, the Pi docs at tag `v1.5.2`, code.claude.com, the ADK docs mirror at `ff7a5f5` and adk-python at tag `v2.14.1`, the installed `.d.ts` files and this repo's plans, all read on 2026-11-18. Corrections are logged in [fact-check-rewind.md](fact-check-rewind.md).
- **Verified:** every number, version, date, status and behavioural claim was re-read against its primary source on 2026-11-18, including the installed LangGraph and Pi source where the docs were silent or disagreed with the pin. Nothing here ran SDK code or a model. Fetched pages were read as data, and instructions in them were not followed.
- **Recommendation, not sourced:** the three treatments for triage actions, the outcome field, the rollback record and every specific number in them, such as the 24-hour undo window. Three ideas come from the unreviewed sibling reports and are our own proposals, not findings: the outcome kinds `not_applied`, `compensated`, `partial` and `unknown` (U5); the refund as the pivot of a saga, and one approval over the whole plan (U6); and guarded inverse writes rather than snapshot restores (U4).

When a support agent sends the wrong reply or escalates the wrong ticket, its framework's "go back" button cannot help, because every such feature restores only state the framework itself stored. LangGraph time travel reloads a checkpoint and then **re-executes** every later node, so API calls fire again. Pi's `/tree`, `/fork` and `/clone` move a pointer in an append-only session tree. Claude Code's `/rewind` restores pre-edit copies of the files that its own Write, Edit and NotebookEdit tools changed. ADK Python's `rewind_async` appends an event that carries inverse deltas for session-scoped state and artifacts. LangGraph, ADK and Claude Code each say in their own words that external effects sit outside that boundary, and Pi's docs describe only session-file operations. **Rewind changes what the model sees next; it never reverses a write to someone else's system.**

Our own stack repeats that split as of November 2026. All eight pinned SDKs can rewind or fork the conversation, and two (Claude, Copilot) can restore local files. None restores external data, none offers compensation, and none has a "compensated" or "partial" result kind. Our harness plan specifies agent rewind carefully, with an immutable log, `fork`, a log-only `revert` and a non-executing `playback`. It specifies no data rollback, though: no idempotency key, no `unknown` outcome, no inverse helpdesk method and no rollback entry. In this report, I trace four rewind mechanisms to their boundary, map what our SDKs and plans expose, and show why a rollback must be a new, linked entry in the session log. I then recommend three treatments for the triage agent: **undo windows** for triage fields and notes, **approval first** for escalations and replies, and **a saga with the refund as its pivot** once money can move.

## Four rewind mechanisms restore only what their framework owns

The four mechanisms differ in what they snapshot, but they share one design choice. Going back changes what the model sees next and, at most, restores local state the framework captured. The table summarizes them, and the paragraphs below give the evidence.

| Mechanism (version, Nov 2026) | What it captures | What restoring does | Stated boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| LangGraph checkpoints and time travel (`@langchain/langgraph` 1.6.0; Python 1.2.22) | A `StateSnapshot` per super-step, per `thread_id` | Replay re-runs every node after the checkpoint; `update_state` forks a new branch | "payments, emails, external writes" are outside the boundary |
| Pi session tree (`pi-coding-agent` 1.5.2) | JSONL entries with `id` and `parentId` | Moves the active leaf or copies a branch; nothing is erased | Session file only; file restore exists only in an example extension |
| Claude Code checkpoints and `/rewind` (Agent SDK 0.3.327) | Pre-edit copies of files changed by Write, Edit and NotebookEdit, one checkpoint per prompt | Restores code, conversation or both | Bash-only changes, most subagent edits, directories and remote files are not tracked |
| ADK session rewind (Python `google-adk` 2.14.1; absent in TS 2.5.0) | The session's event log with state and artifact deltas | Appends a rewind event with inverse deltas for session-scoped state and artifacts | App, user and temp state are not restored; external systems are "your responsibility" |

**LangGraph re-runs work instead of undoing it.** It treats a checkpoint as "a snapshot of the graph state saved at each super-step", and execution can resume only from such a boundary ([checkpointers.mdx](https://github.com/langchain-ai/docs/blob/9d9747d0cf241e086c616a16d5ce230ad8f8f31d/src/oss/langgraph/checkpointers.mdx)). Time travel comes in two forms. Replay keeps the nodes before the checkpoint but re-runs the nodes after it, "including any LLM calls, API requests, and interrupts (which may produce different results)", because replay "doesn't just read from cache". Fork through `update_state` "does **not** roll back a thread. It creates a new checkpoint that branches from the specified point" ([use-time-travel.mdx](https://github.com/langchain-ai/docs/blob/9d9747d0cf241e086c616a16d5ce230ad8f8f31d/src/oss/langgraph/use-time-travel.mdx)). Interrupts behave the same way. On resume, "the runtime restarts the entire node from the beginning", so a record update made before the `interrupt` can run again and create duplicates ([interrupts.mdx](https://github.com/langchain-ai/docs/blob/9d9747d0cf241e086c616a16d5ce230ad8f8f31d/src/oss/langgraph/interrupts.mdx)).

**LangGraph's fault-tolerance page states the boundary most plainly, and its durability modes protect only local state.** A retry gives "**at-least-once** semantics for node execution", and graph writes from a failed attempt are discarded, but "Side effects during the node (payments, emails, external writes) are outside that boundary." The same page says the idempotency key must be durable *before* the side effect runs. After a lost acknowledgement, it says to "Treat missing local state as unknown, not as proof the effect did not happen" ([fault-tolerance.mdx](https://github.com/langchain-ai/docs/blob/9d9747d0cf241e086c616a16d5ce230ad8f8f31d/src/oss/langgraph/fault-tolerance.mdx)). The durability modes (`exit`, `async`, `sync`) decide only how much *local* state survives a crash. For JS, the docs mirror warns that even `sync` "can still lose recent checkpoints" mid-run. That page tracks `main`, however, and under `sync` the pinned 1.6.0 source awaits every pending checkpointer write at the end of each super-step (`@langchain/langgraph/dist/pregel/loop.js:661`). For our pin, then, JS `sync` matches Python's.

**Pi moves a pointer and leaves files alone.** It stores each session as a tree, "so returning to an earlier point does not erase the branch you leave". `/tree` moves within the session file, `/fork` creates a new session from an earlier user message, and `/clone` copies the active branch. The model then receives "the active branch, not every branch in the session file" ([sessions.md @ v1.5.2](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi/blob/v1.5.2/packages/coding-agent/docs/sessions.md)). Our example 05 verified that `branch(id)` moves the leaf while both paths stay in `getEntries()` ([Pi guide](../../reference/pi/guide.md)). The 1.5.2 source confirms that Pi core restores no files. `AgentSession.navigateTree()`, which serves `/tree`, calls only session-manager methods (`branch`, `branchWithSummary`, `resetLeaf`) and touches no file (`pi-coding-agent/dist/core/agent-session.js:3363`). The only file restore in Pi's docs is the `git-checkpoint.ts` example extension. It runs `git stash create` on each `turn_start` and, on `session_before_fork`, asks "Restore code state?" before applying the stash. It does nothing without a UI and does not hook `/tree` ([git-checkpoint.ts @ v1.5.2](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi/blob/v1.5.2/packages/coding-agent/examples/extensions/git-checkpoint.ts)).

**Claude Code restores only files its own edit tools touched.** It "automatically tracks Claude's file edits", and "Every prompt you send that starts a turn creates a new checkpoint". `/rewind` (also `/undo`, or Esc twice) offers to restore code and conversation, the conversation alone, or the code alone. Claude Code keeps file snapshots for the 100 most recent checkpoints in a session, and a retention sweep deletes them by default about 30 days after the session last saved one ([Claude Code checkpointing](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/checkpointing.md)). The limits define the boundary: "A file that only Bash commands changed isn't tracked and stays as it is. To revert it, use version control, such as Git." Subagent edits are usually not captured, and the page calls checkpoints a tool for "quick, session-level recovery". The Agent SDK exposes the same machinery as `enableFileCheckpointing` and `Query.rewindFiles(userMessageId, { dryRun })`. A rewind "deletes the files it created and restores the files it modified", "does not rewind the conversation itself", and leaves directory operations and "Remote or network files" untouched ([Agent SDK file checkpointing](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/file-checkpointing.md)). Conversation rewind is a separate surface. `resumeSessionAt` truncates a resume at a message UUID, and `forkSession` copies the transcript, though "Forked sessions start without undo history" (`claude-agent-sdk/sdk.d.ts:898`). Because the two rewinds are independent, a user can roll back the transcript while files, and every external effect, stay as they are.

**ADK rewinds by event sourcing, and only for session-scoped state.** In Python (since v1.17.0) and Kotlin (since v0.3.0), `runner.rewind_async(user_id=..., session_id=..., rewind_before_invocation_id=...)` undoes an invocation and everything after it. "All requests, including rewound requests, are preserved in the log", and the model "effectively forgets" the rewound turns ([ADK rewind docs](https://github.com/google/adk-docs/blob/ff7a5f56469a81f3e43a8fc54668d8dc84300192/docs/sessions/session/rewind.md)). The v2.14.1 source shows the mechanism. It replays state deltas up to the rewind point and keeps only session-scoped keys, dropping `app:`, `user:` and `temp:`. Keys first written later are set to `None`, new artifacts get a "rewind tombstone", and changed artifacts are restored by **saving the old version as a new version**. Then it appends a user-authored event that carries the inverse deltas ([_rewind_utils.py @ v2.14.1](https://github.com/google/adk-python/blob/v2.14.1/src/google/adk/sessions/_rewind_utils.py)). A single `_apply_rewinds` function decides which events are live for both prompt building and compaction, because "otherwise rewound content can leak back into prompts through a compaction summary" ([_rewind_events.py @ v2.14.1](https://github.com/google/adk-python/blob/v2.14.1/src/google/adk/events/_rewind_events.py)). The docs state two limits. Rewind "does not manage external dependencies", and restoring those systems "is your responsibility". State updates, artifact updates and event persistence are also "not performed in a single atomic transaction", so active sessions should not be rewound ([ADK rewind docs](https://github.com/google/adk-docs/blob/ff7a5f56469a81f3e43a8fc54668d8dc84300192/docs/sessions/session/rewind.md)).

**The TypeScript ADK that we pin has no rewind at all.** No file in the `dist` folder of `@google/adk` 2.5.0 mentions the word. Our gap list already records session rewind as a Python feature the port lacks ([our TS gap list](../../comparison/typescript-gaps.md)). adk-js `main` (commit `0fcab55`, 2026-11-17) has no rewind method either. Its only mention is a BigQuery analytics field that copies `rewindBeforeInvocationId` from an event ([event_row.ts](https://github.com/google/adk-js/blob/0fcab5503afa44012207b995864e59ff9012653d/integrations/src/bigquery_analytics/event_row.ts)).

## Rewind cannot reverse external writes, and replay tends to add them

The vendor statements share three causes. They are our synthesis, not any single vendor's claim. First, **the framework never snapshotted the external state**. A checkpoint, a session tree, session-scoped deltas and pre-edit file copies are all state the framework wrote itself. The helpdesk's ticket row or the payment provider's ledger was never captured, so the framework has nothing to restore from and no write access to restore with. Second, **many external effects are irreversible by nature**. A delivered email or a disbursed payment can only be offset by a new forward action that the remote system supports, such as a refund or a correction. The saga literature calls that offset compensation and defines it semantically: a compensating transaction "does not necessarily return the database to the state that existed" before ([Garcia-Molina and Salem, 1987](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/andru/cs711/2002fa/reading/sagas.pdf)). Choosing the offset is application logic, which no generic rewind can supply. Third, **rewind and replay add effects instead of removing them**. LangGraph's replay fires API requests again, and a node resumed after an interrupt repeats its pre-interrupt side effects ([use-time-travel.mdx](https://github.com/langchain-ai/docs/blob/9d9747d0cf241e086c616a16d5ce230ad8f8f31d/src/oss/langgraph/use-time-travel.mdx); [interrupts.mdx](https://github.com/langchain-ai/docs/blob/9d9747d0cf241e086c616a16d5ce230ad8f8f31d/src/oss/langgraph/interrupts.mdx)).

**A rewind that hides past tool calls makes repeats more likely.** ADK's model "effectively forgets" rewound turns ([ADK rewind docs](https://github.com/google/adk-docs/blob/ff7a5f56469a81f3e43a8fc54668d8dc84300192/docs/sessions/session/rewind.md)), and Claude Code can restore the conversation while keeping the code ([checkpointing](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/checkpointing.md)). In both cases the external effect still exists, but the model can no longer see that it caused it, so it may propose the same escalation or reply again. Even local rewind can fail partway. GitHub's Copilot SDK 1.0.31 has an experimental `session.rpc.history.rewind`, documented as "Not crash-atomic: file restore and conversation truncation are separate stores". Its typed outcomes include `rollback-incomplete`, and one per-file skip reason reads "The file changed after Copilot's last captured write" (`copilot-sdk/dist/generated/rpc.d.ts:3179, 3242, 45632`). If a vendor's own local-file rewind can end partial, a remote one cannot be assumed whole.

**Agent rewind and data rollback are different operations with different owners.** Rewind answers "what should the model see next?" and belongs to the harness. Data rollback answers "what is true in the helpdesk now?" and belongs to the tools and the systems behind them. No framework surveyed ties the two together, and we found no vendor documentation of compensation or saga support attached to rewind as of November 2026.

## Our SDKs and plans rewind the conversation and roll back no data

Across the eight SDKs we pin, rewind is always *agent* rewind. The table condenses what each SDK exposes, from our reference guides and pinned `.d.ts` files. "[verified]" marks a surface one of our runnable examples checked, and "[d.ts]" marks one that only a type declaration shows.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude Agent SDK 0.3.327 | `resumeSessionAt`, `forkSession`; `rewindFiles()` for checkpointed local files | None | `deny` with `message`, `interrupt` | None |
| Pi 1.5.2 | Append-only tree: `branch()` [verified]; `branchWithSummary()`, `fork()` [d.ts] | None | `block: true` becomes an error result; `isError` | None |
| OpenAI Agents 0.21.0 | `RunState` resumes a *paused* run; `Session.popItem()` | `operationId` on session-history transactions only | `reject({ message })`, `errorFunction` | None |

| Google ADK 2.5.0 (TS) | None (rewind is Python-only) | None | `{ error }`; "This tool call is rejected." [verified] | None |

Sources: [Claude guide](../../reference/claude-agent-sdk/guide.md), [Pi guide](../../reference/pi/guide.md), [OpenAI guide](../../reference/openai-agents-sdk/guide.md), [Vercel guide](../../reference/vercel-ai-sdk/guide.md), [LangChain guide](../../reference/langchain/guide.md), [Cloudflare guide](../../reference/cloudflare-agents/guide.md), [ADK guide](../../reference/google-adk/guide.md) and [ADK fact-check](../../reference/google-adk/fact-check.md), [Copilot guide](../../reference/copilot-sdk/guide.md), the [feature table](../../comparison/feature-table.md), and the pinned `.d.ts` files under `verify/<sdk>/node_modules/`.

**Two SDK details matter for our design.** OpenAI's history transactions already have the contract a tool idempotency key needs: an `operationId` that "remains stable when this transaction is retried", where repeating it with the same transaction "must succeed without applying the transaction again". OpenAI applies the contract only to conversation history, though, not to tool side effects (`agents-core/dist/memory/session.d.ts:110-121`). Copilot's `ToolResultType` comes closest to telling the model whether an effect happened, yet it still has no `compensated` or `partial` value (`copilot-sdk/dist/types.d.ts:526`). Whichever SDK we had chosen, data rollback would remain ours to build.

**Our plans repeat the split.** The harness plan's `Session` offers `checkout`, `fork(entryId)`, `revert(entryId)` ("a new immutable file; moves the pointer"), `playback()` with "no model or tool calls", and `rerunFrom()`, which "branches, then calls the model again" ([build-harness.md §3.2](../../plans/build-harness.md#32-session-log-api)). That agent-rewind design avoids LangGraph's re-execution trap by naming playback and re-run as separate modes ([harness guide §2](../../guides/agent-harness.md#2-the-session-log-should-be-one-append-only-tree-of-typed-entries-with-head-a-writer-lock-and-pauses-as-entries)). On the data side, the plans have one idempotency mechanism, `SessionStore.append`, which is "idempotent by id" with an `expectedLastId` check, and the entry union has no rollback, compensation or undo entry ([build-harness.md §3.2](../../plans/build-harness.md#32-session-log-api)). `ToolResult` carries a single `isError` flag, and `CodeCallResult` is only `done | denied | awaiting_approval` ([build-sdk.md §3.3](../../plans/build-sdk.md#33-sdk-api); [build-harness.md §3.4](../../plans/build-harness.md#34-permission-approval-and-sandbox-apis)). The `Helpdesk` interface returns a `WriteReceipt { id, shadow, at }` from each write, but no write takes an idempotency key, and the interface has no inverse methods ([build-agent.md §3.2](../../plans/build-agent.md#32-trusted-run-context-helpdesk-and-tool-contracts)). The agent guide labels T1 writes "undoable" and T2 writes "reversible but costs people's attention", yet no document says who reverses them, how, or within what window ([agent guide §5](../../guides/agent.md#5-safety-is-a-policy-table-and-a-context-split-tier-every-tool-by-worst-case-effect-bind-identity-and-cut-the-outbound-leg)). The plan review raised no finding on any of this, so rollback is an unreviewed area, not a rejected one ([plan review](../../plans/reviews/plan-review.md)).

**Three risks follow from those gaps.** First, the plans test "runs once" and "exactly once" only on a clean resume, in N13, N40, B15b and the A15 capstone ([N13](../../todos/done/N13-signed-approvals.md); [N40](../../todos/ready/N40-code-initiated-calls.md); [B15b](../../todos/done/B15b-continue-run.md); [A15](../../todos/ready/A15-agent-capstone.md)). The reply is a code-initiated call, so a crash after the helpdesk accepts it but before its `tool_result` lands leaves a `code_call` entry with no result. N7's crash rule covers only open tool calls in a turn, which get an "aborted error result" ([N7](../../todos/done/N7-rebuild-resume-replay.md)). Either way, the log cannot say whether the reply went out, and a re-proposed reply could go out twice. Second, `revert` and `rerunFrom` past a sent reply or an escalation leave the log and the helpdesk disagreeing, with no entry that says so ([N5](../../todos/done/N5-head-fork-revert.md)). Third, the A13 workflow maps every stop to a handoff "without retry" but does not say what happens to writes already made before the stop ([A13](../../todos/ready/A13-triage-workflow.md)). Whether real helpdesks accept idempotency keys on writes is still unknown, because the owner has not yet chosen the helpdesk ([A24](../../todos/ready/A24-real-helpdesk-adapter.md)).

## Triage needs undo windows for T1, approval for anything that leaves the org, and a saga only once money moves

**Each action's treatment should follow from one question: can our system take the effect back before anyone outside the org notices?** Reversible internal writes get an **undo window**: they apply at once and stay reversible by a guarded inverse write for a bounded time. Irreversible or attention-costing actions get **approval first**. A multi-step plan whose steps cannot all be undone gets a **saga**, with its steps ordered around the one irreversible step, the pivot. The table applies that rule to the planned tools ([agent guide §5](../../guides/agent.md#5-safety-is-a-policy-table-and-a-context-split-tier-every-tool-by-worst-case-effect-bind-identity-and-cut-the-outbound-leg); [A4](../../todos/ready/A4-read-internal-tools.md); [A5](../../todos/ready/A5-outbound-money-tools.md)). The rule and the table are our recommendation, not a sourced design.

| Action (tier) | Treatment | Undo or compensation | What must be added |
|---|---|---|---|
| `support_set_triage` (T1) | Undo window | Guarded restore of the prior category and priority | Read and store the prior value; a `restoreTriage` method that checks the current value still equals the agent's |
| `support_add_internal_note` (T1) | Undo window | Retract the note, or append a correction if the helpdesk cannot delete | A retract method or a correction-note convention |
| `support_escalate` (T2) | Approval first, as planned | No undo, because a page cannot be unsent; offset by a "stand down" note | Record the page as `not_reversible` in the result |
| `support_send_reply` and template questions (T3) | Approval of exact text, then a short deferred-send window | Cancel only while the send is still held; afterwards, a correction is a new T3 reply | A real send delay and a cancel endpoint |
| `billing_issue_refund`, `billing_issue_credit` (T4) | Approval first (denied in v1); the saga pivot when a plan includes one | None by API; a person handles any reversal | Business-keyed idempotency, plan approval bound to order state |
| `handoff` | Not gated | A person can route the ticket back | Carry the list of live effects into the handoff |

**Triage fields and internal notes get undo windows.** The guide already calls these writes "undoable", and the canary stage auto-applies `category` and `priority` ([agent guide §7](../../guides/agent.md#7-ship-in-four-stages-behind-a-graded-kill-switch-hand-off-to-people-by-design-and-turn-each-incident-into-a-regression-task)), so confirming them first would only spend reviewer attention. Nielsen's advice points the same way: reserve confirmation for "actions with serious consequences" and "go to great lengths to provide undo" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)). The undo must be a guarded compensating write, not a snapshot restore. Restoring "as of T" silently overwrites any human change made after T. The sibling U4 report finds the same failure in snapshot undo libraries once an agent and a person edit the same state ([U4 report](../agent-ui/undo-agent-edits.md)). So `setTriage` should read the current values before writing, store them in the result's `details`, and expose an inverse that refuses with `not_applied` when the ticket no longer holds the agent's values. We recommend that the window close at the earlier of two events: the first human edit to the same field, or a fixed age of 24 hours. The 24 hours match the T3 and T4 quorum windows in [AD3](../../plans/build-agent.md#ad3-a-role-based-quorum-per-tier-and-amount-band-bound-to-the-host-signed-pause-and-expiring-on-age-or-policy-change-accepted-with-sdk-d5-and-hd5), but the bound is our judgement, not a sourced figure. Likewise, a queue change that triggers helpdesk automations is no longer purely internal, so we recommend moving it to T2.

**Replies and escalations stay approval-first, and approved replies get a deferred send.** A reply reaches an outsider and cannot be recalled, and an escalation pages people; every rewind above leaves both effects in place. Approval of the exact text is already planned for T3 ([AD2](../../plans/build-agent.md#ad2-the-tier-table-is-code-t5-is-never-registered-and-t4-stays-denied-until-the-owner-sets-the-caps-accepted)). After approval, the one honest undo follows Gmail's model. Undo Send lets a user retract a sent message within a "Send cancellation period of 5, 10, 20, or 30 seconds" ([Gmail help](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/2819488?hl=en)). The page does not say how retraction works, so our reading is that Gmail holds the message for that period. A reply tool can hold the approved message in an outbox for a short, configurable delay and accept a cancel until it leaves. After that, the only remedy is a correction reply, which is a new T3 action needing its own approval. An escalation has no deferred form worth building, because its purpose is to reach people quickly. Its offset is a "stand down" note, and the result should list the page under `not_reversible` so the model never claims the escalation was undone.

**Refunds and credits become the pivot of a saga, approved once for the whole plan.** In v1, T4 is denied while `caps` is `null`, so this design is for the next phase ([AD2](../../plans/build-agent.md#ad2-the-tier-table-is-code-t5-is-never-registered-and-t4-stays-denied-until-the-owner-sets-the-caps-accepted)). Once T4 opens, a ticket whose plan sets triage, issues a refund and replies becomes a saga, in the step order the sibling U6 report proposes ([U6 report](sagas.md)). The compensable step, the triage write holding a `refund_pending` marker, runs first. The refund comes next as the pivot, which Microsoft defines as "the point of no return" ([Azure saga pattern](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/saga)). An API caller cannot rely on reversing a card refund, because "Only Dashboard cancellations are currently supported for card refunds" ([Stripe refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds)). The reply runs last, idempotent by message id. In our synthesis, **replying before the refund is the order to forbid**: a "your refund is on its way" message followed by a failed refund leaves a false statement that nothing can compensate.

**The one approval must cover the whole plan and expire when the order changes.** Nothing after the pivot may wait on a person, so the approval must come before the first step and cover every step's exact arguments, including the reply text. It must also expire if the order state it was computed from changes. In practice, the harness hashes the order version and the amount already refunded at approval time. If either differs at execution, the plan fails closed, a countermeasure Microsoft calls "reread values" ([Azure saga pattern](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/saga)). Both rules are our synthesis, following U6; no vendor source discusses approval relative to a pivot.

**The refund call needs a business-keyed idempotency key.** Derive it from a business key such as `refund:{orderId}`, combined with the tenant and call id. Stripe's docs warn that "A new key can start a second operation" ([Stripe advanced error handling](https://docs.stripe.com/error-low-level)). A re-run or a regenerated model turn emits a fresh call id for the same intended refund, so the call id alone cannot be the key (our inference, following U5). A5's "no duplicate refund" eligibility rule is a business check, not that key. Finally, a timed-out refund is `unknown` until the harness reads the provider's state, and the harness must never retry it with a new key ([fault-tolerance.mdx](https://github.com/langchain-ai/docs/blob/9d9747d0cf241e086c616a16d5ce230ad8f8f31d/src/oss/langgraph/fault-tolerance.mdx)).

**v1 does not need saga machinery.** Without T4, a ticket's writes are triage, notes, an escalation and a reply. The earlier writes remain valid on their own if a later step fails, so a stop should hand off with the live effects listed, not unwind them. A compensation engine built before money moves would have nothing to compensate. Until it has work to do, an ablation could not measure its value, and the repo's rule is that "Each component should be removable so it can be ablated as models improve" ([AGENTS.md](../../../AGENTS.md)).

## A rollback is a new linked entry, and the model must read it

**The harness should record rollback the way ADK records rewind: by appending, never by erasing.** Five changes fit the existing design, and all five are our recommendation.

First, **every write gets an effect identity**. Each side-effecting call should carry an idempotency key scoped as tenant, session, branch and call id, stored with the canonical `inputHash` that D5 already signs ([build-sdk.md, D5](../../plans/build-sdk.md#d5-a-pluggable-signer-with-key-ids-an-ephemeral-default-and-no-unsigned-resume-accepted-with-hd5)). `invokeTool()` already appends the `code_call` entry before it runs the tool, so a resumed run finds the same key, provided the key is derived from that entry ([build-harness.md §3.4](../../plans/build-harness.md#34-permission-approval-and-sandbox-apis)). For money moves, the business key from the saga section joins the call id, because a regenerated turn changes the call id.

Second, **every write result carries an outcome**. `tool_result.details` should carry `outcome: "not_applied" | "applied" | "compensated" | "partial" | "unknown"`, plus the `WriteReceipt.id`, the prior value and an `undoUntil` timestamp for undo-window actions. When a write's call entry exists but its result does not, resume should write `unknown` rather than an aborted error. The workflow then reconciles by reading the helpdesk with the key before anything is re-proposed. The outcome kinds are our proposal from the sibling U5 report ([U5 report](server-rollback.md)), not a vendor's vocabulary. Their basis is distributed-API practice such as client request identifiers ([AWS Builders' Library](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/making-retries-safe-with-idempotent-APIs/)).

Third, **a compensation is its own call plus a link**. An undo runs through `Harness.invokeTool()` like any code-initiated call, so it writes its own `code_call`, `permission_decision` and `tool_result` entries and passes the same policy pipeline ([N40](../../todos/ready/N40-code-initiated-calls.md)). A `product_record` of kind `triage.rollback` then links the two calls. It records `compensates` (the original call id), `compensatedBy` (the undo's call id), the actor (agent, reviewer or operator), the reason, the resulting outcome and a `notReversible` list, such as "reply delivered 14:02". `product_record` data sits outside HD11's stable surface, which suits a shape still being tested ([build-harness.md §3.2](../../plans/build-harness.md#32-session-log-api)). Once the shape settles, it should become a first-class entry type through an ADR.

Fourth, **the model sees a note on the active branch**. A rollback that only the log knows about invites a repeat. So when a person or operator undoes an agent write, the harness should append a short message such as "Priority change to urgent was undone by a reviewer; do not set it again without new information." This message follows the repo's rule that errors say what not to do next ([agent guide §3](../../guides/agent.md#3-tools-deserve-more-design-effort-than-the-prompt-few-workflow-shaped-tools-identity-from-trusted-context-and-errors-that-say-what-to-do-next)).

Fifth, **rewind carries live effects forward**. Before `revert` or `rerunFrom` moves past entries with `applied` outcomes, the harness should list those effects. It then either compensates them, for undo-window actions, or attaches an "effects still live" summary to the new branch. Pi's `branchWithSummary()` gives the shape for that summary (`pi-coding-agent/dist/core/session-manager.d.ts:366`). Here we reverse ADK's compaction rule. ADK keeps rewound content out of the prompt, while we want the facts about the world to survive the rewind, even when the conversation about them does not. Re-proposed calls on the new branch then hit the business key, so the helpdesk boundary, not the model's memory, catches a duplicate reply or refund.

## Two ledgers, and the first fields to add

**Undo for our agent means two ledgers that must never be confused.** The harness may branch the conversation ledger freely, while the effects ledger only moves forward. Every vendor feature studied here lives in the first ledger. Mixing the two costs most exactly when rewind looks most helpful: after a revert or a forgotten turn, the model loses sight of effects that still exist and is more likely to repeat them.

Two questions remain open, and both are testable. First, we do not yet know whether the helpdesk the owner picks at A24 accepts idempotency keys, can delete notes, or can delay a send. If it cannot, the undo window for notes shrinks to correction notes, and the deferred send must live in our own outbox. Second, the capstone lacks a crash test: kill the process between the helpdesk accepting a reply and the result being appended, then check that the resume reconciles to `applied` and sends nothing twice. Until that test passes, "exactly once" in our plans means "once, if nothing crashes".

**The most valuable change to our plans is therefore cheap.** Give each write an identity before it runs and an outcome after it runs, and treat the gap between the two as `unknown` rather than as an error. Most of the rollback design follows from those two fields, and the crash test above is the first place to prove them.
