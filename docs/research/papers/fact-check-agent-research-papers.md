# Fact-check log: AI agent research papers report

The agent-research-papers report holds up, and its two conclusions stand: the foundational agent-architecture lineage needs no new design, and three specific gaps, a skill's runtime control flow, a tool's actual code against its declared contract, and a coding agent's authority over CI/CD, are real and currently uncovered. Of the 151 rows below, 118 are confirmed (✅), 10 are corrected (✏️, split between a design choice with no outside claim to check, a wrong or missing citation, and two overstated paraphrases caught while drafting U23), and 23 rest on inference, on absence, or on a page the fetch tool could only summarize rather than quote verbatim (⚠️). Each of the six research sections was drafted and fact-checked on its own, before the next was started, per this repo's rule that a long report is fact-checked section by section; the front-matter rows (136-149) were checked last, against the assembled report, and rows 150-151 were added during U23, a later pull request that re-read two papers' own text while drafting a build-guide citation.

No correction changes the report's thesis. Two corrections fixed a date in our own triage notes: the Truthgate paper's submission date (row 145; our notes said 16 November 2026, the paper's own page says 17 November) and Wong's governed-ML-experimentation paper's submission date (row 96; our notes said 17 November, the paper's own page says 18 November). One correction fixed a wrong anchor into `browser-use.md` (row 30). One correction, found during this pull request's code review, separated two statistics the report had conflated. Row 145's 21.7% false-pass rate is Truthgate's full 180-task result, and its "cut that rate by more than half" claim came instead from a separate 60-task prompt-ablation subset (22 cheats cut to 8). The report now states both results against their own sample. Two rows record the drafting agents' own design judgment rather than an outside claim: the harness-boundary-versus-fine-tuning framing in the architectures section (row 8) and the plan-then-execute-as-hook framing in the same section (row 18). The front-matter rows added one substantive check that no section had done: the Truthgate paper (arXiv:2611.23335) was cited only from the triage snapshot's one-sentence paraphrase until row 145 fetched its abstract page directly and rewrote the citing sentence with verified numbers. Two more corrections, found while drafting U23, go to the paper's own text rather than a tool summary of it. Row 150 narrows the Agentic Benchmark Checklist's 38% trivial-pass figure to τ-bench's airline subset, 6% on retail, and moves it from the abstract to the paper's body. Row 151 replaces an overstated paraphrase of the halo-effect paper's own hedge, "mostly measure presentation, not capability," with its exact words, "measure presentation as much as capability."

The ⚠️ rows fall into three groups. Fifteen of them, all in the provenance section (rows 91-94, 96-97, 99-101, 103-104 and 107-110), are not absence claims at all: every one of those six papers was fetched from its own arXiv abstract page, but the fetch tool available in that research pass returned a tool-summarized rendering rather than a byte-for-byte quote, so the section marks each external claim ⚠️ (secondary summary) as a tooling limitation, not a finding about the papers. Seven rows are genuine absence or unverified claims, stated plainly rather than invented: no primary source for the AutoGPT/BabyAGI lineage (row 11) or a citation count for the architectures section's chosen survey (row 15), two figures from OpenAI's own SWE-bench Verified posts that returned an HTTP error to every fetch attempt (rows 25 and 26), no comparably direct critique of GAIA or AgentBench as benchmarks (row 29), the deliberate exclusion of a multi-agent-debate paper (row 61), and a SkillCFI overhead figure that was checked but cut from the final section text (row 70). One row is our own synthesis, correctly marked as not attributed to either paper it follows: the multi-agent section's load-spreading conclusion (row 53).

## Link check

Every relative link and anchor in the report, this log, and the research notes resolves. A script extracts each Markdown link target that is not `http(s):` or `mailto:`, resolves it against the file's folder, and matches each `#anchor` against GitHub-style slugs of the target's headings. On 2026-11-19 it checked 51 links in the report (35 distinct targets, 5 of them with anchors), with no failures; it was run again against every file in [notes/](notes/) individually, also with no failures. External links were fetched during each section's own fact-check pass and returned their pages, with two exceptions already covered above: both of OpenAI's blog posts on SWE-bench Verified returned an HTTP error to every attempt, so the report makes those two claims only from a search-result paraphrase, marked `[secondary]`.
