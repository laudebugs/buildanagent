# Practitioners trust checks, sandboxes and meters they can see: what 18 months of Hacker News adds to our agent stack

- **Status:** research synthesis for todo H3, 2026-11-18. The [build-guides plan §4b](../../plans/build-guides.md#4b-practitioner-research-from-hacker-news-round-h-added-2026-11-18) defines the scope; the guides G1b, G2b and G3b weigh this file beside the three G-round syntheses.
- **Corpus (as of November 2026):** 400 Hacker News threads from 2025-05-18 to 2026-11-18, the most-commented of 1,052 stories with at least 50 points that matched 25 agent-related queries, 111,532 comments once flattened ([corpus.md](corpus.md)).
- **Sources:** six theme notes in [`notes/`](notes/): [what-works](notes/what-works.md), [what-fails](notes/what-fails.md), [harnesses-and-sdks](notes/harnesses-and-sdks.md), [docs-and-dx](notes/docs-and-dx.md), [reliability-evals-cost](notes/reliability-evals-cost.md) and [security-and-build-vs-buy](notes/security-and-build-vs-buy.md). Every claim cites a note section or a comment link. Vendor and paper evidence comes from the [SDK](../agent-sdk/agent-sdk-research.md), [harness](../agent-harness/agent-harness-research.md) and [agent](../agent/agent-research.md) syntheses and the [main report](../agent-sdks-and-harness-design.md). Quotes were checked against the raw corpus; the results are in [fact-check.md](fact-check.md).
- **Counts:** a support count is the number of distinct HN usernames whose comments match a note's keyword filter, sometimes corrected by a hand-read on-topic share. It measures how many people raise a view, not how many endorse it, and the notes put the error at about ±30%. Every count below is approximate, and each comes from the note named beside it.

Our three syntheses rest on vendor docs, source code and papers, which show how agents are meant to work; the 111,532 comments in this corpus show how they work for the people who build with them. The two kinds of evidence mostly agree, but practitioners weigh things the documents barely mention: how quota is metered, whether a harness changes behaviour between releases, and whether the agent can switch off its own sandbox. Section 1 ranks the eleven views with the widest support, §2 sets the disputed views and the vendor evidence side by side, §3 traces how views moved over 18 months, §4 applies the evidence to our 49 design decisions, and §5 names the corpus's biases. Practitioners trust what they can check outside the model (executable tests, a sandbox the agent cannot turn off, state kept in files and a cost meter they can watch), and they leave tools that change silently or meter invisibly. That evidence confirms or adjusts all 31 of our 49 design decisions it touches and adds requirements the syntheses lack: progress detection beyond step caps, completion checked in code, path-based ask rules for tests and hooks, and a visible cost and quota meter.

## 1. Eleven views have wide support, led by quota opacity, sandboxed autonomy and plan files

I rank the eleven views below by the support count their notes give, largest first. The counts rank attention, not votes: where a note sampled the matches, only about 40% (fresh context) to 70% (planning) of matching comments endorsed the view rather than mentioning it ([what-works, Method and limits](notes/what-works.md#method-and-limits)). Each item names its main counter-view, because none of the eleven is unanimous.

1. **Quotas are opaque, and opacity drives switching more than quality does (~570 authors across 150 threads).** That is the harnesses note's count of comments about hitting or burning through usage limits; the reliability note's narrower filter finds ~530 across ~150 ([harnesses-and-sdks, Quotas](notes/harnesses-and-sdks.md#quotas-are-the-most-common-complaint-and-they-drive-switching-more-than-quality-does); [reliability-evals-cost, Quota anger](notes/reliability-evals-cost.md#quota-anger-is-mostly-about-invisibility-and-users-want-the-meter-in-the-harness)). The grievance is invisibility: limits change without notice and the meter cannot be read. About 80 authors across 45 threads say they moved to Codex, naming quota, model reliability and TUI quality as the reasons. "We don't know what the limits are… and we cannot monitor our usage towards the limits." — HenriNext, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44721399), 2025-07-29. "I ended up buying the $100 Codex plan. So far it has been much more generous with usage and more accurate than Claude" — chandureddyvari, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47739625), 2026-04-12. Counter-view: about 40 authors in the samples read never hit limits, usually because they cap parallel agents.
2. **Autonomy belongs inside a sandbox, with the approval prompts removed (~440 authors across 159 threads).** Comments mention YOLO or skip-permissions modes, devcontainers, VMs or bubblewrap; ~101 authors across 60 threads describe their own sandbox in the first person, and ~78 across 45 pair a permission-skipping mode with one ([what-works, sandbox](notes/what-works.md#running-without-approval-prompts-inside-a-sandbox-is-the-trusted-setup); [security, YOLO inside a sandbox](notes/security-and-build-vs-buy.md#self-reported-practice-is-yolo-mode-inside-a-sandbox-and-the-threat-model-is-accidents-more-than-attacks)). The reason is approval fatigue (~41 authors across 26 threads), and the stated threat is the agent's own mistakes. "No permission prompts. The agent has free reign and never has to ask permission, but is in a sandbox." — kstenerud, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49197904), 2026-08-06. "any security mechanism which relies on users to read and intelligently respond to approval prompts is doomed to fail over time" — raesene9, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46594168), 2026-01-12. Counter-view: fewer than ten authors see no need for a sandbox, while ~92 across 35 threads raise microVMs or container escapes and argue that containers are too weak.
3. **The plan belongs in a file before any code is written (~400 authors across 159 threads).** About 11 of 16 sampled mentions endorse planning first, and the plan file doubles as the progress log the next session reads ([what-works, plan file](notes/what-works.md#writing-the-plan-to-a-file-before-coding-is-the-most-widely-shared-practice)). "I like that it ends up in the repo as it means it survives compaction or lets me start a fresh session entirely." — simonw, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46492381), 2026-01-04. Counter-view (~15 authors in the planning threads): small fixes need no plan, and specs cannot be complete up front.
4. **Tests, types and linters are the oracle, and they must run outside the model (~380 authors across 161 threads).** About 9 of 16 sampled mentions endorse the practice, and a further ~90 authors across 65 threads advocate deterministic code around the model for anything that must hold ([what-works, oracle](notes/what-works.md#tests-types-and-linters-are-the-oracle-and-agents-do-best-when-they-can-run-them); [reliability-evals-cost, determinism](notes/reliability-evals-cost.md#non-determinism-is-the-sceptics-core-objection-and-the-shared-answer-is-deterministic-checks-around-the-model)). "The agent makes mistakes but it sees the failing tests and fixes it." — rmoriz, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45164260), 2025-09-08. "Any procedure that needs to be perfect should be done by writing deterministic code." — seunosewa, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46023851), 2025-11-23. Counter-view: agents game an oracle the model can edit; ~28 authors across ~22 threads report deleted, skipped or hard-coded tests first-hand ([what-fails, test gaming](notes/what-fails.md#agents-game-tests-rather-than-fix-code-a-mid-2025-complaint-that-faded)).
5. **Small, reviewable tasks work, and large one-shot changes do not (~260 authors across 128 threads).** About 20 of 36 sampled mentions endorse it, and task size is set by what a human can review or one context window holds ([what-works, small tasks](notes/what-works.md#small-scoped-tasks-work-and-large-one-shot-changes-do-not)). "The trick is to break things into small enough pieces that you can review them." — koolba, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50530221), 2026-11-17. Counter-view: a few authors find very small changes easier to make than to describe.
6. **Fresh context beats long sessions (~255 authors across 137 threads).** Comments mention fresh sessions, clearing context or context rot, and ~90 authors across ~75 threads describe agents degrading as the context fills ([what-works, fresh context](notes/what-works.md#fresh-context-beats-long-sessions-and-state-should-live-in-files); [what-fails, context rot](notes/what-fails.md#context-rot-is-a-named-accepted-failure-and-the-workaround-is-fresh-contexts-not-bigger-ones)). On 1M-token models, ~45 authors report a quality threshold around 200–300k tokens ([reliability-evals-cost, large windows](notes/reliability-evals-cost.md#large-context-windows-degrade-long-before-they-fill-so-experienced-users-cap-them)). "Reset the context as often as you can. LLMs like short contexts" — stavros, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45741747), 2025-10-29. "results get much worse way earlier than when you reach 1M tokens… force a compaction at eg 300k tokens." — danmaz74, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47741559), 2026-04-12. Counter-view: about ten authors, mostly in late 2026, say large windows now hold up.
7. **Review is the binding constraint on agent output (~190 authors across ~115 threads).** Commenters describe reading every line, steering constantly or "babysitting" ([what-fails, review burden](notes/what-fails.md#review-burden-and-babysitting-are-the-most-widely-shared-cost-of-using-agents)). "There literally aren't enough eyeballs to go around reviewing code when everyone is 2-3xing their output." — hysan, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48799587), 2026-07-06. "'Agentic coding works' and 'ship without review' are two different claims." — varshith17, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46716080), 2026-01-22. Counter-view: a smaller group says review is faster than writing, or that a second agent can do part of it.
8. **A thin loop you can read beats a framework (~185 authors across 101 threads).** These authors discuss agent SDKs, frameworks or building their own loop, and the note reads a clear preference for thin libraries; LangChain draws ~46 authors across 34 threads, almost all negative, and ~72 authors across 54 threads say they wrote or run their own harness ([harnesses-and-sdks, frameworks](notes/harnesses-and-sdks.md#agent-frameworks-are-seen-as-obscuring-what-is-sent-to-the-model-and-langchain-is-the-cautionary-example); [security, small loop](notes/security-and-build-vs-buy.md#a-working-agent-loop-is-small-and-practitioners-prove-it-repeatedly)). "Obscuring core logic is the most egregious part of most agent frameworks." — kristjansson, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48478800), 2026-06-10. "We (the Princeton SWE-bench team) built an agent in ~100 lines of code that does pretty well on SWE-bench" — ofirpress, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45001234), 2025-08-24. Counter-views: a framework's accepted value is tracing and observability, and ~25 authors say the small loop is a teaching artefact whose missing 10% (error handling, timeouts, context management, UI) is the real work.
9. **Destructive actions are a permissions failure, not a model failure (~160 authors in three incident threads).** In the Replit, Terraform and production-database threads, that many blame credentials, permissions and backups rather than the agent; the filter overcounts a little, and only about 15 authors report a destructive action first-hand ([what-fails, destructive actions](notes/what-fails.md#destructive-actions-are-rare-first-hand-and-the-consensus-blames-the-operators-permissions)). "The safeguards need to be outside LLM and they need to be deterministic." — throw5, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47569289), 2026-03-30. "you can't blame ai if your production token is on the same machine as the staging/ development environment" — tabs_or_spaces, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47918604), 2026-04-27. Counter-view: about 20 authors in the largest thread also fault the vendors for unconfirmed delete endpoints and co-located backups.
10. **Prompt injection cannot be fixed inside the model, so the answer is to cut a leg of the lethal trifecta (~49 authors across 24 threads, plus ~31 across 25).** The first group calls injection unsolved or fundamental, the second uses the trifecta framing by name, and nobody in the samples read claims a working in-model defence ([security, injection](notes/security-and-build-vs-buy.md#prompt-injection-is-treated-as-unsolved-and-the-accepted-answer-is-architectural-not-a-filter)). "Prompt injection isn't like SQL injection, it has no technical solution (that isn't AGI-complete)." — TeMPOraL, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47540932), 2026-03-27. "The solution is to cut off one of the legs of the lethal trifecta. The leg that makes the most sense is the ability to exfiltrate data" — simonw, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46594053), 2026-01-12. Counter-view: removing a leg can remove the product, and residual channels (DNS, the reply itself, public issues) remain ([security, exfiltration](notes/security-and-build-vs-buy.md#cutting-the-exfiltration-leg-is-the-most-cited-mitigation-and-its-cost-to-usefulness-is-the-main-objection)).
11. **Silent changes and hidden output break trust (~60 authors across seven threads, plus ~45 across 15).** The docs note read about 60 authors objecting to defaults, prompts and visible output that change between releases without notice, and about 45 asking to see what the agent reads and does ([docs-and-dx, silent changes](notes/docs-and-dx.md#silent-changes-to-harness-behaviour-are-the-top-dx-complaint); [docs-and-dx, visibility](notes/docs-and-dx.md#practitioners-want-to-see-what-the-agent-is-doing-and-treat-hiding-it-as-a-regression)). In seven quality threads, ~290 authors use degradation words such as "worse" or "nerfed". Whether the models degraded is disputed, but both sides agree that users cannot tell a harness change from a model change ([harnesses-and-sdks, degradation](notes/harnesses-and-sdks.md#suspected-silent-degradation-eroded-trust-in-claude-code-more-than-any-single-bug)). "There should never be an undocumented change that reduces quality." — bshanks, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47895898), 2026-04-24. "The default view hiding files read is fully a regression imho. It is so helpful for sense of control" — patcon, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46983864), 2026-02-12. Counter-view: about 8 authors call the outrage a normal feedback loop, and about 7 say only the artifact matters, not the stream.

Eight of the eleven views put the check outside the model, in a meter, a sandbox, a file, a test, a permission boundary or a version stamp. The other three (small tasks, review and a thin loop) keep the human able to see what the agent did.

## 2. Practitioners split on seven practices, and mostly confirm the vendor and paper evidence

### 2.1 Seven practices divide practitioners

Seven practices draw both wide use and organised dissent, so a guide should present each as a configurable choice with its evidence, not as settled.

1. **Context files.** AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md are the most discussed artifact (~850 authors across 197 threads), but the endorsed practice is narrow: adding a line after each mistake (~60 authors) and keeping the file short (~35). About 25 authors in the "Evaluating AGENTS.md" thread split over a study ([arXiv 2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988)) finding that context files "do not generally improve task success rates" while raising inference cost by over 20% ([what-works, context files](notes/what-works.md#short-correction-driven-context-files-help-long-or-generated-ones-are-doubted)).
2. **MCP versus CLIs and skills.** About 35 authors across 18 threads prefer CLIs to MCP outright, skills draw ~215 positive authors, and the noisy filters find roughly even numbers raising MCP's context cost and describing its uses (~400 authors each side, 91 and 74 threads). The critics are coding-agent users; the defenders build remote, authenticated integrations for others ([what-works, CLIs and skills](notes/what-works.md#clis-and-skills-beat-mcp-for-local-coding-work-mcp-keeps-a-role-for-third-party-and-team-services); [harnesses-and-sdks, MCP](notes/harnesses-and-sdks.md#mcp-is-judged-too-costly-in-context-for-coding-agents-but-still-useful-for-remote-authenticated-integrations)).
3. **Subagents.** Mentions are the second-highest count in the what-works note (~590 authors across 201 threads) and sentiment is mixed: context isolation for search and review is endorsed, while ~30 authors report handoffs failing on coupled work and quota burning fast ([what-works, subagents](notes/what-works.md#subagents-work-for-isolating-noisy-exploration-not-for-splitting-coupled-work)).
4. **Whether the harness matters.** Explicit statements are rare: ~30 authors say it matters a lot and ~45 that harnesses are interchangeable, and the two camps mostly mean the same thing, prompt overhead and tool reliability ([harnesses-and-sdks, harness importance](notes/harnesses-and-sdks.md#whether-the-harness-matters-is-contested-with-a-slight-lean-towards-it-matters-less-each-model-generation)).
5. **Compaction.** About 45 authors across ~35 threads say compaction loses the plan or should be avoided; about 10 positive claims appear, clustered after May 2026 ([reliability-evals-cost, compaction](notes/reliability-evals-cost.md#compaction-was-distrusted-until-mid-2026-and-users-want-to-steer-and-inspect-it)).
6. **Classifier approval ("auto mode").** About 145 authors across 30 threads discuss Claude Code's auto mode, almost all after it became the default: for some accounts by August 2026, and for interactive sessions "on every plan and provider" in v2.1.284 of 2026-09-28 ([changelog](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)); it is widely used and widely distrusted, and users ask for a middle ground between per-command approval and YOLO mode backed by a hard sandbox ([security, permission prompts](notes/security-and-build-vs-buy.md#permission-prompts-fail-through-fatigue-and-auto-mode-is-adopted-without-being-trusted)).
7. **Agents reviewing agents.** Review by a second agent is mentioned by ~225 authors across 128 threads, and cross-model review roughly tripled per comment by late 2026, but ~20 authors report false positives and warn that two models agreeing proves nothing ([what-works, review](notes/what-works.md#review-is-non-negotiable-but-who-reviews-shifted-from-the-human-to-a-second-agent)).

## 3. Over 18 months, views moved from supervising a junior to containing an autonomous agent

The notes cut the period at slightly different dates (for example, what-works ends its first period in December 2025, what-fails in November), and the per-period rates move with single large threads, so the shifts below give direction, not size.

**Mid-2025: supervise a fast, unreliable junior.** Junior or intern analogies ran at 50 authors per 10,000 comments, against 27 in early 2026 and 8 in late 2026 ([what-works, shifts](notes/what-works.md#shifts-over-time)). The signature complaints were model errors: hallucinated APIs and test gaming each ran at 0.8 authors per 1,000 comments, falling to 0.1 by late 2026 ([what-fails, shifts](notes/what-fails.md#shifts-over-time)). Users moved from Cursor and Copilot to Claude Code because its loop did the planning they had done by hand ([harnesses-and-sdks, Claude Code](notes/harnesses-and-sdks.md#claude-code-displaced-ide-agents-in-2025-because-its-loop-did-the-work-users-had-been-doing-by-hand)). MCP discussion peaked at 60.7 mentions per 1,000 comments, and its security debate treated the protocol as the threat after the GitHub and Supabase exploits ([security, shifts](notes/security-and-build-vs-buy.md#shifts-over-time)).

**Early 2026: plan, then run against gates, and stop trusting the vendor.** About 70 authors date a step change in coding agents to Opus 4.5 in late 2025 ([what-fails, shifts](notes/what-fails.md#shifts-over-time)), and the planning-and-execution thread of February 2026 shows multi-hour runs from a reviewed plan becoming normal. In the same five months the third-party subscription ban, the source leak, quota exhaustion and the degradation debate landed; degradation complaints rose from 1.8 to 6.0 authors per 1,000 comments, switching to Codex peaked, and OpenCode mentions tripled ([harnesses-and-sdks, shifts](notes/harnesses-and-sdks.md#shifts-over-time)). Vendor default changes in March and April 2026 turned prompt caching and reasoning effort into cost levers users reason about, where in mid-2025 both were barely discussed ([reliability-evals-cost, shifts](notes/reliability-evals-cost.md#shifts-over-time)).

**Late 2026: agents review agents inside a sandbox, and the human reviews interfaces.** Sandbox mentions rose from 37 to 52 authors per 10,000 comments, cross-model review from 2.3 to 7.2, and explicit line-by-line human review fell from 6.9 to 3.6 ([what-works, shifts](notes/what-works.md#shifts-over-time)). Talk of microVMs and container escapes rose about ninefold, credential-injecting proxies appeared, and auto mode took over the permission debate ([security, shifts](notes/security-and-build-vs-buy.md#shifts-over-time)). The November 2026 setup census shows a portfolio rather than a winner: by keyword, 134 replies mention Claude Code, 125 Codex, 89 OpenCode and 75 Pi ([harnesses-and-sdks, shifts](notes/harnesses-and-sdks.md#shifts-over-time)). The open question by November is review capacity, the subject of one of the corpus's three final-day threads ([50538303](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50538303), 2026-11-17).

Three things held constant across all three periods: plans in files, tests as the oracle and small reviewable units ([what-works, shifts](notes/what-works.md#shifts-over-time)), and the view that injection must be contained outside the model ([security, shifts](notes/security-and-build-vs-buy.md#shifts-over-time)). What changed is how much autonomy users grant and how hard they wall it in, which is why the late-2026 evidence weighs most for §4.

## 5. The corpus over-represents senior engineers using coding agents, and its counts are mentions

Five biases limit what this synthesis can claim, and each note states them in its Method and limits section.

- **Population.** HN skews toward coding agents, senior engineers, individual subscribers rather than enterprise buyers, terminal users and Claude Code in particular; "Claude Code" was itself a corpus query ([harnesses-and-sdks, biases](notes/harnesses-and-sdks.md#method-and-limits)). Nothing here speaks for customer-support or business-process agents, and products without launch threads (OpenAI Agents SDK, Vercel AI SDK) are too rarely discussed to characterise.
- **Selection and pile-ons.** The corpus keeps stories with at least 50 points and the 400 most-commented, which favours controversy: quota, ban, degradation and incident threads are among the largest. Incident threads attract dunking, which inflates operator-blame counts, and complaint threads outweigh quiet satisfaction ([what-fails, biases](notes/what-fails.md#method-and-limits)).
- **Counting method.** Support counts come from regular expressions over comment text. They catch neutral mentions, questions and idioms and miss paraphrase, so they measure attention, not agreement, with an error the notes put at about ±30%. Vocal heavy users appear in many threads, though each author counts once per claim.
- **Self-reports.** Every comment is a self-report. None measures outcomes, and the notes treat felt productivity as weak evidence ([reliability-evals-cost, productivity](notes/reliability-evals-cost.md#felt-productivity-is-not-trusted-as-evidence-but-nobody-offers-a-better-measure)). Vendor employees and founders of competing tools comment too, and the notes count them like anyone else.
- **Unchecked names and secondary claims.** Model and product names after mid-2026 (Opus 5.5, Fable, gpt-6.1-sol, OpenClaw) are quoted as commenters wrote them and were not checked. Several figures came from commenters' summaries. Re-verification R1 (2026-11-18) checked the AGENTS.md study, the auto-mode default date and the extension incident against primary sources ([fact-check](fact-check.md#re-verification-against-primary-sources-r1)). OpenAI's SWE-bench statement (openai.com returned 403) and the details of the Railway incident (the original post is an X article that does not render without JavaScript) remain secondhand.

These limits shape how §4 should be used: the HN evidence is strongest where a view holds in every period (views 3, 4, 5 and 10) and weakest where a single thread dominates a count (destructive actions, sycophancy, the November census).

## Conclusion: build what practitioners can check, and carry §4 into the guides

Eighteen months of Hacker News threads show practitioners converging on one stance: trust nothing the model asserts about itself, and trust any check, boundary or meter they can see working outside it. That stance agrees with the vendor and paper evidence on ten of fifteen claims in §2.2, qualifies three, and rejects two vendor defaults that our syntheses had already declined. Its largest additions to our stack concern the loop and its gates: progress detection beyond the step cap, completion checked in code, and path-based ask rules for tests, hooks and CI config. The rest concern trust: a sandbox the agent cannot widen as the default for unattended runs, a cost and quota meter on every session, and version stamps that cover defaults, prompts and effort. The next step is for G1b, G2b and G3b to take the rows of §4.1, §4.2 and §4.3 into their reference architectures, mark each HN-derived requirement with its view number and support count, and carry the adjusted open questions (harness questions 3 and 9, agent question 7) into Round P.
