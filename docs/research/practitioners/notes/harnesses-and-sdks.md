# Harnesses and SDKs: what practitioners praise, criticise and switch between (HN, 2025-05-18 to 2026-11-18)

*Round H theme note 3 of 6 ([plan §4b](../../../plans/build-guides.md)). Source: the 400-thread corpus in [corpus.md](../corpus.md), about 111,600 comments once flattened. Quotes are short attributed excerpts. Counts are distinct HN usernames matched by keyword filters, so they are approximate upper bounds on how many people hold a view, not tallies of agreement (see "Method and limits"). Model and product names such as "Fable", "Sol", "Opus 5.5" or "OpenClaw" are quoted as commenters wrote them and are not checked here.*

## Summary

Hacker News commenters treat coding-agent harnesses as interchangeable shells around a model they rent, and they switch whenever the model, the quota or the vendor's conduct changes. Claude Code dominates the discussion (~4,100 authors across 356 threads mention it, partly because "Claude Code" was a corpus query), and in mid-2025 it pulled users away from Cursor, Copilot, Aider and Gemini CLI, mainly because its agent loop beat IDE-centred tools. From early 2026 the praise turned into three complaints: quotas that run out within hours (~570 authors across 150 threads mention hitting limits), suspected silent quality changes (~290 authors in seven degradation threads), and the ban on using subscriptions from third-party harnesses (seven threads, roughly evenly split between defenders and critics). Those complaints drove an explicit migration to Codex (~80 authors in 45 threads say they moved) and a growth of open, model-agnostic harnesses: OpenCode and Pi, run on cheap open-weight plans. By November 2026 an Ask HN census shows Claude Code and Codex used side by side, often with one reviewing the other, and OpenCode and Pi close behind. Agent frameworks barely register: LangChain draws ~46 authors in 34 threads, almost all dismissive, and builders prefer a thin loop they can see through. The note's main conclusion: practitioners value a harness that is small, transparent about what it sends and what it costs, stable between releases and free to use with any model, and they leave a harness that changes silently or ties them to one vendor's quota, whatever the quality of the model behind it.

## Claude Code displaced IDE agents in 2025 because its loop did the work users had been doing by hand

Support: ~110 authors across 69 threads describe moving to Claude Code or ranking it above an alternative, most of them in 2025 and most from Cursor or Copilot. Mentions of Cursor fall from 23.8 per 1,000 comments in mid-2025 to 6.5 in the first half of 2026; Copilot falls from 16.5 to 3.3 by late 2026 (per-period rates over all comments). Users credit the agent loop, the planning and the tools, not the terminal as such: several say they had been reproducing Claude Code's behaviour manually with rules files and to-do lists.

- "basically me doing by hand what Claude Code does internally." — adastra22, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44773871), 2025-08-03
- "when I switched from Cursor to Claude it took me less than 24 hours to realise I wouldn't go back." — solumunus, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45788563), 2025-11-02
- "Cursor is designed to ruthlessly prune context to curtail costs." — laborcontract, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45578014), 2025-10-14
- "I just can't really justifying paying Cursor to middleman my requests to LLM providers when Claude Pro is $20 / month." — epiccoleman, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44563991), 2025-07-14

Counter-views: a minority keeps an IDE tool for completion or prefers it outright. "Cursor has the best tab completion model. I use it together with Claude Code" (nikcub, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44436696), 2025-07-01), and one Copilot user found Claude Code "very lazy" by comparison ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44998675), 2025-08-23). The economic argument (a subsidised first-party plan beats a reseller paying retail token prices) is as common as the quality argument, so the migration measures pricing as much as harness design.

## Quotas are the most common complaint, and they drive switching more than quality does

Support: ~570 authors across 150 threads mention hitting or burning through usage limits; "subsidised" appears from ~280 authors in 110 threads. The density peaks in the first half of 2026 (the "Pro Max 5x quota exhausted in 1.5 hours" thread, [47739260](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47739260), drew 660 comments; "Claude Code to be removed from Anthropic's Pro plan?", [47854477](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47854477), drew 642). The recurring grievance is opacity: limits that change without notice, and a meter users cannot predict.

- "I regularly hit the the Pro limits 3 times a day using sonnet." — blitzar, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44715471), 2025-07-28
- "I hit the rate limit in about ~15 mins on Claude. But it will take me a few hours with Codex." — choilive, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47074501), 2026-02-19
- "I ended up buying the $100 Codex plan. So far it has been much more generous with usage and more accurate than Claude" — chandureddyvari, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47739625), 2026-04-12

Counter-views (~40 authors in the samples read): heavy users who manage context and limit parallel agents report never hitting limits. "I never hit limits since realizing that trying to have too many agents running at once just wasn't effective" (yakkomajuri, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50378756), 2026-11-05); "I've never hit the limits myself" (adefa, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44036474), 2025-05-20). Others defend the limits as the price of a subsidy. The "Codex is more generous" view held for most of the period but reversed for some users in late 2026: "OpenAI plans gave a lot more usage than Anthropic... Now for the first time that's changed" (deaux, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49764490), 2026-09-19).

## Suspected silent degradation eroded trust in Claude Code more than any single bug

Support: in seven threads on quality ([46978710](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46978710), [46810282](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46810282), [47660925](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47660925), [47736476](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47736476), [47739260](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47739260), [47878905](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47878905), [49401549](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49401549)), ~290 authors use words such as "worse", "nerfed", "lazy" or "degraded"; corpus-wide, ~420 authors in 150 threads use degradation vocabulary, though that wider count is noisy. The April 2026 thread links an Anthropic postmortem ([47878905](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47878905)), and several commenters there separate harness changes (context handling, thinking display, cache lifetime) from the model itself. This note did not read the postmortem and records only what commenters say it shows.

- "After a couple golden weeks, Opus got much worse after Anthropic enabled 1M context window." — jfvinueza, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47664098), 2026-04-06
- "I don't want to pay for Opus if I can't trust what it says." — matheusmoreira, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47664083), 2026-04-06
- "None of these problems equate to degrading model performance. Completely different team. Degraded CC harness, sure." — sroussey, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47879094), 2026-04-23

Counter-views (~50 authors in the same threads): the reports are anecdote and frequency bias. "Isn't the more economical explanation that these models were never as impressive as you first thought" (efficax, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47664311), 2026-04-06); "frequency bias is a thing" (solenoid0937, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48253083), 2026-05-24). Both sides agree that users cannot tell harness changes from model changes, and that this ambiguity is itself the problem: one commenter's benchmark thread ([46810282](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46810282)) exists because users had no way to check.

## The subscription ban split HN on fairness but pushed many users towards model-agnostic harnesses

Support: seven threads on third-party use and related billing ([46549823](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46549823), [46578701](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46578701), [47069299](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47069299), [47633396](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47633396), [47952722](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47952722), [47963204](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47963204), [48734373](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48734373)) total over 3,500 comments. Crude keyword splits find ~200 authors using defence vocabulary (subsidy, "their terms", "fair enough") and ~220 using criticism vocabulary (lock-in, rug pull, cancel); a reading of samples confirms a genuine split, not a consensus. The ban also confused builders of their own agents on whether the Claude Agent SDK may use subscription credentials.

- "there's a reason people use this tool instead of your first party one." — satvikpendem, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46551234), 2026-01-09
- "The people mad about this feel they are entitled to the heavily subsidized usage in any context they want" — anonym29, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47075024), 2026-02-19
- "the Claude Agent SDK will use subscription/oauth credentials if present." — firloop, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47069621), 2026-02-19
- "I'm building a system around the Claude Agent SDK. Am I allowed to use it or not?" — qwertox, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47072460), 2026-02-19

The behavioural effect is clearer than the moral verdict. Commenters report cancelling and moving to OpenCode Zen, GLM, Kimi or Ollama Cloud, and some note that OpenAI permits its subscription in other harnesses (a claim by dannyw, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49240804), 2026-08-10, not checked here). Mentions of open-weight models and their coding plans (GLM, Kimi, DeepSeek, Qwen, MiniMax, OpenCode Go) rise from 8.9 to 24.2 per 1,000 comments between mid-2025 and early 2026 and stay near 20 in late 2026 (~1,350 authors, 239 threads).

## Users who left Claude Code mostly went to Codex, and many now run both

Support: ~80 authors across 45 threads say they moved to Codex or prefer it, most in the first half of 2026; ~32 authors across 27 threads describe a split workflow in which Claude writes and Codex reviews. The reasons given are quota, model reliability and Claude Code's TUI quality. Codex's own criticisms (~70 authors, 49 threads) are slowness, over-engineered solutions and weaker UX, so the two are seen as complementary rather than ranked.

- "We were heavy users of Claude Code ($70K+ spend per year) and have almost completely switched to codex CLI." — mmaunder, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45648422), 2025-10-20
- "I switched to Codex mid-January in part because of the dubious quality of the tui itself and the unreliability of the model." — cmrdporcupine, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47882233), 2026-04-23
- "get Codex to review the code and it will find 2-5 actual correctness bugs." — saberience, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48773472), 2026-07-03
- "Claude Code is much better than Codex CLI, but GPT 5 Codex is much better than Sonnet 4.5. I wish I could use one with the other" — stavros, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45737925), 2025-10-28

Counter-views: some found Codex unreliable ("it seems to go off the rails every time", paustint, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48144814), 2026-05-15), and late in the period some returned to Claude Code for newer Anthropic models ("I've come full circle and am back on Claude Code", jpardoe, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50392572), 2026-11-06). The stavros quote states the theme's core tension: users want to pair the best harness with the best model, and vendors bundle them.

## Claude Code's harness quality draws steady criticism, and the source leak reinforced "no secret sauce"

Support: ~155 authors across 70 threads complain about flicker, CPU and memory use, regressions or bloat; the January 2026 flicker thread ([46699072](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46699072)) and the March 2026 source leak ([47584540](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47584540), 1,022 comments) concentrate them. A second cluster (~260 authors across seven threads from mid to late 2026, such as [49498201](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49498201), [50385272](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50385272), [50407882](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50407882) and the Codex daemon thread [50060694](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50060694)) objects to defaults: data or attribution leaving the machine, and background processes installed without asking.

- "it's been like 9 months and still every day it has this behavior" — johnfn, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46523014), 2026-01-07
- "Claude Code has a lot of stupid regressions on a regular basis" — causal, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47463558), 2026-03-21
- "There is no secret sauce in the claude cli, and the agent harness itself is no better (worse IMO) than the others." — loveparade, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47586106), 2026-03-31
- "Anthropic has massively failed at developing a trustworthy product." — fg137, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50412993), 2026-11-08

Counter-views: many daily users report no such problems ("Never noticed any flickering either", cat-whisperer, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46551116), 2026-01-09), some open-harness users moved back because of their own bugs ([khimaros](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46361349), 2025-12-23), and some prefer the vendor pairing: "If you use Opus + Claude Code... you are using it the way it was designed to be used" (TZubiri, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47463497), 2026-03-21). Defaults divide opinion too: one user wants session links in commits by default ([klodolph](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49498447), 2026-08-30).

## Open harnesses won converts for their small prompts, visible internals and model freedom

Support: OpenCode is mentioned by ~880 authors across 190 threads, with mentions tripling between mid-2025 and early 2026 (4.8 to 15.7 per 1,000 comments). Pi is mentioned in a harness context by roughly 390 authors across 106 threads, almost all from 2026 (heuristic filter, see limits); ~64 authors across 37 threads praise its minimalism or extensibility specifically. The July 2026 token-overhead thread ([48883275](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883275), "Claude Code sends 33k tokens before reading the prompt; OpenCode sends 7k") made system-prompt size a public comparison point.

- "pi agent runs rings around claude code. Never eats tons of CPU on big outputs, no flickering" — caspar, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47082479), 2026-02-20
- "The most important feature of Pi is that it is small, and has small system prompt, making it great for locall LLMs." — krzyk, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48168133), 2026-05-17
- "Pi stands in that sweet spot between being very minimal while still offering a catalog of pluggable functionality" — drob518, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48885653), 2026-07-12
- "pi sends 1k (or less)" — drtournier, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883549), 2026-07-12

Counter-views: OpenCode draws its own complaints (~78 authors, 44 threads) about memory leaks, resource use and telemetry; the telemetry claim ([anonym29](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47461554), 2026-03-20) was disputed in the same thread ([flexagoon](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47461718)). Pi's minimalism is a cost for some: "I'd just rather use an harness that's a bit more full featured from the start" (mft_, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883965), 2026-07-12); "I prefer a nice, complete out-of-the-box experience and that's what OpenCode provides" (eloisant, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48011714), 2026-05-04). The token-overhead study itself was criticised for its method ([MallocVoidstar](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883520)), and one commenter argued a larger prompt is a deliberate quality trade-off ([slopinthebag](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883529)).

## Whether the harness matters is contested, with a slight lean towards "it matters less each model generation"

Support: explicit statements are rare (~30 authors say the harness matters a lot, ~45 that harnesses are interchangeable or trivial), but the switching behaviour above implies the second view. The "Claude Code in 200 lines" thread ([46545620](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46545620)) split the same way: the core loop is simple, but reliability features (early-stopping guards, schema checks, compaction) are where the work is.

- "The harness matters A LOT. The model is the engine, the harness is the driver and chassis." — theshrike79, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47611131), 2026-04-02
- "Same model, opus, works better in 3P harnesses such as Factory Droid or Amp." — ahmadyan, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48003920), 2026-05-04
- "The smarter the models get the less the harnesses matter (outside of devx)." — parhamn, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48165419), 2026-05-17
- "models are not as easily hot swappable as I thought." — whalee, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46857171), 2026-02-02

The two camps agree more than they seem to: those who say the harness matters mostly mean prompt and context overhead and tool reliability, which are the very things the minimalists strip down. Whalee's point is the counterweight: models are tuned to their vendor's harness, so swapping costs some quality.

## Agent frameworks are seen as obscuring what is sent to the model, and LangChain is the cautionary example

Support: LangChain or LangGraph appear in only ~46 authors' comments across 34 threads, almost all negative or historical; ~185 authors across 101 threads discuss agent SDKs, frameworks or building their own loop, with a clear preference for thin libraries or no framework. The Apache Burr thread ([48477400](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48477400)) and Cursor's "The Agent SDK" thread ([50273731](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50273731)) carry the most considered discussion. Where frameworks are defended, it is for observability, deployment and deterministic orchestration, not for writing the loop.

- "It seemed to force code into their way of doing things and was deeply opinionated about things that didn't matter like prompt templating." — space_fountain, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47591895), 2026-03-31
- "Obscuring core logic is the most egregious part of most agent frameworks." — kristjansson, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48478800), 2026-06-10
- "The advantage of frameworks isn't that they make it easier to write the actual agent, it's tooling + observability" — fxwin, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48480273), 2026-06-10
- "If you are writing a workflow, use a workflow engine (Airflow, Temporal, etc)" — lukebuehler, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48487728), 2026-06-11

Counter-views: LangGraph is defended for reproducible multi-step data flows ([ossa-ma](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47592630), 2026-03-31), and Pi itself is called "the best agent framework" ([CuriouslyC](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48478387), 2026-06-10), which shows HN does not reject reusable cores, only opaque ones. Commenters also note that framework-era architecture aged badly as models improved ([storus](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47247012), 2026-03-04) and ask for a vendor-neutral standard rather than one more vendor SDK ([pscanf](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50274022), 2026-10-28). The OpenAI Agents SDK (~16 authors) and Vercel AI SDK (~11 authors) are too rarely discussed to characterise; this is a sampling gap, not evidence of indifference.

## Context files are expected to be portable across harnesses

Support: ~100 authors across 32 threads discuss AGENTS.md versus CLAUDE.md, symlinks or harness-specific files, rising in late 2026 around [49367350](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49367350), [49760187](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49760187) and [49814947](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49814947). Users who run several harnesses treat a single shared instruction file as table stakes and resent workarounds; "symlink CLAUDE.md to AGENTS.md" is the standard advice. The topic is covered in more depth by the what-works note; here it matters as one more cost of switching harnesses that users want removed.

## Shifts over time

Mid-2025 (May to December): launch threads for Copilot's coding agent, Jules, Gemini CLI, Kiro and Codex CLI compete for attention, and the dominant story is migration from IDE agents to Claude Code. MCP is at its peak of discussion (60.7 mentions per 1,000 comments) and is mostly debated as a plugin standard. Quota complaints start with the July 2025 weekly limits ([44713757](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44713757)), but praise for Claude Code outweighs them; a few heavy users already report moving to Codex for long, hard tasks ([mmaunder](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45648422)).

Early 2026 (January to June): the period of grievance. The third-party subscription ban (January and February), the flicker and regression threads, the source leak (March), quota exhaustion and the degradation debate (February to April) all land within five months. Switching statements to Codex peak here (54 of the 85 matched comments), as do open-harness mentions (OpenCode at 15.7 per 1,000 comments) and open-weight coding plans (24.2 per 1,000). Pi enters the discussion after Mario Zechner's post ([46844822](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46844822), February 2026).

Late 2026 (July to November): the tone shifts from anger to portfolio management. Claude Code's share of mentions falls (87.7 per 1,000 in mid-2025, 40.5 in late 2026), Codex holds steady at about 22, and Pi's mentions keep growing. The Ask HN census of November 2026 ([50366117](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366117), 411 top-level replies) shows the plural setup: by keyword, 134 replies mention Claude Code, 125 Codex, 89 OpenCode, 75 Pi, 180 open-weight models or plans, 53 local models, 28 Cursor, 24 Copilot and 24 a harness of their own. Complaints move from quotas to defaults and trust (telemetry, attribution, background daemons, data leaving the machine), and some users report returning to Claude for newer models while OpenAI quotas tightened ([deaux](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49764490)).

## Design implications for our SDK, harness and agent

1. **Stay model-agnostic, and let a session use different models per role.** Users switch models monthly and pair one model's writing with another's review. This supports the provider-neutral transcript and explicit provider factory of [SDK decisions 1, 2 and 14](../../agent-sdk/agent-sdk-research.md#6-fourteen-design-decisions-follow-from-the-evidence), and argues for a first-class "review by a second model" subagent, logged as its own linked session per [harness decision 1](../../agent-harness/agent-harness-research.md#6-eighteen-design-decisions-follow-from-the-evidence).
2. **Keep the default system prompt and tool set small, and report preamble size.** Pi's popularity and the 33k-versus-7k comparison show that users measure prompt overhead and blame it for both cost and quality. [Harness decision 18](../../agent-harness/agent-harness-research.md#6-eighteen-design-decisions-follow-from-the-evidence) already reports preamble size per eval task; we should also print it at session start, and [agent decision 7](../../agent/agent-research.md#6-seventeen-design-decisions-for-our-agent) (short, frozen prompt) should hold for the harness's own prompt.
3. **Make cost and quota visible before they run out.** The most common complaint is an unpredictable meter. [SDK decision 5](../../agent-sdk/agent-sdk-research.md#6-fourteen-design-decisions-follow-from-the-evidence) (usage and cost on every message) and decision 7 (USD cap with a stop reason) cover the mechanism; the harness should add a running total and a warning threshold to the UI.
4. **Never change behaviour silently: stamp versions on every session.** Degradation threads show users cannot tell harness changes from model changes. Recording harness version, prompt hash, tool schemas and model id in the first log entry ([agent decision 11](../../agent/agent-research.md#6-seventeen-design-decisions-for-our-agent)) and following the written versioning policy of [SDK decision 13](../../agent-sdk/agent-sdk-research.md#6-fourteen-design-decisions-follow-from-the-evidence) lets a user diff two sessions and answer "what changed?" themselves.
5. **Show exactly what is sent to the model.** The case against frameworks is opacity. Keep the `onPayload` hook of SDK decision 2 and add a way to dump the full request for any step; this is the feature HN asks frameworks for, alongside observability ([harness decision 18](../../agent-harness/agent-harness-research.md#6-eighteen-design-decisions-follow-from-the-evidence)).
6. **Ship a minimal core with extensions, and keep MCP removable.** Practitioners praise Pi's extension model and doubt MCP's context cost. This supports [harness decision 17](../../agent-harness/agent-harness-research.md#6-eighteen-design-decisions-follow-from-the-evidence) (three extension tiers, MCP as a removable built-in) and suggests lazy loading of MCP tool schemas so an unused server costs no context.
7. **Default to off for anything that leaves the machine.** Late-2026 complaints concern telemetry, attribution and background processes. Harness decision 18 already sets content off and no default remote exporter; the same rule should cover auto-update daemons and anything written into commits or pull requests.
8. **Read AGENTS.md natively.** Portability of context files is expected; our harness should load AGENTS.md without a symlink and treat any harness-specific file as an overlay.
9. **Do not build on consumer-subscription credentials.** The ban and the Agent SDK confusion show that harnesses tied to one vendor's subscription terms can break overnight. Our gateway should take API keys or a proxy-injected credential ([harness decision 15](../../agent-harness/agent-harness-research.md#6-eighteen-design-decisions-follow-from-the-evidence)) and treat subscription OAuth as out of scope.
10. **Separate the UI from the protocol.** TUI flicker and CPU complaints hurt Claude Code's reputation independently of its model. [Harness decision 16](../../agent-harness/agent-harness-research.md#6-eighteen-design-decisions-follow-from-the-evidence) (one protocol with thin transports) lets the terminal UI be replaced or rewritten without touching the loop.

## Method and limits

Filters: comments were flattened from the 400 raw threads (111,593 comment nodes with text) with story id, comment id, author, date and stripped text. Tool mentions used word-boundary regexes per product (for example `\bclaude[ -]?code\b|\bCC\b`, `\bcodex\b`, `\bopen ?code\b`, `\bcursor\b`, `\bcopilot\b`, `\bgemini[ -]cli\b`, `\baider\b`, `\blangchain\b|\blanggraph\b`, `\bmcp\b`). Claims used phrase filters: switching ("switched/moved to codex", "back to claude"), combined use ("codex for review", "use both"), quotas ("hit/burn ... limit", "weekly limit"), degradation ("nerfed", "dumbed down", "degraded") within seven named threads, defence and criticism vocabulary within seven subscription-policy threads, TUI quality ("flicker", "buggy", "bloat"), harness importance ("harness matters", "no secret sauce", "200 lines"), frameworks ("agent SDK", "build my own harness"), MCP context cost versus auth and remote use, and AGENTS.md portability. Per-period rates divide matched comments by all comments in that period (26,100 in mid-2025, 54,993 in early 2026, 30,500 in late 2026).

Reading: about 550 matched comments were read as random samples of 12 to 30 per filter, drawn across all three periods and over 120 threads, plus the first top-level replies of four threads ([48883275](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883275), [46844822](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46844822), [46545620](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46545620), [50273731](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50273731)) and a 14-reply sample of the November 2026 census. Quotes come only from comments read in full context of the excerpt.

Counts: every count is distinct usernames matching a filter, not a count of people who agree. Filters catch neutral mentions, questions and replies quoting others, so counts are upper bounds; samples suggest that roughly half to two-thirds of matches in the switching, quota and framework filters express the view named. The Pi count used a harness-context heuristic that still lets through some unrelated uses of "pi"; MCP and degradation counts are the noisiest. The census tally is a keyword match on top-level replies, so a reply that mentions a tool without using it is counted.

Biases: the corpus was built from title and URL queries that include "Claude Code", "Codex CLI", "Cursor agent", "MCP" and "LangChain", so Claude Code and MCP are over-represented by construction, and products without launch threads in the corpus (OpenAI Agents SDK, Vercel AI SDK, Amp) are under-represented. Only stories with 50 or more points and the 400 most-commented were kept, which favours controversy: quota, ban and degradation threads are among the largest. HN skews towards senior engineers, individual subscribers rather than enterprise buyers, terminal users and strong opinions, so the note says little about IDE-first or enterprise-mandated users, and complaints are louder than quiet satisfaction. Several commenters on both sides may be vendor employees or founders of competing tools; this note did not try to identify them.
