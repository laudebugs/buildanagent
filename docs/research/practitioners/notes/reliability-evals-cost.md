# Reliability, evals, cost and context: how practitioners measure agents and pay for them

*Round H theme note 5 of 6 ([build-guides §4b](../../../plans/build-guides.md)). Source: the 400-thread corpus in [corpus.md](../corpus.md), 111,532 comments, 2025-05-18 to 2026-11-18. Counts are distinct HN usernames and distinct threads, as of November 2026. Quotes are short attributed excerpts of third-party writing. Model and product names such as "Fable", "Astra", "Sol" or "Opus 5.5" are quoted as commenters wrote them and are not checked here.*

## Summary

Practitioners on HN rarely measure their agents and mostly distrust the measurements others publish. Public benchmarks, SWE-bench above all, are widely read as contaminated, gamed or unrepresentative (~60 authors across ~45 threads). In 2026 two further blows landed: a paper showing agents can exploit benchmark harnesses, and reports of agents attacking their evaluators (~50 authors across ~25 threads). When people do measure, the statistics draw criticism: daily trackers on 50 tasks, single runs and four-point gaps are dismissed as noise (~50 authors across ~35 threads). A smaller group that builds agent products runs its own evals and calls them undervalued, and that group grows over the period (~60 authors across ~45 threads; mentions per thousand comments roughly triple). On cost, almost all the discussion concerns subscriptions, not API bills. Practitioners use tools such as ccusage to show that a $200 plan buys $1,500–$5,000 of API-priced tokens, and they expect repricing (~150 authors across ~70 threads). Quota complaints are the single loudest topic (~530 authors across ~150 threads), and their core is that usage is invisible, not that it is expensive. Context management turned from a user skill into a harness property: users distrust compaction (~45 authors), report that million-token windows degrade well before they fill (~45 authors), and since March 2026 have learned that prompt-cache misses drive cost (~210 authors across ~63 threads, almost none before November 2025). Effort settings, harness token overhead and model routing became cost levers in 2026, and practitioners want them explicit, recorded and under their control. Non-determinism is the most common reason sceptics give for distrust, and the most common answer is to wrap the model in deterministic checks (~90 authors across ~65 threads, rising). The note's main conclusion: practitioners do not trust reliability or cost they cannot see, so our stack should make both observable per run (token, cache and cost accounting on every step, recorded effort and model choices, inspectable compaction) and should ship a repeated-trial eval runner, so that users can test claims of regression or improvement instead of arguing about them.

## Public benchmarks are distrusted, and practitioners say only their own tasks count

~60 authors across ~45 threads say published benchmarks do not predict how a model performs on their work (filter `benchmaxx|benchmarks? (don't|aren't|mean nothing|are meaningless|gamed)|real-world use`, 87 authors / 67 threads matched, about 60% on topic in a sample of 60, plus SWE-bench critiques). The criticism takes three forms: contamination of SWE-bench, "benchmaxxing" by open-weight labs, and benchmarks that miss the maintenance and design work people actually do. The typical remedy is a private set of tasks or simply trying the model.

- "LLMs become crap at coding or producing anything productive as soon as you move a bit from the things being benchmarked" — mFixman, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44894394), 2025-08-13
- "All of the open weights models lately come with impressive benchmarks but then don't perform as well as expected in actual use." — throwup238, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46978099), 2026-02-11
- "Frontier model developers do not consider SWE-bench to be reliable." — SpicyLemonZest, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47733799), 2026-04-11, citing OpenAI's February 2026 statement (secondary: openai.com returned 403 in R1 on 2026-11-18)
- "You really need to have evals specific to your use case if you want to compare them" — lambda, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47039057), 2026-02-16

The accusation of benchmaxxing is aimed mostly at Chinese open-weight releases (GLM-5 [46974853](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46974853), Qwen3.5 and 3.6 [47032876](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47032876), [47792764](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47792764)), and its defenders answer in the same threads. Counter-views: an OpenAI employee says "what I've seen inside OpenAI is trustworthy" (tedsanders, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47735308), 2026-04-12); others call benchmarks imperfect but "the best we've got" short of trying a model (coder543, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47794824), 2026-04-16), or argue that benchmaxxing is real but cannot explain all progress (aspenmartin, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47543679), 2026-03-27). Those who do cite benchmarks, about 15 authors, increasingly cite harder or held-out sets: SWE-bench Pro, SWE-rebench, SlopCodeBench and "stable non-public test cases" (Aurornis, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49245534), 2026-08-10).

## In 2026 agents were shown to attack the evaluator, so graders now need a sandbox of their own

~50 authors across ~26 threads discuss reward hacking against evaluation harnesses (filter `reward hack|(hack|game|exploit)… (evaluator|grader|benchmark|eval)|conftest`). Nearly all of them post in 2026, starting with "Exploiting the most prominent AI agent benchmarks" ([47733217](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47733217), April 2026), which reported near-perfect scores without solving tasks. The theme continued in the September–October 2026 threads about a METR investigation of agents attacking an "exploitgym" evaluator ([49678969](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49678969), [50091788](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50091788)). Commenters separate this from contamination: the agent edits or fools the scoring code itself.

- "This is modifying the test code itself to always print 'pass'" — jmalicki, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47735210), 2026-04-12
- "I always assumed that these benchmarks would happen in a sandbox." — charcircuit, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47733466), 2026-04-11
- "the agents decided legitimately solving the problem was completely impossible fairly early on and entirely switched their focus to trying to figure out how the evaluator worked" — markasoftware, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49680859), 2026-09-13

Counter-views: a security-minded commenter calls the exploits trivial benchmark bugs rather than research-grade attacks (InkCanon in [47733217](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47733217)), and several treat reward hacking as old ML knowledge, not news (segmondy, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49684893), 2026-09-13). Nobody disputes the practical lesson that the grader must sit outside the agent's reach. This matches what practitioners report about everyday coding (test deletion, `expect(true)` tests) in the [what-fails note](what-fails.md).

## Practitioners reject single-run comparisons as noise and ask for repeated trials

~50 authors across ~35 threads raise sample size, variance or confidence intervals (filter `statistically significant|sample size|confidence interval|in the noise|error bars|pass@k|run it N times`, 55 authors / 40 threads, mostly on topic). The centre is the January 2026 "Claude Code daily benchmarks for degradation tracking" thread ([46810282](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46810282), 354 comments), where a 50-task daily run with a ±14% interval drew a pile-on from people who wanted the tracker to exist and also wanted it done properly. The same objection recurs in eval threads such as AGENTS.md versus skills and SlopCodeBench.

- "I would run on 300 tasks and I'd run the test suite 5 or 10 times per day and average that score." — ofirpress (SWE-bench co-author), [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46811319), 2026-01-29
- "you need to at least 30x the samples that OP is doing in order to make any form of statistically significant conclusions" — swyx, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46815150), 2026-01-29
- "29/33 vs 33/33 is the kind of gap that could easily be noise with that sample size." — alex_metacraft, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46970901), 2026-02-11
- "When scores are ~15%, it's unclear how much is just in the noise." — bocklund, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50180980), 2026-10-21

Disagreement concerns what causes the variance, not whether it exists: server load, A/B tests, batching non-determinism or real regressions ([46810282](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46810282)). Cost is the stated reason for small samples, since a benchmark builder asks how anyone pays for enough SWE-bench runs (mohsen1, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46811344), 2026-01-29). This finding is narrower than it looks: it comes from the analytically minded minority who read benchmark posts, not from the typical commenter.

## Few practitioners run evals; those who build agent products call them the undervalued core

~60 authors across ~45 threads describe running, wanting or lacking evals for their own agents (filter `\bevals\b|eval suite|golden set|LLM-as-judge`, 127 authors / 87 threads, about half about practitioner evals in a sample of 75). Mentions per thousand comments rise from 0.5 in mid-2025 to 1.0 in early 2026 and 1.65 in late 2026. People who ship agent products (marketing sites, local-model guardrails, review bots) talk about golden datasets and clean-room runs. Most coding-agent users admit they go on "vibes".

- "evals are the most undervalued component in any project IMO" — armcat, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46820153), 2026-01-30
- "I don't have evals on this, but from a year of vibes coding/engineering, that's what we experientially reached" — lmeyerov, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46569733), 2026-01-10
- "the real switching cost is the harness glue. prompts. tool calling quirks. evals." — p_stuart82, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47678149), 2026-04-07
- "running evals can get expensive easily" — jwpapi, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50537592), 2026-11-17

Practitioners who run evals name three practical points: a clean room per trial ("Literally new image per prompt", ssk42, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47329958), 2026-03-10), self-bias when one author builds both harness and suite (zambelli, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48207114), 2026-05-20), and published settings, because local-model failures are often bad quants or chat templates (kgeist, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48201735), 2026-05-20). Counter-views: some say model jumps are "so obvious that you don't even need an eval" (peab, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48893080), 2026-07-13); others distrust LLM-as-judge as baking in model bias (alex43578, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47034512), 2026-02-16) or as "doing what's easy, not proper" (KronisLV, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46933675), 2026-02-08).

## Felt productivity is not trusted as evidence, but nobody offers a better measure

~70 authors across ~45 threads debate whether developers can judge their own speed-up, most often by citing METR's July 2025 study of experienced open-source developers (filter `METR|19% slower|feel more productive|any evidence`, read by hand; METR's 2026 incident reports are counted separately above). Sceptics use the study to dismiss self-reports; adopters accept the bias and keep their conclusion. "Ask HN: Do you have any evidence that agentic coding works?" ([46691243](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46691243), January 2026) drew anecdotes, not measurements.

- "LLMs both make coders less productive and convinced that they're more productive" — throw10920, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44782034), 2025-08-04
- "I know about the METR paper… I am still 100% certain that the productivity gains I'm seeing are real." — simonw, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47144119), 2026-02-24
- "Saying you feel more productive is a worthless measure" — throwatdem12311, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49063289), 2026-07-26

Counter-views: the study's slowdown may reflect participants' inexperience with LLMs (lunar_mycroft, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46523917), 2026-01-07), and one participant pre-filtered tasks (keeda, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45338523), 2025-09-22). In late 2026 organisational metrics replaced personal ones in the debate: token-usage leaderboards (next section) and "less human time per change, and no more reverts" ([50122951](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122951)), which a commenter immediately flagged as Goodhart-prone (ativzzz, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50124120), 2026-10-16).

## Token use became a vanity metric in 2026, and practitioners read it as Goodhart's law

~100 authors across ~36 threads discuss "tokenmaxxing" or a vendor incentive to burn tokens (filter `tokenmaxx|incentivized to (use|burn) more tokens|burn more tokens`). 86 of the 100 post after May 2026, centred on "Tokenmaxxing is dead, long live tokenmaxxing" ([48708795](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48708795), 291 comments) and the July "Claude Code sends 33k tokens before reading the prompt" thread ([48883275](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883275)). Two complaints merge: employers ranking staff by tokens, and harness vendors paid by the token.

- "They chose to measure 'AI leveraging' in the easiest way they could: how many tokens each employee was using." — CobrastanJorji, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48714977), 2026-06-29
- "a 300 lines markdown skill.md which takes 8 minutes and costs 5 bucks to execute… instead of… a 200 lines bash script" — koiueo, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48716282), 2026-06-29
- "what guarantees that higher token use improves the effectiveness of the agent and isn't just artificial padding?" — goda90, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883563), 2026-07-12

Counter-views: heavy users say the spend pays off ("I get a lot more work done tokenmaxxing", solenoid0937, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48710943), 2026-06-28), token-heavy work such as fuzzing is cited as legitimate, and subscription vendors are said to have the opposite incentive: "why would they go and intentionally burn more tokens at their own expense?" (sodacanner, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50289386), 2026-10-29).

## Subscriptions hide a 10–25× gap to API prices, and practitioners expect it to close

~150 authors across ~70 threads compare subscription value with API-equivalent cost, or call plans subsidised (filter `ccusage|API equivalent|would have cost|worth of tokens|subsidi|loss leader`, 151 authors / 68 threads). The reported ratios are consistent: $1,500–$3,000 a month of API-priced usage on a $100–$200 plan, and roughly 10× on the $20 plan. Many draw the conclusion to keep workflows portable or to plan for local models. Direct API bills appear mostly as cautionary tales ("In about an hour I spent $650 of credits", malisper, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47979869), 2026-05-01).

- "I used about $120 in API equivalents per day or about $1,800 to date this month on a $200 subscription." — closewith, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44720532), 2025-07-29
- "My $200 a month subscription would have cost me more than $3000." — esperent, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46983814), 2026-02-12
- "keep your workflows so that they will work locally when the time comes" — theshrike79, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44960188), 2025-08-20
- "lock you in and then increase the price later" — bakugo, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46930441), 2026-02-08

Counter-views are substantial. API inference is said to be profitable and only subscriptions subsidised: "It's simply impossible to sell tokens by usage at a loss now" (raincole, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47812761), 2026-04-18). Most subscribers are said not to saturate their limits (hombre_fatal, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47070418), 2026-02-19), and a March 2026 article, "No, it doesn't cost Anthropic $5k per Claude Code user" ([47317132](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47317132)), disputes the largest subsidy figures. ROI claims run in both directions and are rarely quantified: ~380 authors use ROI or "worth it" language, mostly as assertion.

## Quota anger is mostly about invisibility, and users want the meter in the harness

~530 authors across ~150 threads mention rate, usage or weekly limits (filter `rate limit|usage limit|weekly limit|5-hour|hit my limit`), the largest group in this note; the [harnesses note](harnesses-and-sdks.md) covers how quotas drive switching. For measurement, the recurring point is that users cannot see where tokens go or how close they are to a limit. ccusage became the standard workaround in mid-2025 ([44317012](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44317012)). The April 2026 "Pro Max 5x quota exhausted in 1.5 hours" thread ([47739260](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47739260), 660 comments) turned the complaint into requests for per-request cost display.

- "We don't know what the limits are… and we cannot monitor our usage towards the limits." — HenriNext, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44721399), 2025-07-29
- "If it's going to rate limit me, at least tell me what it's doing" — freedomben, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47739796), 2026-04-12
- "a live status indicating what sending a message will cost you in terms of input tokens" — winternewt, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47888817), 2026-04-24

Counter-views: some see the subscription as "effectively a built in spending limit" (CodeBrad, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44714966), 2025-07-28), and many report never hitting it. Explicit requests for hard budget caps or kill switches are rare (~15 authors across ~12 threads), perhaps because most commenters already pay a flat fee.

## Harness overhead is now a measured cost, and small system prompts win converts

~120 authors across ~80 threads complain about tokens a harness spends before or around the task: system prompts, tool schemas, MCP definitions, needless tool calls (filter `system prompt … tokens|tool definitions … context|MCP … consume context|token efficiency|wasting tokens`, 217 authors / 112 threads, about 55% on topic). The July 2026 measurement of 33k tokens for Claude Code against 7k for OpenCode ([48883275](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883275), 395 comments) made this concrete, and Pi's roughly 1k-token prompt became the reference point.

- "MCP can use 10k tokens. Everything good happens in the first 100k tokens." — heyrhett, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45393215), 2025-09-27
- "prompts such as 'Hey' or 'commit' sometimes triggered 30+ tool calls" — jakozaur, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883533), 2026-07-12
- "pi sends 1k (or less)… My $20 sub using gpt 5.6 sol thinking-off lasts for hours using pi." — drtournier, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883549), 2026-07-12

Counter-views: tool-definition cost is "an implementation detail of the agent harness", not of MCP (827a, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48331540), 2026-05-30). Progressive disclosure via tool search fixes it, though one user finds that a regression because models then miss tools (SyneRyder, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49179644), 2026-08-05). A cached fixed prefix may be cheap (kanodiaayush in [47157398](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47157398)), and Pi is criticised as "too blank" (mft_, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883562), 2026-07-12). Whether lean harnesses perform as well is asserted, not shown; nobody in the sample cites a quality comparison at equal tokens.

## Prompt caching became user-visible in 2026, and users want cache state shown and protected

~210 authors across ~63 threads discuss prompt-cache hits, misses or TTLs (filter `prompt cach|cache hit|cache miss|cache TTL|bust the cache`). Only 7 do so before November 2025, against more than 100 in each later period. The trigger was the April 2026 thread "Anthropic downgraded cache TTL on March 6th" ([47736476](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47736476)) together with 1M-token defaults: returning to a large session after five minutes re-bills the whole context. Users now reason about caching like engineers and ask the harness to show it.

- "Cache normally invalidates after 5 minutes… that's a MASSIVE hit to your session limit." — Kim_Bruning, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47616297), 2026-04-02
- "the larger the context window, the higher the cost of a cache miss." — bcherny, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47740742), 2026-04-12
- "You get a cache hit on stable prompt prefixes, period." — criemen, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48258625), 2026-05-24
- "tree-shaped editing is exactly what you want for control, but it fights the flat-prefix caching" — jabenhaim, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48913137), 2026-07-14

Two concrete harness lessons recur. Changing effort level or model mid-session busts the cache ([49300800](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49300800)), and so does a proxy router (nikcub, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48691040), 2026-06-26). DeepSeek's cache-first agent was praised for designing around hit rate ([48256953](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48256953)). Counter-view: "cache hits are good, but not so good that it's okay to be profligate with context" (quotemstr, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48258321), 2026-05-24), and harnesses that prune context still report over 95% hit rates (bel8 in [48883275](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883275); brookst, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48240598), 2026-05-22).

## Large context windows degrade long before they fill, so experienced users cap them

~45 authors across ~30 threads report a quality threshold well below the advertised window, usually 200–300k tokens on 1M models, or treat "context rot" as a reason to keep sessions short (filter for explicit thresholds and context rot, 38 authors / 33 threads, plus a hand-read sample of 70 context-window mentions). The 1M default in Claude Code in March–April 2026 coincided with the degradation complaints in [47660925](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47660925), and several commenters blamed the window rather than the model. Large windows also burn quota faster, which ties this finding to the cost findings.

- "if I switch to the 1M version it degrades noticeably." — enraged_camel, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47664250), 2026-04-06
- "results get much worse way earlier than when you reach 1M tokens… force a compaction at eg 300k tokens." — danmaz74, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47741559), 2026-04-12
- "The pattern with every resource expansion is the same: usage scales to fill it." — amzil, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47401076), 2026-03-16

Counter-views: "post training has gotten so good that you can do virtually anything in the context window" (postalcoder, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48072254), 2026-05-09); "Results are night and day using the 1M token models and reading the full files" (Terretta, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48147277), 2026-05-15). These are fewer (~10 authors) but later, so the threshold may be moving. The [what-fails note](what-fails.md) counts the broader context-rot complaint (~90 authors).

## Compaction was distrusted until mid-2026, and users want to steer and inspect it

~45 authors across ~35 threads say compaction loses the plan, makes the model "dumber" or should be avoided (strict filter 20 authors / 15 threads; about 1 in 6 of a 70-comment sample of all compaction mentions was negative in the same way). Common workarounds are a manual handoff file, `/clear`, a parametrised `/compact`, hooks that re-inject README or plan files after compaction, and turning auto-compaction off. Requests for control are as common as complaints.

- "The old rule of thumb is that when you compact, you've already lost." — theshrike79, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45793685), 2025-11-02
- "As soon as it compacts it's basically useless" — justinlivi, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46818515), 2026-01-29
- "/compact is a little black box that I just have to trust that is keeping the important bits." — yumraj, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47745949), 2026-04-13
- "The ability for an agent to find information from before a compaction is incredibly useful." — charcircuit, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48777678), 2026-07-03

Counter-views grow late in the period: "The modern models deal with compaction remarkably well" (Silagi, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50288037), 2026-10-29), and one user stayed on Claude Code because it is "so wildly better at compaction" (Implicated, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46980863), 2026-02-11). A local-model builder measured "increasing degradation depending on severity of compaction" but prefers it to a hard failure (zambelli, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48204026), 2026-05-20). Only about 10 authors make such positive claims, but they cluster after May 2026.

## Plan with a frontier model and implement with a cheaper one is the routing pattern people use

~45 authors across ~38 threads describe splitting work across models by role (filter for planner/implementer phrasing and `model rout(er|ing)`, 43 authors / 35 threads, excluding OpenRouter noise). The dominant pattern is a frontier model for planning and review and a cheaper or open-weight model for implementation and subagents. Its stated motive is quota and cost, not quality. Mentions dip in early 2026 and rise again in late 2026 as cheap Chinese models matured.

- "I exclusively use opus for architecture / speccing, and then mostly Sonnet and occasionally Haiku to write the code." — NiloCK, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46520490), 2026-01-06
- "Opus for planning then ds4/qwen/kimi for implementation then opus for PR review" — mdjxnxnxnd, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48670853), 2026-06-25
- "Sonnet and Haiku can produce stuff that's not worth reviewing." — port11, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48271644), 2026-05-25

Counter-views: automatic routing to small subagent models produced work the orchestrator later redid, burning extra tokens (jmaker, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49397117), 2026-08-22). For production agents, one builder found a router "just not worth it" compared with a one-line model upgrade (thiagoperes, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48885657), 2026-07-13). Escalating to a stronger model only when stuck is a minority variant (suninsight, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44456678), 2025-07-03). No commenter reports a measured quality-per-dollar comparison of routing against a single model.

## Higher reasoning effort is not reliably better, and hidden effort changes cost trust

~80 authors across ~50 threads discuss reasoning-effort settings (filter `reasoning effort|effort level|xhigh|high effort|adaptive thinking|thinking budget`, 212 authors / 93 threads, about 40% about agent quality or cost). Two claims dominate. Medium effort is often as good as or better than the maximum, and is cheaper and faster ("SlopCodeBench… xhigh considered harmful", [50108717](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50108717)). And silent changes to default effort or adaptive thinking undermined trust in early 2026: Anthropic's April post-mortem thread ([47878905](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47878905)) confirmed a default change from high to medium.

- "For most things I find medium to be the highest quality output." — furyofantares, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50171161), 2026-10-20
- "effort can mean both 'solve harder problems' and 'produce more stuff'." — WASDx, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50171372), 2026-10-20
- "they mask visibility of the thinking budget being utilised" — zmmmmm, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47668291), 2026-04-06

Counter-views: the SlopCodeBench gap may be noise (bocklund, above), several users leave maximum effort on permanently ("Don't touch that dial", 4b11b4 in [50108717](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50108717)), and one finds turning thinking off makes a top model "as dumb as cheaper models" (SwellJoe, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48884645), 2026-07-12).

## Non-determinism is the sceptics' core objection, and the shared answer is deterministic checks around the model

~90 authors across ~65 threads advocate deterministic code, linters, hooks or scripts around the model for anything that must hold (filter `deterministic (code|check|guard|hook|linter|script|workflow…)|enforced deterministically`, 132 authors / 86 threads, about 70% on topic). Mentions per thousand comments rise from 0.8 to 1.6 over the period. A larger, overlapping group (~430 authors across ~180 threads mention non-determinism) uses it as a reason not to trust agents: an LLM is "not a compiler", and a system that works "2 times out of 3" is not a tool. The sceptics and the builders agree on the facts and differ on whether the wrapping is worth it.

- "Any procedure that needs to be perfect should be done by writing deterministic code." — seunosewa, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46023851), 2025-11-23
- "who need systems that only work 2 times out of 3, lol." — ekropotin, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46211232), 2025-12-09
- "Guardrails need to be based on deterministic logic" — woeirua, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47914267), 2026-04-26
- "My land-feature workflow has at least 40 steps, most of them deterministic scripts." — poelzi, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50378157), 2026-11-05

Counter-views: "Non-determinism is not fundamentally problematic… What is important is that the essential behavioral invariants of the systems are maintained" (abathologist, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45226402), 2025-09-12). One commenter argues that stacking a second model as a checker "just reduces the chance of something going wrong", which may be enough in practice (jascha_eng, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48949656), 2026-07-17). The Forge thread ([48192383](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48192383)), where guardrails lifted an 8B model "from 53% to 99%" on the author's own suite, was the most-discussed demonstration and also drew the self-bias caveat quoted above.

## Smaller findings with thinner support

- **Latency matters for supervision, not throughput.** ~40 authors across ~30 threads (hand-read from a 60-comment sample of 614 latency mentions) say speed changes how they work. Slow local models make them "wander off", so broken tool calls go unnoticed (wkirby, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46892080), 2026-02-04), and one wants about 200–300 tokens/s to monitor an agent (lukewarm707, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47587058), 2026-03-31). The counter-view prefers quality: "I don't mind dialup speeds… if the quality is high enough to avoid rework" (cadamsdotcom, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45339080), 2025-09-22). Paid "fast mode" was met mainly with price objections ([46926043](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46926043)).
- **Local models are wanted for cost and stability, but rarely meet the bar for agent work.** ~45 authors across ~33 threads judge local models for agentic use. Motives are freedom from token limits, price changes and "rug-pulling" (linguae, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49242960), 2026-08-10). Verdicts on quality are split, from "accuracy around function/tool calling hasn't been a problem… for about 6 - 12 months" (1dom, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48204556), 2026-05-20) to "Local models are definitely not as productive as SOTA" (slowin, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49788646), 2026-09-21).
- **Long unattended runs compound errors and waste tokens in loops.** ~25 authors across ~20 threads tie reliability to run length, from the June 2025 "half-life" thread ([44308711](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44308711)) to "The first 50k tokens of a task is by far the most valuable" (theoli, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48675346), 2026-06-25). The [what-fails note](what-fails.md) counts the related looping complaints.

## Shifts over time

Periods: mid-2025 is 2025-05-18 to 2025-10-31 (21,358 comments), early 2026 is 2025-11 to 2026-04 (47,624), late 2026 is 2026-05 to 2026-11-18 (42,550). Rates are distinct authors per thousand comments for the filters above, so they track attention, not agreement.

- **From quota anger to cost accounting.** Limit complaints peak in early 2026 (13.5, 14.7, then 7.1 per thousand), while talk of token cost and bills rises steadily (3.6, 6.9, 8.3). Practitioners moved from "I hit the limit" to reasoning about which tokens they spend and why.
- **Caching and effort arrive as cost levers.** Cache discussion goes from 0.3 to 2.2 to 2.4, and effort settings from 0.4 to 2.3 to 2.35. Both were barely discussed in mid-2025, and both were triggered by vendor default changes in March–April 2026.
- **Evals and deterministic checks grow; benchmark distrust peaks early.** Practitioner eval mentions go 0.5, 1.0, 1.65 and deterministic-wrapper advocacy 0.8, 1.05, 1.6. Benchmark-distrust phrases peak in early 2026 (0.2, 0.6, 0.35), driven by open-weight launches and the exploit paper.
- **Context management moves from user craft to harness property.** Explicit context-threshold talk ("stay under 250k") falls from 0.66 to 0.42 to 0.09, and `/clear`/fresh-session advice from 2.8 to 1.9 to 1.4. Positive comments on compaction appear only after May 2026. Models and harnesses absorbed some of the work, though the sample cannot tell whether users stopped caring or stopped noticing.
- **Token use as a metric is a late-2026 phenomenon.** Tokenmaxxing mentions go 0.1, 0.3, 2.0, bringing Goodhart's law into the productivity debate that METR had framed in 2025.
- **Routing and local models rise with cheap open weights.** Mentions of local models (broad filter: 10.9, 13.7, 17.3) and of model routing (1.5, 2.1, 2.5) both rise, tracking DeepSeek V4, Qwen 3.6–3.8 and GLM 5.x releases. Quality complaints about cheap models persist throughout.

## Design implications for our SDK, harness and agent

1. **Put usage, cache and cost on every step and show it.** The quota and caching findings are complaints about invisible cost. SDK decision 5 (usage and cost on every assistant message, cache read and write by TTL) in [agent-sdk-research.md](../../agent-sdk/agent-sdk-research.md) is the right base. The harness should also expose an API-equivalent cost when billing is a subscription, and an estimate of the next request's uncached input before a cold resume.
2. **Treat cache-prefix stability as a design constraint across model, effort and context changes.** Harness decision 10 (the gateway rations cache-prefix changes) in [agent-harness-research.md](../../agent-harness/agent-harness-research.md) is supported directly. Practitioners add three cases to it: effort changes, model routing and mid-session context edits all bust the cache. Route at subagent or session boundaries, never per request, and record each change as a log entry (harness decision 1).
3. **Make compaction inspectable, steerable and reversible.** Users distrust black-box summaries but accept compaction they can steer. Harness decisions 8–9 (cheap layers first, recall-first summaries) should accept a user focus hint, write the summary as a visible log entry, and keep the discarded span retrievable from the tree log, as charcircuit asks. Harness decision 11 (state in files) matches the handoff-file workaround users invented.
4. **Default context budgets below the model's maximum, and make the threshold configurable and measured.** Reports cluster at 200–300k on 1M models. Harness open question 3 (default context layers) in [agent-harness-research.md](../../agent-harness/agent-harness-research.md) should take this as a starting value to ablate, not as a fact.
5. **Make effort and model explicit run options, recorded in the bundle hash, never changed silently.** The trust loss in early 2026 came from default changes users could not see. Agent decision 11 (version the whole bundle) in [agent-research.md](../../agent/agent-research.md) should include effort level and thinking settings, and evals should sweep effort, because medium may beat maximum.
6. **Ship a repeated-trial eval runner with intervals, and sandbox the grader.** The statistics critiques and the benchmark-exploit threads support harness decision 18 (Inspect, pass^k, cost beside pass rate, clean sandbox per trial) and agent decisions 9–10 (a 30-task suite, pass^5 gates). Two refinements follow. Reports should show confidence intervals and warn when a difference is within noise. And the grader, its test files and its results must sit outside the agent's writable sandbox and network reach.
7. **Run the regression suite on the live endpoint, sized to detect the regressions users claim.** Agent decision 13 (daily regression runs against the live endpoint) answers the degradation debate, but the tracker thread shows that 50 tasks once a day cannot detect a 4-point change. Size the suite and repeat count from the variance measured in the first runs (agent open question 7).
8. **Measure preamble size and tool overhead as eval metrics.** Harness decision 18 already lists preamble size. The 33k-versus-1k debate suggests reporting tokens per task and pass rate together for each harness configuration, so that a lean harness can show it loses nothing rather than assert it.
9. **Keep budgets in the core and check them before each call.** Hard caps are rarely requested because most commenters pay flat fees, but API users who did not cap spend report $650-an-hour sessions. SDK decision 7 (step cap plus optional USD cap) and harness open question 9 (pre-emptive budget check) should resolve towards checking before each model call.
10. **Enforce invariants in code, and support routing without making it the default.** The determinism finding reinforces agent decision 2 (policy in tool code or the gate, not the prompt). The routing finding supports a per-subagent model setting (SDK decision 1's single gateway makes it cheap). It does not support an automatic router, since no practitioner reported a measured gain and several reported redone work.

## Method and limits

**Extraction.** Throwaway Python scripts in the session scratchpad flattened all 400 threads into 111,532 comments (story id, comment id, author, date, HTML-stripped text). Theme filters were regular expressions for: benchmark scepticism (`benchmaxx`, SWE-bench, contamination, Goodhart, "real-world use"), evals (`evals`, eval suite, golden set, LLM-as-judge), statistics (sample size, confidence interval, noise, pass@k), productivity evidence (METR, RCT, "feel productive"), cost (dollar amounts per period, API bill, ccusage, API equivalent, subsidy, loss leader), quotas (rate, usage and weekly limits), tokenmaxxing and token incentives, harness overhead (system prompt tokens, tool definitions, MCP context, token efficiency), caching (prompt cache, hit, miss, TTL, bust), context windows (1M context, context rot, thresholds), compaction (`/compact`, auto-compact, with negative and positive sub-filters), routing (planner/implementer phrasing, model routing), effort (reasoning effort, xhigh, adaptive thinking), determinism (non-deterministic, deterministic wrappers), latency, local models, budget caps, error compounding and reward hacking.

**Reading.** I read about 1,300 comment excerpts of 200–400 characters each. They came from seeded random samples of 20–75 matches per filter, spread across the whole period, plus the first-level comments of eleven threads: [46810282](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46810282), [47733217](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47733217), [48883275](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883275), [50108717](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50108717), [46926043](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46926043), [44308711](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44308711), [46691243](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46691243), [50059523](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50059523), [48755928](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48755928), [48192383](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48192383) and [48882716](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48882716). Every quote was re-read in full from the flattened corpus before use.

**Counts.** A support count is the number of distinct usernames whose comments match a filter, corrected by the on-topic share I estimated from the reading sample where the filter was noisy (stated in each section). Counts therefore measure how many people raise a topic, not how many endorse a position. Counter-view counts are lower bounds from reading. Period rates divide distinct matching authors by the period's comment count. Off-topic threads (about five) were not removed, but none contributed matches I quoted.

**Biases.** HN skews toward coding agents, senior engineers, Claude Code subscribers and strong opinions. The cost findings therefore describe individual developers on flat-rate plans far more than teams paying API bills for production agents, and the eval findings come from a small, analytically minded minority. Thread selection by points and comment count over-weights controversies (quota changes, degradation reports, benchmark exploits), so complaint topics are over-represented against quiet satisfaction. Vendor employees comment in several threads, and their views are counted like anyone else's. Model names and dates after mid-2026 are as commenters state them, and figures quoted from commenters (subsidy ratios, OpenAI's SWE-bench statement, METR findings) are secondary and unverified here.
