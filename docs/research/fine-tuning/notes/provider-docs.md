# Provider fine-tuning: what OpenAI, Anthropic and Google offer (as of November 2026)

Research notes, gathered 2026-11-19. Each page was fetched with `curl`, Markdown variants where the site offers them (OpenAI's `.md` pages, Claude's `.md` pages), and read as data.

## Takeaway

Self-serve fine-tuning has nearly gone from two of the three providers. OpenAI closed fine-tuning to new organisations on 7 May 2026 and ends new jobs for everyone on 6 January 2027. Its last SFT base models, GPT-4.1 and GPT-4.1 mini, shut down on 14 January 2027, and the base of its reinforcement fine-tuning, `o4-mini`, shut down on 23 October 2026. Anthropic's API has never offered fine-tuning. Its only tunable model, Claude 3 Haiku on Amazon Bedrock, has reached end of life in Bedrock's own lifecycle table, although Bedrock's fine-tuning page still lists it. Google is the one provider still selling tuning of its current models: SFT on five Gemini models and reinforcement learning fine-tuning (RLFT) on Gemini 3.5 Flash and 3.1 Flash-Lite, on Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform (formerly Vertex AI). The Gemini API and AI Studio offer no tuning.

## OpenAI

- **Model optimization guide** ([model-optimization.md](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/model-optimization)): "OpenAI is winding down the fine-tuning platform. The platform is no longer accessible to new users, but existing users of the fine-tuning platform will be able to create training jobs for the coming months." "All fine-tuned models will remain available for inference until their base models are deprecated." The page also says it "covers evals and fine-tuning workflows that are being moved into legacy documentation".
- **Workflow** (same page): "Optimizing model output requires a combination of evals, prompt engineering, and fine-tuning". Step 1 is "Write evals", step 2 "Prompt the model", and step 3 "For some use cases, it may be desirable to fine-tune". "The prompt engineering process may be all you need in order to get great results for your use case."
- **Four benefits over prompting alone** (same page): more examples than fit in context; "shorter prompts with fewer examples and context data, which saves on token costs at scale and can be lower latency"; training on proprietary data "without having to include it via examples in every request"; and training "a smaller, cheaper, faster model to excel at a particular task where a larger model is not cost-effective".
- **Methods table** (same page): SFT (classification, nuanced translation, specific formats, "Correcting instruction-following failures") on `gpt-4.1-2025-04-14`, `gpt-4.1-mini-2025-04-14`, `gpt-4.1-nano-2025-04-14`; vision fine-tuning on `gpt-4o-2024-08-06`; DPO on the three GPT-4.1 models. The table lists no reinforcement fine-tuning.
- **SFT guide** ([supervised-fine-tuning.md](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/supervised-fine-tuning)): "Eligible existing customers can create training jobs until January 6, 2027, unless the base model shuts down earlier." "`gpt-4.1-nano-2025-04-14` and its fine-tuned versions shut down on October 23, 2026. `gpt-4.1-2025-04-14`, `gpt-4.1-mini-2025-04-14`, and their fine-tuned versions shut down on January 14, 2027." Data: "The minimum number of examples you can provide for fine-tuning is 10"; "We see improvements from fine-tuning on 50–100 examples"; "We recommend starting with 50 well-crafted demonstrations"; "If 50 examples have no impact, rethink your task or prompt before adding training data."
- **Distillation**: `/guides/distillation` now redirects (HTTP 301) to `supervised-fine-tuning#distilling-from-a-larger-model`, which describes distillation as SFT on a larger model's outputs: "Tune a prompt for a larger model (like `gpt-4.1`) until you get great performance against your eval criteria", capture its outputs, then "Tune a smaller model (like `gpt-4.1-mini`) using the dataset you created from the large model."
- **RFT**: `/guides/reinforcement-fine-tuning` now redirects (HTTP 301) to the API reference `/api/reference/resources/fine_tuning`.
- **Deprecations page** ([deprecations.md](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)), "Update to OpenAI's self-serve fine-tuning": notified May 7, 2026. "May 7, 2026: Creating fine-tuning jobs or training is not available to organizations that have not previously run fine-tuning." "July 2, 2026: Creating fine-tuning jobs is no longer available to organizations that have not run inference on a fine-tuned model in the past 60 days." "Jan 6, 2027: Active existing customers will no longer be able to create new fine-tuning jobs on this date. Inference on fine-tuned models will be disabled only when the underlying base model is deprecated." The page gives no reason for the change.
- **Deprecations, models.** Upcoming: GPT-4o (`gpt-4o-2024-08-06`, `gpt-4o-2024-11-20`), GPT-4o mini, GPT-4.1 and GPT-4.1 mini shut down on Jan 14, 2027 (notice Oct 14, 2026), replacement `gpt-6-luna`. Past: `ft-o4-mini-2025-04-16` and `o4-mini-2025-04-16` shut down Oct 23, 2026, replacement `gpt-5.6-terra`; `gpt-4.1-nano` the same day. "Fine-tuned versions of the models listed above follow the same shutdown schedule as their underlying base models."
- **Notice periods** (same page): "Generally available models: At least 6 months."
- **Evals**: "Nov 30, 2026: Evals dashboard, API, and graders for eval workflows" become "Read-only", with "Migrate to Promptfoo" as the replacement.
- **Pricing** ([pricing.md](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)), "Fine-tuning", per 1M tokens, Standard: `gpt-4.1-2025-04-14` training $25.00, input $3.00, output $12.00, cached input $0.75; `gpt-4.1-mini-2025-04-14` training $5.00, input $0.80, output $3.20, cached $0.20; `gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18` training $3.00, input $0.30, output $1.20. "Fine-tuning is closed to new users. Existing users can continue training for the coming months." Base rates on the same page: `gpt-4.1` $2.00 / $8.00 / cached $0.50; `gpt-4.1-mini` $0.40 / $1.60 / $0.10; `gpt-6-luna` $0.10 input, $0.50 output (short context).
- **Optimizing LLM accuracy** ([optimizing-llm-accuracy.md](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/optimizing-llm-accuracy)): optimization is "more of a matrix". "Context optimization" is for when "the model lacks contextual knowledge", knowledge "is out of date", or it needs proprietary information; it "maximizes response accuracy". "LLM optimization" is for "inconsistent results with incorrect formatting", wrong "tone or style", or reasoning "not being followed consistently"; it "maximizes consistency of behavior". Fine-tuning best practice: "Start with prompt-engineering"; "Start with 50+ examples"; "if you have a RAG application, fine-tune the model with RAG examples in it". It calls logging pilot prompts and outputs into a training set "prompt baking".
- **Agent guide** ([A practical guide to building agents](https://cdn.openai.com/business-guides-and-resources/a-practical-guide-to-building-agents.pdf), PDF read 2026-11-19), "Selecting your models": "build your agent prototype with the most capable model for every task to establish a performance baseline. From there, try swapping in smaller models". Principles: "Set up evals to establish a performance baseline"; "Focus on meeting your accuracy target with the best models available"; "Optimize for cost and latency by replacing larger models with smaller ones". The PDF never mentions fine-tuning (`grep -i "fine\|tuning"` matches only "define" and "distilling insights").
- **TypeScript SDK**: `openai` 7.49.0, installed in `verify/openai-agents-sdk/node_modules`, still types `method.type: 'supervised' | 'dpo' | 'reinforcement'` (`resources/fine-tuning/jobs/jobs.d.ts`, lines 268 and 516) and `client.fineTuning` (`client.d.ts:357`).

## Anthropic

- **Claude docs index** ([llms.txt](https://platform.claude.com/llms.txt)): no page mentions fine-tuning (`grep -i "fine-tun\|finetun\|distill"` finds nothing).
- **Prompt engineering overview** ([overview.md](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/overview)): assumes "A clear definition of the success criteria", "Some ways to empirically test against those criteria" and "A first draft prompt". "Not every success criterion or failing eval is best solved by prompt engineering. For example, you can sometimes improve latency and cost more easily by choosing a different model."
- **Haiku fine-tuning announcement** ([Fine-tune Claude 3 Haiku in Amazon Bedrock](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fine-tune-claude-3-haiku), July 10, 2024; update: "generally available. (November 1, 2024)"): Anthropic "fine-tuned Haiku to moderate online comments", improving "classification accuracy from 81.5% to 99.6% while reducing tokens per query by 85%". SK Telecom quote: "a 73% increase in positive feedback for our agents' responses and a 37% improvement in key performance indicators". Vendor claims.
- **Model deprecations** ([model-deprecations.md](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations)): `claude-3-haiku-20240307` "Retired", notice February 19, 2026, retirement April 20, 2026, replacement `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`.
- **Bedrock, fine-tuning page** ([custom-model-supported](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/custom-model-supported.html)): lists "Anthropic Claude 3 Haiku anthropic.claude-3-haiku-20240307-v1:0:200k us-west-2" as the only Anthropic model.
- **Bedrock, lifecycle (Legacy)** ([model-lifecycle-legacy](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-lifecycle-legacy.html)): under "Models that have reached EOL", Claude 3 Haiku in regions including us-west-2: Legacy March 10, 2026, EOL September 10, 2026. "The following models have passed their EOL date. They have been removed from the Regions listed, and requests to them fail." Also: "after the model is in Legacy state, you cannot create new fine-tuning jobs on that model."
- **Bedrock methods** ([custom-models](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/custom-models.html)): supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement fine-tuning and distillation; training is charged "number of tokens in training data corpus × number of epochs" plus "model storage charged per month per model".
- **Bedrock RFT** ([reinforcement-fine-tuning](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/reinforcement-fine-tuning.html)): supported on Amazon Nova 2 Lite, OpenAI gpt-oss-20B and Qwen3 32B; uses GRPO; "improves model accuracy by up to 66% on average compared to base models" (vendor claim). Reward functions are Lambda code or a model-as-judge.

## Google

- **Gemini API** ([model tuning](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/model-tuning), now served from aistudio.google.com): "With the deprecation of Gemini 1.5 Flash-001 in May 2025, we no longer have a model available which supports fine-tuning in the Gemini API or AI Studio". "We don't have immediate plans to bring fine-tuning support".
- **Introduction to tuning** ([tuning](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/tuning), the old Vertex AI URL redirects here): prompting suits "Limited labeled data" and "Rapid prototyping"; fine-tuning when "You have labeled data: A sizable dataset to train on (think 100 examples or more)" and for "Complex or unique tasks". "Start with prompting to find the optimal prompt. Then, move on to fine-tuning (if required) to further boost performance or fix recurring errors."
- **Methods table** (same page): SFT, "Hundreds to thousands of labeled examples", for "Strict output formatting, classification, sentiment analysis, entity extraction, and domain-specific style or tone"; preference tuning, "Hundreds to thousands of preference pairs"; RLFT, "Dozens to a few hundred prompts", for "Multi-step reasoning, complex domain rules, verifiable tasks (such as code or SQL generation)". RLFT scores candidates "on a [-1, 1] scale" with "up to 16 reward functions" of four types (string match, a Gemini autorater, code execution, Cloud Run) and "updates a parameter-efficient LoRA adapter".
- **Supported models** (same page): SFT on Gemini 3.8 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, 3.5 Flash, 3.1 Flash-Lite, 2.5 Pro, 2.5 Flash-Lite and 2.5 Flash; preference tuning on 2.5 Flash-Lite and 2.5 Flash; RLFT on 3.5 Flash and 3.1 Flash-Lite.
- **Model versions** ([model-versions](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/model-versions)): `gemini-2.5-pro`, `gemini-2.5-flash` and `gemini-2.5-flash-lite` retire October 20, 2026. `gemini-3.5-flash` released May 19, 2026, retirement "May 19, 2027 or later"; `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` May 7, 2026, "May 7, 2027 or later". These are "Models available for at least 12 months after release". So the tuning page still lists three retired models, and both preference-tuning models among them.
- **Pricing** ([Agent Platform pricing](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/pricing), redirects to cloud.google.com/model-garden/pricing): "Training tokens are calculated by the total number of tokens in your training dataset, multiplied by your number of epochs." Gemini 3.5 Flash, SFT and RLFT, "$0.01 / 1,000 count" (one price cell spans both rows); 3.5 Flash-Lite and 3.1 Flash-Lite SFT $0.003 / 1,000; 2.5 Pro SFT $0.025 / 1,000. Gemini 3.5 Flash inference, global: $1.50 per 1M input tokens (≤200K), $0.15 cached, $9.00 output. "Tuned model endpoint will be 1.5 times of the base model in non-global region. Tuned model endpoint has the same prediction price as the base model in global region."
