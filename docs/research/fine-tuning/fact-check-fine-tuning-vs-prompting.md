# Fact-check log: fine-tuning versus prompting report

The fine-tuning versus prompting report holds up, and its thesis stands: an agent builder should default to prompting, tools and evals, and tune only for a gap an eval proves, at a volume or latency that repays the training, on a base model that lives long enough to repay it. Of the 169 rows below, 103 are confirmed (✅), 47 needed a correction (✏️) and 19 rest on inference or on absence (⚠️). The first five sections were each checked as soon as they were drafted, before the next was written. The last four (Teach, Examples, Plan table and Conclusion) were drafted in one pass and checked together, a departure from the per-section rule that their block records. The writing review's fixes are re-checked in rows 113–120, the first code-review round's (pull request #62) in rows 121–144, the second round's in rows 145–147, the edits after the owner's decisions in rows 148–156. The gateway README's tuned-model copy is checked in rows B21-1 to B21-8, and that copy's first code-review round in rows B21-9 to B21-13.

None of the corrections changes a finding. Eight narrowed a claim to what its source says: LoRA Land's models are not all "small" (row 2), MAP attributes the "brittle" remark to interviewed teams in general (row 3), Bedrock tunes non-Google models too (row 9), LoRA trains "selected weight matrices" (row 20), Qi et al. broke "guardrails" (row 21), only two agent papers name a closed comparison model (row 32), Bedrock's RFT models are not all open-weight (row 53), and Cognition benchmarked on Scale AI's SWE-Bench Pro (row 82). Four fixed numbers or arithmetic: the base-model lifetimes are 16 to 30 months, not 12 to 21 (row 8), the break-even condition for a tuned GPT-4.1 mini includes its doubled output rate (row 59), MAP gives question ids for 16 of 17 aspects (row 77), and the example's figures are now computed from named rates (row 106). Two replaced an unchecked phrase ("the best-known tuning service", row 4; "one published result", row 17), and one each fixed "has never offered" (row 44), "a single region" (row 61), the shipped catalog's contents (row 98) and the notice-versus-lifetime comparison (row 118). Two added or fixed a citation (rows 90 and 92), and three record wording changes with no outside claim (rows 116, 117 and 120). The code review's eighteen corrections (rows 121–144) also leave the findings standing. They fix how the gateway takes a caller's catalog (rows 121 and 123), add MAP's domain-knowledge and cost reasons for tuning and qualify six sentences to match (rows 124 and 126–131), soften one judgement in the conclusion (row 132), and label inferences and judgements that the text had stated flatly (rows 133 and 135–141, one of them the AWS mention removed in row 138).
