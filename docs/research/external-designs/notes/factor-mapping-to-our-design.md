## 1. What are the twelve factors, and what does each claim?

### Takeaway
The guide lists 12 factors plus an appendix "Factor 13" on pre-fetching. Its thesis is that good agents are "mostly deterministic code" with small LLM steps, and that teams outgrow frameworks at a 70–80% quality bar; its evidence is the author's experience with "at least 100 SaaS builders", not measurements.

### Cited Findings
- Thesis: builders "Get to 70-80% quality bar", "Realize that getting past 80% requires reverse-engineering the framework", then "Start over from scratch"; the fastest route is "to take small, modular concepts from agent building, and incorporate them into their existing product" — [README, "Why 12-factor agents?"](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/README.md). This is an opinion essay from a vendor (HumanLayer); one HN commenter calls a later HumanLayer post "a long ad for his product" — [HN 49033041](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49033041) (secondary, hostile).
- The README's naive loop: "LLM determines the next step … Deterministic code executes the tool call … The result is appended to the context window … Repeat until … 'done'"; the initial event may be "a user message, maybe a cron fired, maybe a webhook" — [README, "Agents as loops"](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/README.md).
- Factor titles, in the repo's order, with each one's core claim:
  1. **Natural Language to Tool Calls** — translate a request into a structured object; "From there, deterministic code can pick up the payload" — [factor-01](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-01-natural-language-to-tool-calls.md).
  2. **Own your prompts** — "Don't outsource your prompt engineering to a framework"; "treat them as first-class code"; benefits include tests and evals and "Role Hacking" — [factor-02](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-02-own-your-prompts.md).
  3. **Own your context window** — "You don't necessarily need to use standard message-based formats"; pack history into custom (e.g. XML-style) events, possibly a single user message; "Consider hiding errors and failed calls from context window once they are resolved"; filter sensitive data — [factor-03](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-03-own-your-context-window.md).
  4. **Tools are just structured outputs** — "structured output from your LLM that triggers deterministic code"; "Just because an LLM 'called a tool' doesn't mean you have to go execute a specific corresponding function in the same way every time" — [factor-04](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-04-tools-are-structured-outputs.md).
  5. **Unify execution state and business state** — "you can engineer your application so that you can infer all execution state from the context window"; benefits: one source of truth, trivial serialization, resume from any point, fork by copying a subset of the thread; keep things like session ids and "password contexts" outside but minimise them — [factor-05](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-05-unify-execution-state.md).
  6. **Launch/Pause/Resume with simple APIs** — launch, query, resume and stop with simple APIs; pause for long-running operations; webhooks resume "without deep integration with the agent orchestrator"; orchestrators often cannot pause "between the moment of tool selection and tool execution" — [factor-06](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-06-launch-pause-resume.md).
  7. **Contact humans with tool calls** — have the model always emit JSON and declare intents such as `request_human_input` or `done_for_now`; benefits: outer-loop (`Agent->Human`) workflows started by cron or events, "Multiple Human Access", Agent->Agent extension, durability with factor 6 — [factor-07](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-07-contact-humans-with-tools.md).
  8. **Own your control flow** — break the loop on some tool calls (`request_clarification`, `deploy_backend` waits for approval) and continue on others (`fetch_git_tags`); add summarisation or caching of tool results, "LLM-as-judge on structured output", compaction, tracing, "client-side rate limiting", "durable sleep / pause / 'wait for event'"; the author's "number one feature request" is interrupt-and-resume between tool selection and invocation — [factor-08](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-08-own-your-control-flow.md).
  9. **Compact Errors into Context Window** — feed errors back for "self-healing"; an `errorCounter` limits "~3 attempts of a single tool" (the example counts `consecutive_errors` and resets on success); a threshold is "a great place to escalate to a human"; you can "restructure how it's represented, remove previous events from the context window" — [factor-09 L31–62, L81](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-09-compact-errors.md).
  10. **Small, Focused Agents** — "Agents are just one building block in a larger, mostly deterministic system"; keep agents to "3-10, maybe 20 steps max"; "As context grows, LLMs are more likely to get lost"; grow scope as models improve — [factor-10](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-10-small-focused-agents.md).
  11. **Trigger from anywhere, meet users where they are** — trigger from Slack, email, SMS, events, crons, outages; respond on the same channels; quickly looping in humans lets agents use "higher stakes operations" (the page is also "the humanlayer pitch") — [factor-11](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-11-trigger-from-anywhere.md).
  12. **Make your agent a stateless reducer** — two images (reducer, `foldl`) and "This one is mostly just for fun" — [factor-12](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/factor-12-stateless-reducer.md).
  - Appendix **Factor 13 — pre-fetch all the context you might need**: "If you already know what tools you'll want the model to call, just call them DETERMINISTICALLY" — [appendix-13](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/blob/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content/appendix-13-pre-fetch.md).
- The `content/` folder also holds older single-digit copies (`factor-1-…` to `factor-9-…`) that differ from the zero-padded files the README links; we used the README-linked files — [content/ listing at the pin](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents/tree/d20c728368bf9c189d6d7aab704744decb6ec0cc/content).

### Inferences
- The factors split into three kinds for mapping: **loop mechanics** (4, 6, 8, 9, 12), **state and context** (3, 5, 13) and **product shape** (1, 2, 7, 10, 11). Our SDK and harness plans cover the first two kinds; the agent plan covers the third.
- Factor 12 carries no argument of its own; any mapping rests on factors 5 and 6, which it illustrates.

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence backs any factor in the repo; the guide is practitioner opinion. Another researcher covers the factors in depth, so these notes do not assess the guide's own sources.

## 2. Factor by factor: what our documents say, and the verdict

### Takeaway
We are aligned on 8 of 13 (1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12), partly aligned on 4 (3, 5, 7, 13) and have one real gap (11, triggers and ingress). Where we differ, it is deliberate and argued: we keep the standard message format for prompt caching (3), keep business state in the helpdesk and policy store rather than the log (5), and route every human contact through code-decided, gated paths (7).

### Inferences
- The closest fit is between factors 4, 6 and 8 and our "model proposes, code executes, pauses are log entries" core; 12f's "number one feature request" (pause between tool selection and invocation) is the centre of SDK decision 8, HD5 and AD1. A report can say our design already implements 12f's strongest claims, with signing and policy re-evaluation on top.
- Our two deliberate departures share one reason, which 12f does not weigh: **external effects and caching**. Custom context formats (F3) fight a stable cache prefix, and a single "thread" holding business state (F5) breaks once that state lives in someone else's system and must survive a fork or rewind.
- Factor 12 adds nothing to check beyond factors 5 and 6; report it as aligned and move on.

### Gaps
- Whether a custom single-message context format would beat message-based context for our roles is untested here; neither our evidence nor 12f measures it.
- Whether `support_get_ticket` in the planner's toolset lets the planner read the raw ticket body (which the context split forbids) was not checked; it bears on F13's suggestion and on AD5's taint rule, and should be verified against A4/A11 before the report claims anything.

## 3. Where our evidence base supports or contradicts the factors

### Takeaway
Our fact-checked research and the HN corpus support factors 2, 4, 8, 9 and 10 with measurements 12f lacks, and they qualify factors 3, 5 and 11: context management pays less as windows grow, a rewound thread cannot undo business effects, and fast human approval decays into rubber-stamping.

### Cited Findings
- **F2, F8 (own prompts and control flow):** "A thin loop you can read beats a framework" (~185 HN authors across 101 threads), LangChain draws ~46 authors "almost all negative" ([practitioner research §1, view 8](../../practitioners/practitioner-research.md#1-eleven-views-have-wide-support-led-by-quota-opacity-sandboxed-autonomy-and-plan-files)); HN confirms "Frameworks obscure prompts and responses" ([practitioner research §2.2, row 9](../../practitioners/practitioner-research.md#22-hn-confirms-ten-vendor-or-paper-claims-qualifies-three-and-contradicts-two-vendor-defaults)). Counter-view: ~25 authors say the thin loop's missing 10% is "the real work" ([SDK guide §9 L219](../../../guides/agent-sdk.md#9-the-public-api-should-be-functions-with-options-objects-an-explicit-provider-and-nothing-that-reaches-outside-the-process-by-default)). Main report: "Most teams should write the thin parts of an agent themselves" ([main report L3](../../agent-sdks-and-harness-design.md)).
- **F8, F10 (deterministic code around small steps):** τ-bench pass^k fell from 0.604 to 0.383 at k=4, so "a policy that must never break belongs in code" ([agent guide §2 L46](../../../guides/agent.md#2-choose-the-least-autonomy-the-task-allows-a-workflow-in-code-with-one-bounded-loop-for-the-ambiguous-middle)); ~260 HN authors endorse small reviewable tasks and ~90 advocate deterministic code around the model (L61, same section); multi-agent on sequential planning −70.0% ([agent guide §2 table L36–42](../../../guides/agent.md#2-choose-the-least-autonomy-the-task-allows-a-workflow-in-code-with-one-bounded-loop-for-the-ambiguous-middle)); "Fresh context beats long sessions" (~255 authors) ([practitioner research §1, view 6](../../practitioners/practitioner-research.md#1-eleven-views-have-wide-support-led-by-quota-opacity-sandboxed-autonomy-and-plan-files)). The HN corpus is about coding agents, so it "transfers to a support product only weakly" ([agent guide §2 L61](../../../guides/agent.md#2-choose-the-least-autonomy-the-task-allows-a-workflow-in-code-with-one-bounded-loop-for-the-ambiguous-middle)).
- **F10 "what if LLMs get smarter":** harness gains shrink as models improve (OpenHands +13.60 → +5.47 points), and context compression and general subagents can hurt some tasks ([harness guide §11 L253](../../../guides/agent-harness.md#11-every-component-should-be-removable-because-the-value-of-harness-scaffolding-decays-as-models-improve)) — supports 12f's "slowly expand agent scope", and our removability rule is the mechanism.
- **F1, F4 (tools):** a rewritten tool description cut completion time by 40%; input examples raised accuracy from 72% to 90% (Anthropic) ([agent guide §3 L67](../../../guides/agent.md#3-tools-deserve-more-design-effort-than-the-prompt-few-workflow-shaped-tools-identity-from-trusted-context-and-errors-that-say-what-to-do-next)); at least three of eight SDKs get typed output from a final tool call ([SDK guide §4 L111](../../../guides/agent-sdk.md#4-tools-should-take-standard-schema-validate-inside-the-sdk-and-return-every-failure-to-the-model-as-data)).
- **F9 (errors):** the MCP spec warns that without error results "the LLM would not be able to see that an error occurred and self-correct"; OpenAI's default generic error text is the counter-example ([SDK guide §4 L103](../../../guides/agent-sdk.md#4-tools-should-take-standard-schema-validate-inside-the-sdk-and-return-every-failure-to-the-model-as-data)); HN adds that agents answer failed tools by inventing mocks ([SDK guide §4 L105](../../../guides/agent-sdk.md#4-tools-should-take-standard-schema-validate-inside-the-sdk-and-return-every-failure-to-the-model-as-data)) — a failure mode 12f's "self-healing" framing does not mention.
- **F3 (context) — qualified:** masking halves cost while matching summarisation's solve rate (arXiv 2508.21433), but the context-management gain on SWE-bench Verified fell from 35.7 to 2.7 points as windows grew from 32K to 128K ([harness guide §3 L80–82](../../../guides/agent-harness.md#3-context-policy-should-run-five-removable-layers-cheapest-first-and-keep-every-compaction-visible-and-reversible)); ~45 HN authors distrust compaction they cannot see (L88). So "owning" context is supported, while 12f's "can dramatically improve your agent's performance" holds mainly for small windows (our inference from those figures).
- **F5 (unify state) — supported and qualified:** OpenAI had to add `RunState` bookkeeping because history and paused-run state "live in two stores that can diverge", which the harness research calls "the strongest argument for one store" ([harness guide §2 L45](../../../guides/agent-harness.md#2-the-session-log-should-be-one-append-only-tree-of-typed-entries-with-head-a-writer-lock-and-pauses-as-entries)). But "Rewind changes what the model sees next; it never reverses a write to someone else's system" ([rewind-vs-rollback, intro](../../reversible-actions/rewind-vs-rollback.md)).
- **F6, F7 (pauses and humans):** ~60 HN authors across ~53 threads wish the agent had asked instead of guessing ([SDK guide §7 L171](../../../guides/agent-sdk.md#7-an-approval-should-end-the-run-in-a-signed-resumable-log-state-not-block-a-callback)) — supports F7.
- **F11's "High Stakes Tools" premise — contradicted in part:** "any security mechanism which relies on users to read and intelligently respond to approval prompts is doomed to fail over time" (approval fatigue, ~440 authors run without prompts in a sandbox) ([harness guide §5 L135](../../../guides/agent-harness.md#5-the-permission-pipeline-should-be-ordered-deny-first-and-fail-closed-and-its-approvals-should-be-signed-entries)); the GitHub MCP leak got through because "many users already opt for an 'Always Allow' confirmation policy" ([agent guide §5 L109](../../../guides/agent.md#5-safety-is-a-policy-table-and-a-context-split-tier-every-tool-by-worst-case-effect-bind-identity-and-cut-the-outbound-leg)). Our answer is to keep prompts rare by tiering, so "prompts are a budget" (L141).
- **F13 (pre-fetch):** OpenAI's guide: "Don't make the model fill arguments you already know" ([agent guide §3 L69](../../../guides/agent.md#3-tools-deserve-more-design-effort-than-the-prompt-few-workflow-shaped-tools-identity-from-trusted-context-and-errors-that-say-what-to-do-next)); tool definitions are the largest fixed preamble cost (deferred loading 77K → 8.7K tokens) ([harness guide §4 L98](../../../guides/agent-harness.md)) — both support fetching deterministically and offering fewer tools.

### Inferences
- 12f is directionally consistent with the vendor guidance our research already cites (Anthropic's workflow-vs-agent line, OpenAI's "deterministic solution may suffice"); citing it adds a practitioner voice for the no-framework argument but no new evidence.
- The strongest correction our evidence makes to 12f is on human-in-the-loop: 12f treats "quickly loop in a variety of humans" as what unlocks high-stakes tools, while our evidence says approvals only work if they stay rare and show provenance and blast radius.

### Gaps
- No HN comment in our corpus discusses 12-factor agents by name, so practitioner reception of the guide itself is unknown from our data.
- No source in our base measures custom context formats (F3) or pre-fetching (F13) against the message-based default.

## 4. What should change: concrete suggestions, by plan section or todo

### Takeaway
Five changes are worth proposing, all small and testable offline: an ingress design for triggers and approvals (F11), write identities and outcomes for helpdesk effects (F5), a resolved-error mask and a broader error counter as ablatable defaults (F9, F3), pre-fetching what intake already holds (F13), and an optional internal-question pause (F7). None overturns an accepted decision.

### Cited Findings
- Plan sections the suggestions would touch: A24 scope ("which helpdesk … the approval surface and how it authenticates approvers") ([A24](../../../todos/ready/A24-real-helpdesk-adapter.md)); `InboundTicket` ([build-agent §3.2 L298](../../../plans/build-agent.md#32-trusted-run-context-helpdesk-and-tool-contracts)); the agent API `triage()` / `resolve()` ([build-agent §3.4 L454–458](../../../plans/build-agent.md#34-roles-gates-and-the-workflow)); U7's recommendation "Give each write an identity before it runs and an outcome after it runs, and treat the gap between the two as `unknown`" and its untested crash case ([rewind-vs-rollback L110–112](../../reversible-actions/rewind-vs-rollback.md#two-ledgers-and-the-first-fields-to-add)); HD3's layer 2 defaults and N39's ablation ([HD3](../../../plans/build-harness.md#hd3-layers-1-2-3-and-5-on-by-default-and-a-budget-capped-at-200k-tokens-provisional)); D11 marked provisional, "N39 retunes" ([D11](../../../plans/build-sdk.md#d11-three-identical-failing-calls-stop-the-run-and-the-depth-cap-is-2-provisional)); A11's planner input ([A11](../../../todos/ready/A11-planner-context.md)); AD12 provisional on shadow labels ([AD12](../../../plans/build-agent.md#ad12-a-missing-fact-becomes-a-templated-question-through-the-reply-gate-and-missing-authority-becomes-a-handoff-provisional)); the third host-tool pattern deferred ([build-harness §1 L41](../../../plans/build-harness.md#1-goal-and-non-goals)).

### Inferences
All suggestions below are ours, not sourced recommendations.
1. **F11, ingress (largest gap).** Add an ingress component to the agent plan, either in A24's scope or as a new Stage 5 todo: a webhook receiver or poller that turns helpdesk events into `triage(InboundTicket)` and approval-surface callbacks into `resolve()`. Specify at-least-once delivery handling: dedupe by helpdesk event id, one `triage()` per ticket version (the session writer lock returns `would_block` for concurrent runs, [HD2](../../../plans/build-harness.md), but does not stop a re-triage after completion), and a retry policy. Record it as an AD15 or an ADR beside 0031. Test offline with `fakeHelpdesk` delivering duplicate and out-of-order events.
2. **F5, effects ledger.** Adopt U7's two fields in the agent plan's `Helpdesk` writes (`WriteReceipt` at [build-agent §3.2 L319](../../../plans/build-agent.md#32-trusted-run-context-helpdesk-and-tool-contracts)) and the harness's `code_call` / `tool_result` entries: an idempotency key written before the call, and an outcome (`applied | not_applied | unknown`) after it, plus the crash test U7 names, in A13 or the capstone (A15). This answers 12f's factor 5 on our terms: execution state stays unified in the log, and the log records the identity of every business effect without pretending to own it.
3. **F9 and F3, error compaction.** Add an optional layer-2 rule that masks a failed tool call and its error once a later call with the same tool succeeds, off by default and ablated in N39 beside the other layers. Separately, have N39 compare D11's identical-call rule with 12f's consecutive-errors-per-tool rule (three consecutive `isError` results from one tool, any input). Both changes are tunable defaults and need no ADR.
4. **F13, pre-fetch.** In A11, put the requester summary and order list that intake already loaded into the planner's operator-origin input, and move `support_get_requester` and `support_list_requester_orders` to `exposure: "deferred"` or drop them; consider running `help_search_articles` in code from the reader's `category` and `product`. Measure on cost per resolved ticket and pass^5 in A20 before keeping it (agent guide §6's gate).
5. **F7, internal questions.** When AD12 is retuned from shadow labels, consider a second clarification target: a question to an internal role (e.g. `support_lead`), sent through the harness's existing `clarification/request` and resolved with an answer, so the planner can resume rather than hand off. It reuses the existing pause, so it needs no SDK change; adopt it only if shadow data shows handoffs that a one-line internal answer would have resolved.
6. **Not suggested:** custom single-message context formats (F3) and role hacking (F2), because our caching rules argue against them and nothing in our evidence measures a gain; a run-time LLM judge (F8), because AD10 keeps classifiers from approving; generic wait-for-event pauses (F6), until a triage step needs to wait on an asynchronous helpdesk job.
7. **For the report's framing:** credit 12f in the research notes as the practitioner source that `oss-frameworks.md` L162 could not fetch, and log that closure as a fact-check row rather than editing the note silently, per step 2 of AGENTS.md's "Every document is drafted, fact-checked and reviewed before it merges" (done: [Later closures](../../fact-check-2026-11-18.md#later-closures)).

### Gaps
- Whether the helpdesk the owner picks at A24 delivers webhooks, supports idempotency keys or only polling is unknown until A24; suggestion 1's design depends on it (U7 raises the same open question).
- The value of suggestions 3 and 4 is unmeasured; they should ship as ablatable defaults, not as decisions.
