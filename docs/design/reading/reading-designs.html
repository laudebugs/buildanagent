Reading settings and a sticky header

Two changes: a header that stays reachable while you scroll, and settings for the typeface pairing and the text size. Each option is drawn for desktop (left) and a phone (right) in the manual's own style. On a phone the header today takes two rows, about 100 px, so a sticky one has to shrink to a single slim row.

1. A sticky header

Contents, Glossary, Sources, search and the theme toggle stay in reach. Phones put the three links behind a menu button so the header fits one row. In every option a thin progress line along the header fills as you read down the chapter (here it shows 38%), so you can see how far through the page you are.

H1 · Always there, one slim row

The header is fixed at the top on every screen: 56 px on desktop, 52 px on a phone, with a thin progress line along its bottom edge that fills as you read down the chapter. Simple and predictable. It always costs a little reading space.

H2 · Hides when you read down, returns when you scroll up

On a phone the header slides away as you scroll down, so you get the whole screen, and comes back on the first upward scroll. On desktop it stays visible. The progress line stays at the very top of the screen even while the header is hidden, so you always see how far you are. The usual choice for reading apps.

2. Reading settings

An "Aa" button in the header opens the settings. Four typeface pairings (each a heading face and a body face; the code font stays the same) and five text sizes. The choice is saved on the device and applies to every chapter. The fonts are examples: Book is today's pairing, and the others are open-licence fonts we would self-host, loaded only when chosen.

S1 · A panel under Aa, a sheet on phones

A list of pairings, each with a sample, and a five-step size bar. Compact and quick, in the same shape as the notes popover.
